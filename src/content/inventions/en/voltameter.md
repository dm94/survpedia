# Brief description

A voltameter (also historically termed a coulometer) is an electrochemical instrument used to measure electric charge, current strength, and electrical quantity through the quantitative chemical decomposition of an electrolyte. Invented in 1834 by Michael Faraday during his foundational investigations into electrochemistry, the voltameter established the fundamental relationship between electric current and chemical change. By accurately measuring the volume of gas evolved or the mass of metal electrodeposited at an electrode, the voltameter provided the first absolute standard for quantifying electrical current long before modern electronic multimeters existed.

## Use / Function

- **Absolute Current Calibration:** Determining the exact electrical current flowing through a circuit according to Faraday's laws of electrolysis.
- **Coulomb Measurement:** Integrating variable electric current over time to determine total electric charge transferred ($Q = I \times t$).
- **Calibration of Galvanometers:** Serving as the primary chemical standard to calibrate electromechanical tangent galvanometers and ammeters.
- **Electrochemical Equivalent Determination:** Establishing the atomic mass and valency of chemical elements in early chemistry laboratories.

## Operating principle

1. **Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis:** The mass of a substance altered or deposited at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of electric charge passed through the solution.
2. **Ion Migration:** An electric current passing through an electrolytic bath causes positive cations to migrate toward the negative cathode and negative anions toward the positive anode.
3. **Electrolytic Decomposition:** In a water voltameter, water acidulated with sulfuric acid is split into hydrogen gas at the cathode and oxygen gas at the anode ($2\text{H}_2\text{O} \rightarrow 2\text{H}_2 + \text{O}_2$).
4. **Volumetric Quantification:** The liberated hydrogen gas accumulates inside an inverted, graduated glass tube (eudiometer), displacing liquid.
5. **Charge Calculation:** Knowing the volume, temperature, and ambient barometric pressure of the collected hydrogen gas, the total Coulombs transferred is computed using Faraday's constant ($96,485\text{ C/mol}$).

## How to create it

1. **Electrolysis Vessel Assembly:** Prepare a chemical-resistant glass beaker or reservoir fitted with a sealed bottom rubber or cork stopper.
2. **Electrode Insertion:** Insert two inert platinum or lead/graphite foil electrodes through the base of the vessel, soldering insulated copper wires to the external terminals.
3. **Acidified Electrolyte Preparation:** Fill the vessel with distilled water containing approximately 5% to 10% pure sulfuric acid to provide high ionic conductivity without consuming the electrodes.
4. **Positioning Graduated Tubes:** Invert two graduated glass tubes (burettes or test tubes) filled completely with the acid electrolyte directly over the cathode and anode electrodes.
5. **Circuit Integration:** Connect the voltameter in series with the direct current power source (such as a battery or dynamo) and the load circuit under test.
6. **Measurement Execution:** Record the starting time, allow current to flow while collecting gas, disconnect the circuit, and read the gas volume at ambient temperature and pressure.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Chemically inert **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** for the reservoir and graduated tubes; conductive **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)** (platinum foils, nickel, or pure copper sheets) for electrodes; pure **[Water](/en/materials/water)**; and **[Sulfuric Acid](/en/materials/sulfuric-acid)** for creating the conducting electrolyte.
- **Tools:** Glass cutter, gas burettes with fine volume graduations, mercury thermometer, barometer, soldering iron.
- **Substitutes:** Copper electrodes immersed in an acidified copper sulfate solution form a copper weight voltameter, where charge is measured by weighing the cathode on an analytical balance before and after the run.

## Variants and improvements

- **Hofmann Voltameter:** A three-branched glass apparatus featuring stopcocks at the top of each measuring limb and a central reservoir bulb for easy filling and gas evacuation.
- **Copper Weight Voltameter:** Uses copper plates in copper sulfate solution; eliminates gas compressibility errors by measuring solid metal deposition directly on a sensitive balance.
- **Silver Voltameter:** Employs a platinum crucible cathode and pure silver anode in silver nitrate solution; served as the official international legal standard for defining the Ampere from 1893 to 1948.
- **Detonating Gas Voltameter:** Collects hydrogen and oxygen combined in a single graduated vessel, doubling gas collection speed for rapid approximate checks.

## Limits and risks

- **Explosive Gas Mixture:** The combination of pure hydrogen and oxygen gas produced during water electrolysis is highly explosive and will violently detonate if ignited by a stray electrical spark or flame.
- **Acid Hazard:** Uses concentrated sulfuric acid; splashes can cause chemical burns to skin and eyes, requiring neutralizing wash and goggles.
- **Temperature and Pressure Variations:** Gas volume measurements must be mathematically normalized to standard temperature and pressure (STP) using the ideal gas law and water vapor pressure corrections.
