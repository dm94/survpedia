# Brief description

A rotary kiln is a long, slightly inclined, rotating cylindrical thermal reactor used for continuous thermal processing of solid materials. Operating as a continuous-flow furnace, it enables large-scale calcination, roasting, and thermal decomposition required for industrial cement, lime, and chemical production.

## Use / Function

- **Cement Manufacturing:** Clinkering limestone and clay mixtures at high temperatures ($1400\text{–}1500\,^\circ\text{C}$) to make Portland cement.
- **Lime Calcination:** Continuously calcining **[Limestone](/en/materials/limestone)** into quicklime for steelmaking and chemical plants.
- **Ore Roasting & Metallurgy:** Heating metallic ores in controlled atmospheres to drive off sulfur, moisture, or organic impurities.
- **Activated Charcoal Production:** Thermal activation and carbonization of organic materials in low-oxygen environments.

## Operating principle

1. **Inclined Rotation:** A heavy steel cylinder lined with refractory brick is mounted on roller bearings at a slight incline ($2\text{–}4^\circ$) and rotated slowly ($1\text{–}5\text{ RPM}$).
2. **Counter-Current Flow:** Raw material feed enters at the elevated upper end and slowly tumbles downward toward the lower discharge end under gravity.
3. **Heat Exchange:** A burner installed at the lower discharge end injects fuel and air, blowing hot combustion gases upward against the downward flow of material.
4. **Thermal Reaction:** As the material tumbles down through progressively hotter thermal zones, it undergoes drying, calcination, and chemical synthesis before discharging into a cooler.

## How to create it

1. **Fabricate the Shell:** Roll and weld heavy **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** plates to form a long, rigid cylindrical tube.
2. **Install Refractory Lining:** Line the inner wall with high-density **[Refractory Brick](/en/materials/refractory-brick)** and heat-resistant mortar to protect the outer steel shell from extreme operating temperatures.
3. **Mount Riding Rings & Rollers:** Fix heavy **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** riding rings (tyres) around the shell and position them on sturdy steel trunnion rollers mounted on reinforced **[Concrete](/en/materials/concrete)** foundations.
4. **Drive Mechanism:** Attach a girth gear around the cylinder mesh with a pinion gear driven by an electric or mechanical drive with a speed reducer.
5. **Install Burner & Air Seals:** Position a high-capacity burner tube at the lower discharge end and install heat-resistant rotary seals at both ends to minimize stray air infiltration.

## Materials needed

- **Kiln Vessel:** Heavy structural **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** plate and structural rings.
- **Thermal Insulation:** High-temperature **[Refractory Brick](/en/materials/refractory-brick)** and alumina/fireclay mortar.
- **Support & Drive:** Heavy **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** trunnion rollers, tyres, and large drive gears.
- **Foundation:** Reinforced **[Concrete](/en/materials/concrete)** footings.
- **Fuel Source:** Pulverized coal, **[Charcoal](/en/materials/charcoal)**, natural gas, or heavy fuel oil.

## Variants and improvements

- **Wet Process Kiln:** Feeds raw materials as a liquid slurry; requires longer kiln cylinders to accommodate a drying zone.
- **Dry Process Kiln with Preheater:** Uses multi-stage cyclone preheaters to heat dry powder before entering a shorter rotary kiln, drastically reducing fuel consumption.
- **Direct-Fired vs. Indirect-Fired:** Direct-fired kilns pass combustion gases through the material bed; indirect-fired kilns heat the outer shell externally for gas-sensitive chemical processes.

## Limits and risks

- **Thermal Strain & Shell Cracking:** Severe thermal gradients or refractory failure can overheat and deform the outer steel shell.
- **Refractory Wear:** Continuous abrasive motion of tumbling material erodes inner brick linings, requiring periodic shutdowns and relining.
- **Dust Emission:** High gas velocities inside the drum generate fine particulate dust requiring cyclone collectors or baghouses.
- **Dust Ring Formation:** Molten or sticky ash and mineral compounds can fuse onto internal walls, forming restrictive rings that block material flow.
