# Brief description

A sphygmomanometer is a device used to measure blood pressure, consisting of an inflatable cuff, a measuring unit (mercury pressure gauge or aneroid manometer), and a mechanism for inflation such as a hand bulb and valve.

## Use / Function

- **Primary use:** Non-invasive clinical measurement of arterial blood pressure (systolic and diastolic).
- **Secondary uses:** Monitoring cardiovascular health, diagnosing hypertension or hypotension, and evaluating patient response to treatment during surgery or emergency care.
- **Scale:** Personal medical instrument used in clinics, hospitals, and field medicine.

## Operating principle

1. **Arterial Occlusion:** An inflatable bladder inside an inelastic fabric cuff is wrapped around a limb (usually the upper arm) and inflated until the pressure exceeds the arterial systolic pressure, stopping blood flow through the brachial artery.
2. **Controlled Decompression:** The pressure in the cuff is gradually released through a control valve.
3. **Acoustic Detection (Auscultation):** As pressure drops below systolic pressure, turbulent blood flow resumes, creating audible tapping vibrations known as Korotkoff sounds, heard using a **[Stethoscope](/en/inventions/stethoscope)** placed downstream over the artery.
4. **Pressure Measurement:** The pressure reading when the first sound is heard corresponds to systolic pressure. The pressure reading when the sounds completely disappear corresponds to diastolic pressure.

## How to create it

1. **Inflatable Bladder and Cuff:** Construct an airtight rubber bladder fitted with two flexible **[Natural Rubber](/en/materials/natural-rubber)** tube ports. Enclose this bladder in a durable, non-stretching **[Fabric](/en/materials/fabric)** sleeve with adjustable fasteners or straps.
2. **Inflation Assembly:** Connect one tube port from the bladder to a squeezable rubber bulb fitted with a one-way intake valve and a fine-threaded adjustable release valve made of **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)**.
3. **Manometer Gauge:** Connect the second tube port to a vertical glass column containing **[Mercury](/en/materials/mercury)** resting in a sealed reservoir, calibrated with millimeter markings (0–300 mmHg), or connect it to a calibrated aneroid metallic bellows gauge.
4. **Assembly and Calibration:** Test the system for airtightness by inflating the bladder to 200 mmHg and confirming the pressure holds steadily when the valve is closed.

## Materials needed

- **Essential Materials:** **[Natural Rubber](/en/materials/natural-rubber)** (bladder, tubing, bulb), **[Fabric](/en/materials/fabric)** (inelastic cuff sleeve), **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)** (valves, fittings, gauge body), **[Mercury](/en/materials/mercury)** or a calibrated metallic bellows diaphragm.
- **Tools:** Glassblowing tools (for mercury column), metal lathe/machining tools, sewing tools, pressure calibration reference standard.
- **Substitutes:** Water manometer column (requires a taller tube ~4.1 meters for 300 mmHg), aneroid pressure gauge instead of liquid mercury.

## Variants and improvements

- **Riva-Rocci Mercury Sphygmomanometer:** The original 1896 design using a mercury column and upper-arm cuff.
- **Aneroid Sphygmomanometer:** Replaces liquid mercury with a flexible metal capsule (bellows) that expands or contracts with pressure, turning a needle indicator.
- **Digital / Oscillometric Sphygmomanometer:** Uses electronic pressure sensors to measure pressure oscillations automatically without needing auscultation.

## Limits and risks

- **Toxicity:** Mercury column models carry a hazard of toxic chemical exposure if glass tubes break.
- **Operator Dependency:** Manual measurement requires trained ears and technique to accurately identify Korotkoff sounds.
- **Cuff Sizing:** Incorrect cuff width relative to arm circumference leads to false high or low readings.
