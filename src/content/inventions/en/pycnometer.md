# Brief description

A pycnometer is a precision glass vessel calibrated to hold a precisely known volume of liquid at a designated temperature. By weighing the vessel empty, filled with a reference liquid (pure water), and filled with an unknown liquid or solid sample, it enables extremely accurate density and specific gravity determinations.

## Use / Function

- **Specific Gravity Determination:** Measuring the density of liquids, slurries, paints, and molten resins with higher precision than a floating hydrometer.
- **Powder & Soil Porosity:** Determining the skeletal (true) density of granular materials, crushed ores, sand, and powders.
- **Chemical Purity Testing:** Verifying the concentration and purity of alcohols, acids, oils, and chemical reagents by comparing densities against standard reference tables.

## Operating principle

1. **Fixed Internal Volume:** The vessel features a ground glass stopper pierced with a fine capillary borehole running through its vertical axis.
2. **Capillary Displacement:** When the pycnometer is completely filled and the ground stopper inserted, excess liquid is forced out through the capillary bore and carefully wiped away, leaving an exact, reproducible volume without air bubbles.
3. **Gravimetric Comparison:** Using an analytical balance, the mass of the unknown fluid occupying the fixed volume is compared to the mass of an equal volume of pure water at the same temperature: $\rho = \frac{m_{\text{sample}}}{V_{\text{pycnometer}}}$.

## How to create it

1. **Blow Glass Flask:** Blow or form a small, stable flat-bottomed glass flask (typically 10 to 50 ml capacity) with a tapered neck ground to a standard taper.
2. **Fabricate Ground Stopper:** Machine or hand-grind a solid glass stopper with an identical taper to seat firmly and airtightly into the flask neck.
3. **Drill Capillary Bore:** Bore a very fine vertical capillary channel (approx. 0.5 to 1 mm diameter) through the center of the glass stopper.
4. **Grind Stopper Top Flat:** Polish the top edge of the stopper flat and smooth so that wiping off overflowing liquid produces a flat, repeatable meniscus.
5. **Calibrate Volume:** Weigh the completely dry flask and stopper on a balance scale ($m_0$), fill it completely with pure degassed distilled water at 20°C, insert the stopper, wipe the exterior dry, and reweigh ($m_1$) to calculate the exact internal volume ($V = (m_1 - m_0) / \rho_{\text{water}}$).

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** High-purity **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** flask and stopper, distilled **[Water](/en/materials/water)** for calibration.
- **Tools:** Glassblowing torch, emery or silicon carbide grinding slurry for ground-joint mating, sensitive balance scale, lint-free wiping cloths.
- **Substitutes:** A small medicine vial fitted with a pierced rubber or cork stopper and a thin glass thermometer capillary tube can serve as an expedient pycnometer.

## Variants and improvements

- **Gay-Lussac Pycnometer:** Standard cylindrical bottle with a capillary stopper, universally used for low-viscosity liquids.
- **Thermometer Pycnometer:** Features a built-in precision thermometer ground into one neck and a capillary side-arm with scale markings to monitor temperature fluctuations during weighing.
- **Hubbard Pycnometer:** Wide-mouth design with a conical ground stopper, specialized for heavy viscous fluids, asphalt, tar, and semisolids.

## Limits and risks

- **Thermal Expansion:** Density measurements are extremely sensitive to temperature changes; holding the flask with bare hands will transfer body heat and cause liquid to expand out the capillary bore.
- **Trapped Air Bubbles:** Any microbubble trapped along the flask walls or inside the capillary invalidates the volumetric calibration.
- **Fragility:** Thin-walled glass vessels can chip or shatter under mechanical shock, especially around the precision-ground stopper joint.
