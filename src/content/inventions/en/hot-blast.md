# Brief description

A hot blast stove (or Cowper stove) is a regenerative heat exchanger that preheats the air blast blown into a blast furnace by extracting heat from the furnace's waste exhaust gases.

## Use / Function

- **Efficiency Enhancement:** Preheats blast furnace air to 300°C–800°C+, reducing fuel consumption by roughly one-third, depending on furnace design and operating conditions.
- **Increased Smelting Temperature:** Elevates flame temperature inside the furnace hearth, enabling the smelting of refractory iron ores.
- **Scale:** Industrial iron and steel smelting works.

## Operating principle

1. **Combustion Phase (Heating Up):** Hot, combustible waste gases exiting the top of the **[Blast Furnace](/en/inventions/blast-furnace)** are mixed with air and burned inside a refractory-lined stove tower containing a dense matrix of firebrick checkerwork. The brickwork absorbs and stores thermal energy.
2. **Blast Phase (Heat Transfer):** Once the checkerwork reaches high temperatures, gas flow is switched. Cold compressed air from the blowing engine is driven through the hot checkerwork in reverse direction, absorbing heat before entering the blast furnace tuyeres.
3. **Cyclic Operation:** Blast furnaces typically use three or four stoves in parallel rotation so that while one stove preheats the blast air, the others are being heated by exhaust gas.

## How to create it

1. **Construct the Outer Shell:** Erect a cylindrical tall steel shell insulated with high-grade refractory lining.
2. **Build the Checkerwork Matrix:** Fill the interior chamber with a honeycomb grid of high-alumina **[Refractory Brick](/en/materials/refractory-brick)** with vertical flues for gas flow and maximum surface area heat storage.
3. **Install Combustion Chamber:** Incorporate an internal or external combustion chamber at the base fitted with gas burners and air inlets.
4. **Attach Reversing Valves:** Install heavy-duty **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** water-cooled slide valves to switch between waste gas heating mode and cold blast air injection mode.
5. **Connect to Blast Furnace Tuyeres:** Pipe the superheated air through insulated steel hot-blast mains directly into the **[Blast Furnace](/en/inventions/blast-furnace)** tuyere ring.

## Materials needed

- **Checkerwork & Lining:** **[Refractory Brick](/en/materials/refractory-brick)** (fireclay or alumina brick).
- **Outer Casing:** **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** plates and structural frames.
- **Valves & Piping:** **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** heat-resistant valves and insulated steel piping.
- **Fuel Source:** Combustible exhaust gases from the blast furnace stack.

## Variants and improvements

- **Neilson's Iron Pipe Stove (1828):** Early hot blast system passing air through iron pipes heated externally over a coal fire (heated air to ~300°C).
- **Cowper Brick Regenerative Stove (1857):** Modern firebrick checkerwork design capable of heating air blast above 800°C–1000°C.
- **External Combustion Chamber Stove:** Eliminates thermal stress cracking in the checkerwork wall.

## Limits and risks

- **Thermal Shock:** Rapid temperature changes cause cracking and degradation of refractory checkerwork.
- **Valve Wear & Leakage:** High temperature and abrasive gas cause rapid deterioration of control valves.
- **Explosion Risk:** Unburned gas-air mixtures trapped during valve switching can cause severe industrial explosions.
