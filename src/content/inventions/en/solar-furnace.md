# Brief description

A solar furnace is a structure that uses concentrated solar power to produce high temperatures, typically for industrial manufacturing, metallurgy, or chemical processing. It uses parabolic mirrors or heliostats to focus sunlight onto a single focal point without relying on fossil fuels or electricity.

## Use / Function

- **Metallurgy & Smelting:** Melting metals, producing high-purity alloys, and refining materials without chemical contamination from combustion gases.
- **Material Testing:** Subjecting ceramics, heat shields, and refractory materials to extreme thermal stress.
- **Chemical Synthesis:** Driving high-temperature endothermic chemical reactions, such as solar thermal hydrogen production or lime calcination.
- **Scale:** Ranges from small experimental parabolic dishes (generating several hundred degrees Celsius) to massive multi-megawatt industrial facilities capable of reaching over 3,000°C.

## Operating principle

1. **Light Collection:** Flat tracking mirrors (heliostats) track the sun's trajectory across the sky and reflect parallel rays of sunlight toward a large parabolic collector.
2. **Parabolic Focus:** The concave parabolic mirror focuses all incoming parallel rays onto a single central target area (the focal point).
3. **Concentration & Heat Generation:** Optical concentration factors ranging from hundreds to tens of thousands multiply the solar flux, raising the temperature at the target to extreme levels in seconds.
4. **Clean Heating:** Energy is transferred purely via electromagnetic radiation, creating a completely clean, non-contaminating thermal environment inside a reaction chamber or crucible.

## How to create it

1. **Parabolic Frame:** Construct a rigid, parabolic structural frame using **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or wood to support reflective elements with geometric accuracy.
2. **Reflective Surface:** Mount high-reflectivity silvered **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** **[Mirrors](/en/materials/mirror)** or polished aluminum sheets onto the parabolic framework.
3. **Tracking Heliostat:** Build a moveable flat mirror array mounted on an adjustable dual-axis frame to direct sunlight continuously toward the parabolic dish as the sun moves.
4. **Focal Receiver:** Install a refractory crucible or insulated target chamber at the exact geometric focus of the parabola using fireproof **[Bricks](/en/materials/brick)** or ceramic insulation.
5. **Alignment & Calibration:** Align all mirrors precisely so that reflected sunlight converges strictly on the receiver target.

## Materials needed

- **Reflector:** High-quality **[Mirrors](/en/materials/mirror)** or polished aluminum.
- **Structural Support:** **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** tubing, frame joints, or timber.
- **Target Vessel:** Refractory **[Bricks](/en/materials/brick)**, ceramics, or graphite crucible.
- **Tracking Mechanism:** Gears, levers, or automated drive motors for tracking the sun.

## Variants and improvements

- **Simple Parabolic Dish:** Direct single-stage parabolic reflector suitable for smaller applications; must turn directly toward the sun.
- **Heliostat & Stationary Concentrator:** Uses moving flat mirrors on the ground to reflect light into a large fixed parabolic mirror, allowing the heating chamber to remain static.
- **Solar Power Tower:** Uses thousands of ground-based heliostats focusing light onto a central receiver atop a tower to heat molten salt or steam for electricity generation.

## Limits and risks

- **Weather Dependence:** Completely reliant on clear, unclouded direct sunlight; performance drops to zero during night or heavy overcast.
- **Extreme Radiation Hazards:** Concentrated solar beams cause immediate blindness and catastrophic skin burns; protective optical shielding is mandatory.
- **Thermal Shock:** Rapid temperature changes at the focus can crack unheated ceramic materials or crucibles.
