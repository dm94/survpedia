# Brief description

A community-scale biological water purification system that uses a bed of fine sand and a naturally developed active biological film to remove pathogens, turbidity, and organic contaminants from raw water without chemicals or electricity.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Providing safe, pathogen-free drinking water for villages, towns, and decentralized communities.
- **Secondary Uses:** Pre-treatment for industrial processes and municipal water networks.
- **Scale:** Community to municipal infrastructure (processing thousands of liters per day).

## Operating principle

1.  **Hypogeal Biological Layer (Schmutzdecke):** Water slowly percolates through the top 2–3 cm of sand, where a biological film of beneficial algae, bacteria, and protozoa forms. This biological layer consumes pathogens, bacteria, and viruses.
2.  **Mechanical Strain and Adsorption:** Fine sand grains trap suspended silt, parasites, and eggs.
3.  **Predation and Natural Die-Off:** In the deeper, oxygen-poor lower sand layers, remaining bacteria die off due to lack of food and oxygen.
4.  **Underdrainage Collection:** Cleaned water enters a bottom layer of gravel and flows out through perforated drainage pipes.

## How to create it

### Community Slow Sand Water Filter Construction

1.  **Filter Basin:** Construct a watertight rectangular tank of **[Concrete](/en/materials/concrete)**, **[Stone](/en/materials/stone)** masonry, or brick, approximately 1.5–2 meters deep.
2.  **Underdrain System:** Lay perforated drainage pipes or spaced bricks at the floor, sloping gently toward a clean-water outlet weir.
3.  **Gravel Bed:** Lay graded layers of **[Gravel](/en/materials/gravel)** over the underdrain (coarse gravel at the bottom, fine gravel on top, total depth ~30 cm) to prevent sand from entering the outlet pipes.
4.  **Sand Column:** Fill the basin with washed, fine silica **[Sand](/en/materials/sand)** (effective size 0.15–0.35 mm) to a depth of 0.8–1.0 meter.
5.  **Water Column & Flow Control:** Maintain a water layer of 1.0 meter above the sand bed and regulate the flow rate using an inlet valve to keep percolating velocity low (0.1–0.2 meters per hour).
6.  **Maturation Period:** Run raw water through the filter continuously for 2–3 weeks, discarding the output until the biological "Schmutzdecke" layer fully matures and pathogen removal reaches >99%.

## Materials needed

- **Filter Media:** Washed silica **[Sand](/en/materials/sand)** and graded **[Gravel](/en/materials/gravel)**.
- **Basin Structure:** **[Concrete](/en/materials/concrete)**, **[Brick](/en/materials/brick)**, or **[Stone](/en/materials/stone)**.
- **Adsorption Additive (Optional):** A layer of **[Activated Charcoal](/en/materials/activated-charcoal)** or **[Charcoal](/en/materials/charcoal)** near the bottom for odor and chemical removal.
- **Plumbing:** Valves, overflow pipes, and drain tubes.

## Variants and improvements

- **BioSand Filter (Household):** Compact intermittent concrete or plastic container filter for individual families.
- **Pre-Filter Settling Basins:** Sedimentation ponds placed before the sand filter to settle out heavy mud and prevent rapid clogging.
- **Dual-Bed Filters:** Incorporates a top anthracite coal layer to increase run times between cleanings.

## Limits and risks

- **Turbidity Limits:** High mud/silt content (>50 NTU) clogs the top sand layer rapidly; raw water must be settled first.
- **Drying Hazard:** If the water level drops and the sand dries out, the biological Schmutzdecke layer dies and the filter must be re-matured for weeks.
- **Cleaning Protocol:** When flow rate drops, the top 1–2 cm of sand ("scraping") must be carefully removed and washed, followed by re-establishing the biological film.
