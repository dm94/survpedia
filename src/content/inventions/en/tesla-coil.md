# Brief description

A Tesla coil is an electrical resonant transformer circuit used to produce high-voltage, low-current, high-frequency alternating-current electricity. Designed by Nikola Tesla, it demonstrates resonant inductive coupling and generates high-frequency electrical discharges across air gaps.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Generating high-voltage, high-frequency radio frequency (RF) electrical discharges and wireless electrical transmission experiments.
- **Secondary Uses:** Early radio transmitters (spark-gap radio), spark gap accelerators, vacuum tube leak detection, and physics education.
- **Scale:** Small laboratory benchtop apparatus to large industrial and high-voltage testing installations.

## Operating principle

1. **Energy Storage:** High-voltage AC charges a primary capacitor through a step-up transformer.
2. **Spark Gap Discharge:** When voltage across the spark gap reaches breakdown, a spark jumps, closing the primary circuit and causing high-frequency oscillations.
3. **Resonant Induction:** Energy transfers inductively from the heavy primary coil (few turns) to the secondary coil (many turns).
4. **Resonance Tuning:** Both primary and secondary tuned circuits oscillate at the exact same resonant frequency, amplifying secondary voltage to hundreds of thousands of volts.
5. **Top Terminal Discharge:** Accumulated charge on the top capacitance sphere (toroid) breaks down surrounding air, creating brilliant electrical sparks.

## How to create it

1. **Primary Circuit Assembly:** Wire a high-voltage transformer to a spark gap and a high-voltage capacitor bank.
2. **Primary Coil Winding:** Wind a heavy **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** tube or thick **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** into a wide, low-turn spiral (3–6 turns) around the base.
3. **Secondary Coil Winding:** Tightly wind fine enamel-insulated copper **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** around a non-conductive form (such as **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** or plastic PVC pipe) for several hundred turns.
4. **Top Terminal Mount:** Attach a smooth metallic sphere or toroid (e.g., aluminum ducting or metal sphere) to the top of the secondary winding to act as a terminal capacitor.
5. **Grounding:** Connect the bottom of the secondary coil to a solid earth ground.
6. **Tuning:** Adjust the primary tap point to match the resonant frequency of the secondary circuit.

## Materials needed

- **Coil Conductors:** Heavy enamel-coated copper **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** and thick copper tubing.
- **Insulation & Form:** **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** or non-conductive plastic tubing.
- **Capacitors:** High-voltage dielectric capacitor bank (glass/polyethylene plates or ceramic caps).
- **Power Supply:** High-voltage step-up transformer (e.g., neon sign transformer or ignition coil).
- **Spark Gap:** Adjustable copper electrodes mounted on heat-resistant insulating material.

## Variants and improvements

- **Spark Gap Tesla Coil (SGTC):** Traditional design using an air spark gap switch.
- **Solid State Tesla Coil (SSTC):** Uses power transistors (IGBTs or MOSFETs) instead of a spark gap for precise frequency drive.
- **Dual Resonant Solid State Tesla Coil (DRSSTC):** Combines solid-state switching with resonant tuning for long, controllable sparks.

## Limits and risks

- **Electrocution Hazard:** High-voltage primary circuits are lethal; extreme caution is required around primary power supplies.
- **Radio Frequency Interference:** Emits powerful electromagnetic interference (EMI) across broadcast bands if unshielded.
- **Ozone & UV Generation:** Electrical discharges produce ozone, nitrogen oxides, and ultraviolet radiation; operate in well-ventilated spaces with UV eye protection.
