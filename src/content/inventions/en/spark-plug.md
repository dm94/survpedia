# Brief description

A spark plug is an electrical device fitted into the cylinder head of a spark-ignition engine. It conducts high-voltage current from an ignition system into the combustion chamber to produce an electric spark, igniting compressed fuel and air.

## Use / Function

- **Primary use**: Delivering thousands of high-voltage sparks per minute to ignite air-fuel mixtures in gasoline, alcohol, or gas engines.
- **Secondary uses**: Heat dissipation from the combustion chamber to the cylinder head cooling system.
- **Scale**: Small two-stroke engines up to high-performance aircraft and automotive engines.

## Operating principle

**Dielectric Breakdown and Thermal Conduction**:

1. **High Voltage Pulse**: An ignition coil generates a pulse of 12,000 to 25,000 volts along the central conductor wire.
2. **Dielectric Breakdown**: The intense electric field between the central electrode and ground electrode ionizes the gas gap, making it conductive.
3. **Plasma Spark**: Current arcs across the gap, forming a high-temperature plasma spark that ignites the compressed gas.
4. **Heat Transfer**: The ceramic insulator absorbs combustion heat and transfers it to the metal shell and cylinder head.

## How to create it

### Basic Ceramic Spark Plug

1. Machine a threaded metal shell out of **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** with an external hex nut shape and ground electrode tab.
2. Form a dense, heat-resistant **[Ceramics](/en/materials/ceramics)** insulator body (alumina porcelain) with a central axial bore.
3. Insert a **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** core conductor with a high-nickel tip inside the ceramic insulator bore.
4. Seal the conductor inside the insulator using conductive glass frit or copper powder compressed under heat.
5. Press-fit or crimp the ceramic insulator tightly inside the threaded steel shell with copper sealing gaskets.
6. Bend the ground electrode to establish a precise gap (0.6–1.0 mm) relative to the central electrode.

## Materials needed

- **Electrodes**: **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** or nickel alloy (or platinum/iridium for longevity).
- **Insulator**: High-purity sintered **[Ceramics](/en/materials/ceramics)** (Alumina Porcelain).
- **Shell & Threads**: Low-carbon **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**.
- **Seals**: **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** washers or conductive glass powder.
- **Tools**: Threading lathe, ceramic kiln, electrode gapping gauge.

## Variants and improvements

- **Hot vs Cold Plugs**: Varying length of insulator nose to control operating heat range.
- **Resistor Spark Plugs**: Internal ceramic resistor to suppress radio-frequency electromagnetic interference.
- **Multi-Ground Electrode Plugs**: Multiple ground tabs to extend operational life and reduce erosion.

## Limits and risks

- **Insulator Cracking**: Cracked ceramic allows high voltage to short to the metal body without producing a spark.
- **Fouling**: Carbon soot or oil accumulation short-circuits the electrode gap, preventing ignition.
- **Pre-Ignition**: Overheated electrodes can glow hot enough to ignite fuel before the spark occurs, risking severe engine damage.
