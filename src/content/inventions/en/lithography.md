# Brief description

Lithography is a planographic chemical printing process based on the chemical immiscibility of oil and water. Unlike relief or intaglio printing, the printing surface remains completely flat; the image is defined chemically, allowing rapid, high-volume replication of complex drawings, maps, and text with minimal plate wear.

## Use / Function

- **Map and Illustration Printing**: Enabled the mass duplication of intricate maps, technical diagrams, and detailed artwork that were difficult to reproduce with woodcuts or metal engravings.
- **Publishing and Books**: Provided a fast, inexpensive method for printing books and sheet music, democratizing access to illustrated technical literature.
- **Scale**: Local print shops to industrial-scale publishing houses.

## Operating principle

Lithography operates on the fundamental physical principle that **oil and water do not mix**:

1. **Drawing**: The desired image is drawn onto a highly polished, flat slab of fine-grain limestone using an oily or greasy medium (such as a crayon made of beeswax, soap, and tallow).
2. **Chemical Treatment**: The stone is treated with a mixture of weak acid (like nitric acid) and gum arabic solution. This chemically etches the bare parts of the stone, making them highly hydrophilic (water-attracting), while leaving the greasy drawn lines hydrophobic (water-repelling) but oleophilic (oil-attracting).
3. **Dampening**: The stone is wiped with water. The water clings to the bare limestone but is repelled by the greasy drawing.
4. **Inking**: An oil-based printing ink is rolled over the stone. The greasy ink is repelled by the wet, bare stone, but clings perfectly to the greasy drawn lines.
5. **Printing**: Paper is placed on the stone and pressed under a roller, transferring the ink from the stone to the paper. The stone can be dampened and re-inked repeatedly without losing detail.

## How to create it

### 1. Preparing the Stone
- Source a flat, thick slab of dense limestone (Solnhofen limestone is historically preferred for its purity).
- Grind and polish the surface perfectly flat using fine sand and water until it has a smooth, satin texture.

### 2. Creating the Drawing
- Formulate a greasy drawing medium by melting together **[Beeswax](/en/materials/beeswax)**, tallow, soap, and lampblack. Roll this into crayons or dissolve it in spirits to use as liquid ink.
- Draw the image directly on the polished stone (in reverse).

### 3. Etching and Printing
- Mix a dilute solution of nitric acid with a gum arabic solution (or starch paste) and brush it over the entire stone. Let it dry to chemically fix the image.
- Wet the stone with a damp sponge.
- Roll oil-based ink (**[Ink](/en/materials/ink)** mixed with linseed or **[Vegetable Oil](/en/materials/vegetable-oil)**) across the stone.
- Lay a sheet of dry **[Paper](/en/materials/paper)** on top and pass it through a flatbed scraper press to transfer the image.

## Materials needed

- **Limestone Slab**: A flat piece of fine-grained calcium carbonate stone. Sourced from **[Limestone](/en/materials/limestone)**.
- **Water**: Essential to keep the bare stone wet during inking. Sourced from **[Water](/en/materials/water)**.
- **Greasy Crayon/Ink**: Made from **[Beeswax](/en/materials/beeswax)**, tallow, or vegetable soap.
- **Oil-Based Ink**: Sourced from **[Ink](/en/materials/ink)**.
- **Paper**: For the final prints. Sourced from **[Paper](/en/materials/paper)**.
- **Vegetable Oil**: Used as a base binder for inks. Sourced from **[Vegetable Oil](/en/materials/vegetable-oil)**.

## Variants and improvements

- **Chromolithography**: Using multiple limestone plates (one for each color ink) to print high-quality, full-color illustrations.
- **Offset Lithography**: Instead of printing directly from the stone/metal plate to the paper, the ink is transferred ("offset") to a rubber roller, which then prints onto the paper. This is the foundation of modern high-speed industrial printing.
- **Photolithography**: Using light-sensitive chemical coatings to project images onto plates. This is the core technology used to manufacture modern silicon microchips and printed circuit boards.

## Limits and risks

- **Stone Fragility and Weight**: Lithographic limestone slabs are extremely heavy, bulky, and prone to cracking under high press pressure.
- **Chemical Sensitivity**: The chemical balance between the acidity of the etch and the water-oil balance must be carefully controlled; if the stone dries out during inking, the entire plate will smudge and be ruined.
- **Reversed Image**: The artist must draw the image backward on the stone for it to print correctly.
