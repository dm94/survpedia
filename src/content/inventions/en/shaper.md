# Brief description

A metal shaper is a linear reciprocating machine tool used to cut flat surfaces, keyways, slots, and complex linear profiles into metal workpieces. By pushing a single-point cutting tool back and forth across a work table using a mechanical ram driven by a quick-return mechanism, it allows accurate precision machining of metal components without requiring multi-toothed milling cutters.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Machining flat, horizontal, vertical, or angled surfaces on metal blocks and forgings.
- **Secondary Uses:** Cutting internal keyways, gear rack teeth, dovetails, splines, and T-slots.
- **Scale:** Workshop and manufacturing scale, ranging from small foot-powered or belt-driven shapers to heavy industrial shaping machines.

## Operating principle

1. **Reciprocating Motion:** A crank mechanism, slotted arm, or hydraulic cylinder moves a horizontal ram back and forth over the workpiece.
2. **Quick-Return Mechanism:** The forward cutting stroke moves at a slower speed with high mechanical advantage, while the return stroke retracts rapidly to save time.
3. **Automatic Table Feed:** At the end of each return stroke, a ratchet mechanism advances the work table perpendicularly by a small increment.
4. **Tool Relief (Clapper Box):** The single-point tool bit is held in a hinged clapper box that swings up slightly on the return stroke to avoid dragging or dulling the cutting edge against the workpiece.

## How to create it

1. **Cast & Frame Bed:** Cast a rigid **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** frame with heavy vertical and horizontal column ways to absorb cutting forces and prevent vibration.
2. **Drive Mechanism & Whitworth Crank:** Fabricate a large bull gear fitted with an adjustable crank pin to drive a slotted rocker arm, establishing the quick-return linear stroke for the ram.
3. **Ram & Clapper Box Assembly:** Machine a heavy sliding ram fitted with a tool head and hinged clapper box at the front to hold a hardened **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** cutting bit.
4. **Cross-Rail & Table:** Mount a movable work table on a cross-rail attached to the main column, allowing vertical position adjustment and horizontal feed.
5. **Feed Mechanism:** Attach a ratchet and pawl linkage connected to the main bull gear shaft to turn the cross-feed screw automatically after every stroke.

## Materials needed

- **Frame, Ram & Table:** **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or heavy structural **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** for damping and strength.
- **Drive Gears, Pins & Lead Screws:** Tough **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**.
- **Bearings & Bushings:** **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)** or brass.
- **Cutting Tools:** High-carbon tool steel or high-speed steel.

## Variants and improvements

- **Vertical Shaper (Slotter):** A shaper with a vertical ram, specialized for internal keyways and deep internal slots.
- **Hydraulic Shaper:** Replaces mechanical crank linkages with hydraulic cylinders for smooth, adjustable cutting stroke speeds.
- **Planer Machine:** A larger variation where the workpiece moves on a long table under a stationary tool ram, used for massive structural parts.

## Limits and risks

- **Stroke Length Limitations:** Best suited for workpieces shorter than the ram stroke (typically 30–60 cm).
- **Single-Point Tooling:** Slower material removal rate compared to multi-tooth rotary milling machines.
- **Safety Hazards:** High forces and reciprocating ram movements create pinch points; flying chips require eye protection.
