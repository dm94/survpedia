# Brief description

A mechanical device that generates high-voltage static electricity through friction or electrostatic induction without requiring permanent magnets or continuous chemical reactions. Early designs like the Wimshurst and Holtz machines produce tens of thousands of volts at very low currents.

## Use / Function

- **High-Voltage Research:** Generating sparks and electrical charges for physics experiments.
- **X-Ray Tube Power:** Supplying high potential to early Crookes and X-ray tubes before transformers.
- **Ozone Production:** Creating ozone through electrical discharges for air or water purification.
- **Electrotherapy & Ignition:** Providing ignition sparks or static fields in specialized apparatus.

## Operating principle

1. **Electrostatic Induction:** Two contra-rotating insulated glass or acrylic disks carry metal foil segments.
2. **Charge Separation:** Neutralizing brushes touch opposing segments simultaneously, transferring small initial charges.
3. **Positive Feedback:** As the disks spin in opposite directions, electrostatic induction amplifies charge differences exponentially.
4. **Collection:** Metal comb collectors pick up positive charges on one side and negative charges on the other without physical contact.
5. **Storage & Spark:** High voltage builds up in connected **[Leyden Jar](/en/inventions/leyden-jar)** capacitors until it discharges across an adjustable spark gap.

## How to create it

1. **Rotational Disks:** Mount two parallel glass or resin disks on a central axle driven by pulleys and belts to rotate in opposite directions.
2. **Conductive Segments:** Attach equal, evenly spaced tin or brass foil sectors radially around the outer face of both disks.
3. **Neutralizing Bars:** Position diagonal brass rods with wire brushes at 90-degree angles across each disk face.
4. **Charge Collectors:** Install U-shaped metal combs pointing close to the sectors at the horizontal diameter.
5. **Spark Gap & Leyden Jars:** Connect collectors to two metal electrodes with ball terminals and two Leyden jars to increase spark intensity.

## Materials needed

- **Disks:** **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** plates, acrylic, or shellac-coated wood.
- **Sectors:** **[Tin](/en/materials/tin)** foil or **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** sheet cutouts.
- **Conductors & Brushes:** **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** or **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** and thin brass hair.
- **Frame & Belts:** **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** frame and **[Leather](/en/materials/leather)** or rubber drive belts.
- **Capacitors:** Glass jars lined with foil to form **[Leyden Jar](/en/inventions/leyden-jar)** units.

## Variants and improvements

- **Friction Generators:** Earlier machines (e.g., Ramsden) rubbed glass disks against leather pads, generating less voltage.
- **Wimshurst Machine:** Self-starting induction machine unaffected by atmospheric humidity compared to earlier designs.
- **Van de Graaff Generator:** Modern variant using a moving rubber belt to carry charge to a hollow spherical terminal.

## Limits and risks

- **Low Current:** Generates thousands of volts, but extremely low amperage (microamperes), making it unsuitable for continuous power.
- **Humidity Sensitivity:** Damp air causes charge leakage across insulated surfaces.
- **Shock Hazard:** Leyden jars store significant energy; high-voltage discharges can cause painful shocks or involuntary muscular contraction.
