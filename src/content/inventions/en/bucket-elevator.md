# Brief description

A continuous vertical conveyor mechanism designed to elevate bulk loose materials (grain, flour, coal, gravel, or crushed ore) using a series of open buckets attached to a continuous loop of belt or chain moving between upper and lower sprockets or pulleys enclosed in a protective casing.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Vertical transport of bulk agricultural crops (wheat, corn, seeds) into storage silos and processing mills.
- **Secondary Uses:** Lifting coal, cement, limestone, or crushed mineral ores in chemical plants, foundries, and mining operations.
- **Scale:** From small farm mill elevators to industrial bulk handling terminals.

## Operating principle

1. **Continuous Loop:** An endless belt (made of heavy rubberized fabric or leather) or double strand of steel roller chain loops over a driven top head pulley and a bottom boot pulley.
2. **Scooping/Scoop Feeding:** As buckets pass around the bottom boot pulley submerged in a boot hopper, they scoop up incoming loose material.
3. **Vertical Elevation:** The moving belt or chain carries the filled buckets vertically upward inside a tall housing tube.
4. **Centrifugal Discharge:** As buckets loop over the top head pulley at speed, centrifugal force throws the material outward into a discharge chute.

## How to create it

1. **Build the Head and Boot Sections:** Construct a lower boot casing with a material inlet hopper and an upper head casing with a discharge spout from timber or sheet metal.
2. **Mount Shafts and Pulleys:** Install a top drive shaft with a crowned pulley/sprocket on bearings at the head, and a sliding tension shaft at the boot.
3. **Assemble the Loop Belt or Chain:** Splice a heavy multi-ply canvas, rubber, or leather belt (or double roller chain strand) to fit the center distance tightly.
4. **Attach Buckets:** Fasten deep metal or stiffened canvas buckets uniformly along the outer face of the belt using flat countersunk elevator bolts.
5. **Install Casing and Drive Mechanism:** Enclose the ascending and descending legs in twin wooden or sheet metal casings to contain dust, and connect the top shaft to a reduction gear or pulley system.

## Materials needed

- **Buckets:** Pressed **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**, **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** sheet, or rigid molded composite.
- **Belt / Chain:** Multi-ply **[Fabric](/en/materials/fabric)**, heavy **[Leather](/en/materials/leather)**, or forged steel chain.
- **Drive Pulleys & Shafts:** **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** shafts and sprockets.
- **Enclosure Structure:** Structural **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** sheet housing.
- **Power Coupling:** Drive **[Rope](/en/materials/rope)** or belt pulleys.

## Variants and improvements

- **Centrifugal Discharge Elevator:** High-speed bucket loop where centrifugal force clears buckets at the head discharge spout.
- **Continuous Bucket Elevator:** Slower-moving loop where buckets are placed side by side with no gap; as one bucket turns over the head pulley, its back acts as a chute for the following bucket (ideal for fragile or coarse materials).
- **Positive Discharge Elevator:** Buckets are inverted over a secondary idler sprocket at the head to ensure sticky or damp materials dump completely.

## Limits and risks

- **Dust Explosion Hazard:** Fine agricultural or coal dust inside enclosed elevator casings can ignite explosively from friction sparks or static electricity; explosion relief vents are critical.
- **Belt Slippage & Jamming:** Overshed material in the boot can jam the lower pulley or cause belt slippage on the head pulley.
- **Bucket Rollback:** If power fails while fully loaded, gravity will pull the heavy ascending leg down backward; a mechanical ratchet backstop is required on the head shaft.
