# Brief description

A chip log is a classical navigation tool used by mariners to estimate the speed of a vessel through water. It consists of a wooden board attached to a knotted line, which is dropped overboard to measure distance covered over a fixed time interval measured by a sandglass.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Measuring ship speed through water in knots (nautical miles per hour).
- **Secondary Uses:** Dead reckoning navigation and estimating distance traveled between celestial position fixes.
- **Scale:** Maritime navigation (individual vessel scale).

## Operating principle

1. **Hydrodynamic Drag:** The wooden board (chip) is weighted with lead along one rounded edge so it floats vertically in water, acting as a drag anchor that remains relatively stationary in the water column.
2. **Line Unspooling:** As the ship moves forward, the log line unspools freely from a reel.
3. **Knot Counting:** Knots tied at precise uniform intervals along the line pass through an observer's hands while a 28-second or 30-second sandglass runs.
4. **Speed Calculation:** The number of knots paid out during the sandglass time directly indicates the ship's speed in knots.

## How to create it

1. **Shape the Chip:** Cut a quarter-circle piece of hardwood, weighted at the curved edge with embedded **[Lead](/en/materials/lead)** so that it floats upright in water.
2. **Rig the Bridle:** Attach a three-part rope bridle to the corners of the chip log so it pulls perpendicular to the water when submerged. Secure one leg with a removable wooden peg that releases under a sharp tug to retrieve the log flat through the water.
3. **Prepare the Log Line:** Measure a long, light **[Rope](/en/materials/rope)** line (approx. 150–200 meters). Tie knots along the line at intervals proportional to the sandglass duration (e.g., 47 feet 3 inches apart for a 28-second sandglass).
4. **Construct the Sandglass:** Create a small glass or sealed double flask containing fine dry **[Sand](/en/materials/sand)** calibrated to drain in exactly 28 seconds.
5. **Mount on Reel:** Wind the line onto a free-spinning wooden reel.

## Materials needed

- **Wood:** Hardwood board for the chip and a turning reel.
- **Rope:** Flexible cordage/line for the main log line and bridle.
- **Lead:** **[Lead](/en/materials/lead)** weight for sinking the bottom edge of the board.
- **Glass & Sand:** Sealed glass vessel and uniform dry **[Sand](/en/materials/sand)** for the timer.

## Variants and improvements

- **Standard Chip Log:** The classic wooden board and sandglass system used from the 16th to 19th centuries.
- **Mechanical Impeller Log:** Submerged propeller/impeller turned by water flow and linked to a mechanical counter.
- **Pitometer Log:** Measures dynamic fluid pressure differences using a Pitot tube.

## Limits and risks

- **Current Interference:** Measures speed through water, not speed over ground; ocean currents can distort navigational dead reckoning.
- **Fouling & Snagging:** Floating debris or seaweed can catch on the line or chip log, giving inaccurate readings.
- **Human Error:** Requires precise coordination between the crew member casting the log and the timekeeper holding the sandglass.
