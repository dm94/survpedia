# Brief description

A fuel injector is a mechanical or electromechanical valve that delivers atomized fuel directly into the combustion chamber or intake air stream of an internal combustion engine at precise pressures and timings.

## Use / Function

- **Primary use**: Spraying finely atomized liquid fuel (diesel, gasoline, or biofuel) under high pressure into engine cylinders for efficient combustion.
- **Secondary uses**: Reducing emissions, optimizing air-fuel ratios across varied engine speeds and loads.
- **Scale**: Small single-cylinder engines to massive marine diesel engines.

## Operating principle

**High Pressure Atomization and Hydrodynamic Metering**:

1. **Pressure Accumulation**: Liquid fuel is pressurized by a high-pressure mechanical pump.
2. **Needle Valve Lift**: The high fuel pressure overcomes the force of a heavy spring, lifting a precision-machined needle valve off its seat.
3. **Spray Atomization**: Fuel is forced through microscopic nozzle orifices at high velocity, breaking into a fine mist of tiny droplets.
4. **Combustion**: The fine fuel mist mixes intimately with compressed hot air, igniting rapidly and cleanly.

## How to create it

### Mechanical Direct Injector (Bosch Type)

1. Machine a high-strength **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** injector body with internal precision channels and a ground needle seat.
2. Precision-grind a hardened steel needle valve to fit the body with clearance measured in micrometers.
3. Position a heavy calibrated **[Spring Steel](/en/materials/spring-steel)** coil spring above the needle to hold it firmly closed against normal pressure.
4. Drill micro-holes (0.15–0.3 mm diameter) at the nozzle tip using precision drills or electrical discharge machining.
5. Calibrate opening pressure by adjusting shims behind the spring.

## Materials needed

- **Body and Nozzle Valve**: Hardened tool **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or alloy steel.
- **Springs**: High-fatigue **[Spring Steel](/en/materials/spring-steel)**.
- **Seals and Bushings**: Precision **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** or copper washers for high-temperature sealing.
- **Tools**: Precision lathe, internal grinding machine, micrometer, pressure testing bench.

## Variants and improvements

- **Mechanical Injectors**: Driven purely by fuel hydraulic pressure overcoming a mechanical spring.
- **Common Rail Electronic Injectors**: Solenoid or piezoelectric actuation allowing multiple injection events per cycle at extremely high pressures (over 2000 bar).
- **Unit Injectors**: Integrated pump and nozzle assembly per cylinder driven directly by the camshaft.

## Limits and risks

- **Tight Tolerances**: Requires machining tolerances of 1–2 micrometers; poor precision results in fuel leakage or seized needles.
- **Fuel Cleanliness**: Microscopic particles or dirt in fuel will instantly clog or score the nozzle orifices; high-grade fuel filtration is strictly required.
- **High Pressure Hazards**: Fuel leaks under ultra-high pressure can penetrate human skin, causing severe tissue damage and toxic blood poisoning.
