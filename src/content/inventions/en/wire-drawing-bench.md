# Brief description

A metalworking machine bench that reduces thick metal rods into thin, uniform wire by pulling ductile metal through a series of progressively smaller conical holes (dies) in a hardened steel drawplate. The resulting wire is essential for electrical generators, telegraph lines, springs, screens, cables, and fasteners.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Manufacturing long, uniform metal wires (copper, iron, steel, brass) of precise diameters.
- **Secondary Uses:** Work-hardening metal wire for high-tensile springs, musical instrument strings, and structural cables.
- **Scale:** Hand-operated workshop bench to waterwheel-powered or steam-driven industrial wire mills.

## Operating principle

1.  **Preparation & Tapering:** The end of a forged metal rod is hammered thin to pass through the selected drawplate die hole and grip with tongs.
2.  **Tensile Pulling:** High mechanical pulling force—supplied by a winch drum, hand lever, or waterwheel crankshaft—pulls the rod through the die hole.
3.  **Plastic Deformation:** Compression inside the tapered die compresses the metal's cross-sectional area while stretching its length.
4.  **Annealing:** Because drawing increases brittleness (work hardening), wire must be periodically heated in a furnace and slow-cooled (annealed) between reduction stages.

## How to create it

1.  **Bench Frame:** Build a rigid wooden work bench reinforced with iron straps to withstand immense pulling tension.
2.  **Drawplate:** Forge a heavy plate from hardened tool steel and drill a series of smooth, tapered conical holes ranging from large rod sizes down to fine wire diameters.
3.  **Gripping Tongs & Winch:** Mount a heavy wooden or iron winding drum (winch) equipped with a ratchet and attach a stout chain to self-tightening steel drawing tongs.
4.  **Lubrication Stand:** Install a grease or soap basin at the entrance of the drawplate to reduce friction and die wear during drawing.

## Materials needed

- **Bench & Winch Frame:** Heavy **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** timber and **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** mounting hardware.
- **Drawplate & Tongs:** Hardened carbon **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or alloy tool steel.
- **Lubricant:** Animal fat, tallow, or soap flakes to minimize die friction.

## Variants and improvements

- **Manual Hand Drawplate:** Primitive handheld steel plate clamped in a vise for short wire lengths.
- **Lever / Winch Draw Bench:** Hand-cranked or horse-powered bench for thicker industrial rod sizes.
- **Continuous Multi-Die Wire Machine:** Modern industrial machine passing wire continuously through multiple powered drums and die blocks submerged in liquid coolant.

## Limits and risks

- **Wire Snap:** Excessive tension or un-annealed brittle metal can snap under load, releasing whipping wire ends that can inflict severe injuries.
- **Die Wear:** Frictional wear widens the die holes, requiring re-forging and re-drilling of the drawplate holes.
- **Ductility Limits:** Metals with low ductility (such as cast iron) cannot be drawn into wire.
