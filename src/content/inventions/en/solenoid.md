# Brief description

A solenoid is an electromechanical device consisting of a cylindrical coil of insulated wire wrapped around a movable soft iron plunger or core. When energized with an electric current, it generates a controlled uniform magnetic field that pulls or pushes the plunger, converting electrical energy into linear mechanical force.

## Use / Function

- **Electromechanical Actuation:** Converts electrical signals directly into push or pull linear physical movement.
- **Fluid & Gas Control Valves:** Drives solenoid valves to start, stop, or divert water, fuel, or compressed air in automated piping systems.
- **Circuit Breakers & Relays:** Actuates high-current electrical switches, starter motors, and safety interlocks in machinery.
- **Automated Latching:** Controls door locks, starter pinions, and mechanical detents in industrial and automotive machinery.
- **Scale:** Sub-centimeter micro-actuators to heavy industrial equipment.

## Operating principle

1. **Magnetic Field Generation:** Passing an electric current through the helical insulated **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** coil generates a uniform magnetic field along the coil's central longitudinal axis.
2. **Flux Concentration:** A high-permeability soft **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** core inside or near the coil concentrates magnetic flux lines, dramatically increasing the magnetic pull force.
3. **Plunger Displacement:** The magnetic field exerts an attractive force on a movable iron plunger, drawing it rapidly toward the center of the coil.
4. **Spring Return:** When the electrical power is cut, the magnetic field collapses instantly, and an internal spring restores the plunger to its resting position.

## How to create it

1. **Wind the Coil:** Uniformly wind hundreds of turns of enameled **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** around a hollow plastic or fiber bobbin tube.
2. **Fabricate the Plunger:** Machining a soft **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** cylindrical rod to fit smoothly inside the bobbin tube with minimal friction.
3. **Enclose in Iron Frame:** Surround the coil with an outer soft **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** frame (yoke) to complete the low-reluctance magnetic circuit path and boost output force.
4. **Install Return Spring:** Insert a helical steel spring at the base of the plunger to provide mechanical return action when de-energized.
5. **Connect Terminals and Test:** Connect the wire ends to terminal pins, apply a DC voltage source, and verify rapid linear plunger movement and spring return.

## Materials needed

- **Coil:** Magnet wire (enameled **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)**) and insulated bobbin tube.
- **Magnetic Core & Yoke:** High-purity soft **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** rod and outer steel frame.
- **Mechanical Assembly:** Non-magnetic return spring and mounting bracket.

## Variants and improvements

- **Linear Push-Pull Solenoid:** Standard bidirectional or single-acting linear solenoid used for latching and mechanical switching.
- **Rotary Solenoid:** Uses inclined ball-bearing ramps to convert linear core movement into high-torque rotational stroke.
- **Proportional Solenoid:** Varies plunger position smoothly in proportion to the input electric current rather than acting as a binary on/off switch.

## Limits and risks

- **Inductive Voltage Spikes:** Cutting coil current rapidly produces a dangerous back-EMF voltage spike that can damage driving transistors without a flyback diode.
- **Joule Heating:** Continuous energization causes electrical resistance heating in the coil, risking insulation breakdown and fire if uncooled.
- **Mechanical Friction & Wear:** Repetitive movement causes plunger wall friction and mechanical fatigue over millions of cycles.
