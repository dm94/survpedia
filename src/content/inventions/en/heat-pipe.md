# Brief description

A passive heat-transfer device that combines thermal conductivity and liquid-vapor phase transition to transfer heat across significant distances with extremely high effective thermal conductivity. With no moving parts, a heat pipe rapidly absorbs heat at an evaporator end and discharges it at a condenser end in a closed, continuous thermodynamic loop.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Passive, high-efficiency heat transport from hot concentrated components or solar collectors to radiators/coolers.
- **Secondary Uses:** Thermal management in electronics, waste-heat recovery in kilns/boilers, and temperature equalization in sensitive instruments.
- **Scale:** Micro-pipes in electronics to large industrial heat-recovery heat exchangers.

## Operating principle

1.  **Evaporation:** At the hot interface (evaporator), working fluid inside the sealed vacuum tube absorbs heat and vaporizes.
2.  **Vapor Flow:** The expanding vapor travels rapidly down the center cavity toward the cooler end due to a pressure gradient.
3.  **Condensation:** At the cold interface (condenser), the vapor condenses back into liquid, releasing its latent heat of vaporization.
4.  **Capillary Return:** The liquid working fluid is absorbed by an internal porous wick structure and returns to the evaporator by capillary action (or gravity in a thermosyphon), repeating the cycle endlessly without external pumps.

## How to create it

1.  **Envelope Vessel:** Prepare a clean, high-thermal-conductivity tube (such as copper or aluminum) sealed at one end.
2.  **Wick Structure:** Line the inner wall with a fine mesh screen, sintered metal powder, or longitudinal micro-grooves to create capillary suction.
3.  **Working Fluid Selection:** Fill the tube with a precise quantity of working fluid (distilled water, ethanol, or methanol) matched to the operating temperature range.
4.  **Evacuation & Sealing:** Evacuate non-condensable air from the vessel (creating a partial vacuum so the liquid boils at lower temperatures) and crimp/weld the remaining open tip shut.

## Materials needed

- **Pipe Outer Envelope:** **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)**, **[Aluminum](/en/materials/aluminum)**, or **[Stainless Steel](/en/materials/stainless-steel)** tube.
- **Wick Material:** Fine copper or stainless steel wire mesh, or sintered metal powder.
- **Working Fluid:** **[Water](/en/materials/water)** (for 30°C–200°C), **[Alcohol](/en/materials/alcohol)** (for lower temperatures), or ammonia.

## Variants and improvements

- **Gravity-Assisted Thermosyphon:** Simplest variant lacking an internal wick structure; relies purely on gravity to return condensed liquid to the bottom evaporator.
- **Sintered Powder Wick:** Highest capillary capability, allowing heat pipes to operate upside down or against gravity.
- **Loop Heat Pipe (LHP):** Separates vapor and liquid pathways for long-distance heat transport with lower flow resistance.
- **Variable Conductance Heat Pipe (VCHP):** Incorporates a non-condensable gas reservoir to passively regulate operating temperature.

## Limits and risks

- **Dry-Out Limit:** Excessive heat flux vaporizes liquid faster than the wick can return it, causing sudden loss of thermal conductance.
- **Entrainment Limit:** High vapor velocity can sweep returning liquid droplets away from the wick back toward the condenser.
- **Non-Condensable Gas Contamination:** Gas leaks or internal chemical reactions degrade vacuum pressure, drastically lowering efficiency.
- **Freezing/Bursting:** Freezing of water working fluid at sub-zero temperatures can deform or burst the outer metal envelope.
