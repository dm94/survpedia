# Brief description

A Bunsen burner is a fundamental laboratory gas burner that mixes combustible gas with regulated amounts of ambient air prior to ignition. Developed by Robert Bunsen and Peter Desaga in 1855, it produces a high-temperature, non-luminous, sootless flame ideal for chemical analysis, glassworking, and laboratory heating.

## Use / Function

- **Chemical Heating & Reaction Acceleration:** Provides clean, controllable heating for boiling liquids, evaporating solvents, and driving endothermic chemical reactions.
- **Qualitative Analysis (Flame Tests):** Emits minimal background light, allowing characteristic spectral emission colors of metallic elements to be easily observed.
- **Sterilization:** Rapidly sterilizes metal needles, wire loops, and glass instruments through direct thermal exposure in microbiological procedures.
- **Glassworking:** Softens glass tubing for bending, drawing capillary tubes, and sealing test tubes or ampoules.
- **Scale:** Laboratory benchtop apparatus.

## Operating principle

1. **Gas Injection:** Pressurized fuel gas (**[Propane](/en/materials/propane)**, **[Biogas](/en/materials/biogas)**, or natural gas) enters through a small orifice (jet) at the base of a vertical metal barrel.
2. **Venturi Induction:** As high-velocity gas shoots upward through the barrel, it creates a localized low-pressure zone (Venturi effect) that draws surrounding air into adjustable collar holes at the base.
3. **Premixing:** The fuel gas and oxygen-rich air thoroughly mix as they flow upward through the hollow tube before reaching the top opening.
4. **Controlled Combustion:** Igniting the gas mixture at the burner orifice creates a stable flame. Adjusting the rotating collar controls air intake: a closed vent yields a cooler yellow safety flame, while an open vent yields a hot, two-zone blue flame reaching up to 1,500°C.

## How to create it

1. **Machining the Metal Base:** Cast or lathe-turn a heavy **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** or cast iron base with an internal gas inlet tube to prevent tipping during operation.
2. **Drill Gas Jet Orifice:** Drill a micro-pinhole nozzle in the center of the gas inlet to accelerate fuel gas flow into a high-speed jet.
3. **Fabricate the Barrel Tube:** Cut a hollow **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** cylinder with threads at its base and drill wide air-entry ports near the bottom end.
4. **Fit the Adjustable Air Collar:** Craft a tight-fitting rotating sleeve with matching air ports that fits over the barrel's lower entry holes to regulate oxygen intake.
5. **Assemble and Test Seal:** Screw the barrel onto the base over the gas jet nozzle, connect a flexible hose to a gas supply (**[Propane](/en/materials/propane)** or **[Biogas](/en/materials/biogas)**), test for leak-free joint seals with soapy water, and ignite.

## Materials needed

- **Burner Body:** Machined **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** tube, threaded collar, and heavy base.
- **Fuel Source:** Combustible gas supply such as **[Propane](/en/materials/propane)**, methane, or **[Biogas](/en/materials/biogas)**.
- **Hose & Valves:** Flexible rubber or synthetic hose with needle-valve flow control.

## Variants and improvements

- **Meker-Fisher Burner:** Features a top grid matrix to divide the flame into multiple smaller flamelets, preventing flashback and reaching higher temperatures (~1,700°C).
- **Tirrill Burner:** Adds an integrated needle valve at the bottom of the gas jet for independent fine-tuning of both gas volume and air intake.
- **Micro-Burner:** Compact version designed for fine flame operations, micro-scale chemistry, and field work.

## Limits and risks

- **Flashback Hazard:** Excess air flow or reduced gas pressure can cause the flame to burn back down inside the barrel into the gas nozzle, overheating the burner body.
- **Fire & Burn Risks:** Open high-temperature blue flames are nearly invisible in bright daylight and pose severe fire and skin burn hazards.
- **Toxic Gas Accumulation:** Unignited fuel gas leakage can lead to explosive vapor accumulation or asphyxiation in poorly ventilated spaces.
