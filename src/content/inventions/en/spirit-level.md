# Brief description

A spirit level (or bubble level) is an instrument designed to indicate whether a surface is horizontal (level) or vertical (plumb). Invented by Melchisédech Thévenot around 1661, it utilizes a sealed curved glass vial containing a volatile liquid (spirit) and an air bubble to determine true horizontal and vertical alignment with high accuracy.

## Use / Function

- **Masonry and Carpentry:** Ensures foundations, walls, timber frames, and beams are perfectly level or plumb during construction.
- **Surveying and Civil Engineering:** Mounts onto theodolites, transits, and leveling staffs to establish horizontal reference planes across long distances.
- **Machinery Installation:** Verifies that heavy lathes, mills, surface plates, and steam engines are seated evenly to prevent distortion and uneven wear.
- **Scale:** Handheld workshop tool up to large surveying instrument component.

## Operating principle

1. **Buoyancy and Gravity:** An air bubble trapped inside a liquid-filled container always rises to the highest point owing to buoyancy and gravitational forces.
2. **Vial Curvature:** The interior of the glass tube is ground with a slight barrel-like longitudinal curve or arc.
3. **Equilibrium Position:** When the vial is perfectly horizontal, the highest point of the arc lies precisely at the midpoint. Graduations etched on the outside of the vial mark this center point; when the bubble is centered between the lines, the surface is level.
4. **Low Surface Tension Liquid:** Alcohol or **[Ether](/en/materials/ether)** is used instead of water because of its low surface tension and low viscosity, enabling rapid bubble movement and preventing freezing in cold climates.

## How to create it

1. **Forming and Grinding the Glass Vial:** Heat a tough **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** tube, seal one end, and precision-grind the interior surface to produce a uniform gentle barrel curvature.
2. **Filling and Sealing:** Fill the vial nearly to capacity with ethanol or **[Ether](/en/materials/ether)**, leaving a small pocket of air. Torch-seal the open end of the glass tube under controlled thermal conditions.
3. **Etching Target Lines:** Etch pair markings symmetrically around the apex center of the glass cylinder using fine acid or abrasive.
4. **Fabricating the Stock/Frame:** Mill a straight, rigid body stock out of aged hardwood (**[Wood](/en/materials/wood)**) or machined **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)**/**[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** with flat, parallel reference faces.
5. **Calibrating and Potting the Vial:** Place the stock on a verified horizontal reference surface. Set the sealed glass vial into a carved recessed opening, adjust until the bubble centers exactly, and cement in place with plaster, pitch, or epoxy resin.

## Materials needed

- **Sealed Vial:** Hardened **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** tube.
- **Liquid Fill:** Ethanol or **[Ether](/en/materials/ether)** (low freezing point and low surface tension).
- **Body Stock:** Seasoned hardwood (**[Wood](/en/materials/wood)**), cast **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)**, or structural **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**.
- **Fixing Medium:** Plaster, resin, or pitch for securing vial setting.

## Variants and improvements

- **Plumb Vial:** Incorporates a second vial mounted perpendicular to the base for vertical measurements.
- **45-Degree Vial:** Adds a diagonal vial to check miters and angular chamfers.
- **Precision Engineer's Level:** Uses ground tubular vials with sensitivity of 0.02 mm/m for setting up machine tools.
- **Bullseye Level:** A circular flat-bottomed vial that measures inclination in two axes simultaneously.

## Limits and risks

- **Thermal Expansion:** Extreme temperature changes expand the liquid, altering bubble size and slightly affecting response speed.
- **Vial Dislodgement:** Dropping or knocking the tool can shift the vial out of calibration within its housing.
- **Reference Surface Wear:** Dents or burrs on the bottom metal or wood reference edge destroy measurement accuracy.
