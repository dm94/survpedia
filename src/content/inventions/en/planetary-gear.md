# Brief description

A planetary gear (or epicyclic gear system) is a gear assembly consisting of one or more outer planet gears revolving around a central sun gear, enclosed within an outer internal-toothed ring gear (annulus). It enables high power density, compact gear reduction, or speed multiplication in a coaxial shaft arrangement.

## Use / Function

- **Automatic and Hub Transmissions**: Providing multiple gear ratios in automotive automatic gearboxes, heavy construction vehicles, and bicycle internal hub gears.
- **Speed Reducers**: High-torque gearboxes for industrial machinery, wind turbines, electric vehicle powertrains, and robotics.
- **Differential Mechanisms**: Distributing power between driven axles or combining mechanical power inputs.
- **Scale**: Small precision instrument gearheads to multi-megawatt industrial gearboxes.

## Operating principle

The mechanism coordinates three main components: a central sun gear, multiple planet gears mounted on a rotating planet carrier, and an outer ring gear (annulus).

1. **Torque Distribution**: Input torque is transmitted to either the sun gear, planet carrier, or ring gear while holding one of the other components stationary or controlling its rotation.
2. **Epicyclic Motion**: As the sun gear rotates, it drives the planet gears, which mesh simultaneously with the sun gear and the outer ring gear, forcing the planet carrier to rotate.
3. **Coaxial Speed Transformation**: Because the load is divided among multiple planet gears, high gear reduction or speed multiplication is achieved compactly along a single central shaft axis.

## How to create it

1. **Machining the Sun and Planet Gears**: Cut spur or helical gear teeth into cylindrical metal blanks to create the central sun gear and identical planet gears with matching pitch and module.
2. **Fabricating the Ring Gear (Annulus)**: Machine an internal gear with inward-facing teeth designed to mesh with the planet gears.
3. **Constructing the Planet Carrier**: Fabricate a rigid disc or cage with precisely spaced shafts or pins to hold the planet gears at fixed angular offsets with low friction.
4. **Assembly and Housing**: Mount the sun gear on a central shaft, fit the planet gears onto the carrier pins, slide the assembly inside the ring gear, and support all shafts with rolling-element bearings or bronze bushings.

## Materials needed

- **Essential materials**:
  - **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**: High-strength alloy steel for gear teeth and carrier pins to endure heavy contact stress and shear loads.
  - **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)**: Cast iron for rigid outer gear housings and gear bodies.
  - **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)**: Low-friction alloy for planet gear bushings or thrust washers.
- **Tools**:
  - Gear Shaping / Hobbing Machine: For precision cutting of internal ring gear teeth and external spur gear profiles.
  - **[Vernier Caliper](/en/inventions/vernier-caliper)**: For checking tooth pitch, gear center distances, and carrier pin alignment.
- **Possible substitutes**:
  - Machined hardwood or engineering thermoplastics: Suitable for low-torque, educational, or demonstration models.

## Variants and improvements

- **Compound Planetary Gear**: Features multiple tied planet gear sets on a single carrier to achieve extremely high reduction ratios in a single stage.
- **Helical Epicyclic Gear System**: Angled teeth provide smoother tooth engagement, higher load capacity, and quieter operation compared to straight spur gears.
- **Ravigneaux Planetary Gearset**: A complex dual-sun, dual-planet gear system widely used in automatic transmissions to yield multiple forward and reverse ratios.

## Limits and risks

- **Manufacturing Precision**: Requires tight tolerances on gear pitch and pin positioning; inaccuracies cause unequal load sharing among planets and premature failure.
- **Heat and Lubrication Requirements**: High power density generates localized friction, requiring continuous oil bath or pressure lubrication to prevent overheating and gear scoring.
- **Complex Assembly and Maintenance**: Disassembly and servicing require specialized tools due to nested gear sets and tight internal clearances.
