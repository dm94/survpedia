# Brief description

A grain silo is a structure designed for storing bulk grain after harvest under controlled conditions. It protects stored crops from moisture, heat, fungal contamination, and pests, ensuring multi-year food security.

## Use / Function

- **Primary use**: Long-term airtight or ventilated storage of harvested cereal grains, pulses, and seeds.
- **Secondary uses**: Seed preservation for future planting seasons, bulk pest control through controlled gas fumigation or anaerobic respiration.
- **Scale**: Small homestead pit silos, farm-level timber or brick tower silos, industrial steel elevator silos.

## Operating principle

**Moisture Control and Pest Asphyxiation**:

1. **Moisture Exclusion**: Sealed walls prevent ground water and atmospheric humidity from penetrating, keeping grain moisture content below 13% to prevent germination and fungal rot.
2. **Thermal Insulation**: Thick walls (earth, stone, or insulated metal) buffer diurnal temperature swings, preventing internal condensation.
3. **Anaerobic Micro-environment**: Sealed silos allow grain respiration and insects to consume remaining oxygen, producing carbon dioxide that smothers insect pests naturally.

## How to create it

### 1. Simple Underground Sealed Pit Silo

1. Dig a circular pit in well-drained soil above the water table.
2. Line the floor and walls with stone, burnt **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)** tiles, or concrete sealed with pitch or natural resin.
3. Thoroughly dry the harvested grain in sunlight until moisture content drops below 12%.
4. Pack the pit densely with dry grain to minimize air pockets.
5. Seal the top opening with a thick layer of clay, straw, and stone caps to form an airtight barrier.

### 2. Above-Ground Tower Silo

1. Construct a solid foundation using **[Stone](/en/materials/stone)** or **[Concrete](/en/materials/concrete)**.
2. Erect cylindrical walls using mortared brick, stone, or heavy **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** staves held together with iron hoops.
3. Fit an inclined roof to shed rainwater.
4. Install an airtight loading hatch at the top and a sealed discharge spout at the base.

## Materials needed

- **Structural Walls**: **[Stone](/en/materials/stone)**, **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)**, **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)** bricks, or **[Concrete](/en/materials/concrete)**.
- **Reinforcement**: **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)** hoops or iron bands.
- **Sealants**: Natural pitch, resin, lime mortar, or clay.
- **Tools**: Shovels, trowels, buckets, saws.

## Variants and improvements

- **Hermetic Bag Silos**: Flexible multi-layer polyethylene bags providing hermetic seals for smallholders.
- **Ventilated Aerated Silos**: Silos equipped with mechanical blowers to circulate ambient dry air through the grain column.
- **Metal Cylinder Silos**: Galvanized sheet iron silos that offer rodent proofing and simple modular assembly.

## Limits and risks

- **Spoilage Hazard**: Grain stored with moisture content above 14% will ferment, rot, or generate lethal heat.
- **Dust Explosions**: Suspended fine grain dust in enclosed spaces can ignite violently with a spark.
- **Asphyxiation Hazard**: High carbon dioxide or low oxygen inside sealed silos can cause fatal suffocation upon entry without ventilation.
