# Brief description

The Tesla turbine is a bladeless centripetal flow turbine patented by Nikola Tesla in 1913. Known as a boundary layer or cohesion turbine, it consists of a series of smooth parallel discs key-slotted to a central shaft and enclosed within a cylindrical housing. Instead of directing fluid against curved blades or buckets, the Tesla turbine relies on boundary layer adhesion and viscous drag of a moving fluid (steam, compressed air, or water) to rotate the discs at extremely high speeds.

## Use / Function

- **Primary use**: High-speed rotary mechanical drive and compact power generation using steam, compressed gas, or pressurized liquids.
- **Secondary uses**: Reversible fluid pump or blower when driven mechanically by an external motor; processing viscous fluids without clogging.
- **Scale**: Small to medium-scale mechanical installations and portable power generation units.

## Operating principle

**Boundary Layer and Viscous Drag Dynamics**:

1. **Fluid Injection**: Pressurized working fluid (steam or compressed air) enters through tangential nozzles at the outer perimeter of the cylindrical housing.
2. **Adhesion and Viscosity**: Due to surface tension and molecular adhesion, a thin layer of fluid adheres to the smooth surface of the rotating discs (boundary layer). Viscous drag transfers kinetic energy from the fluid to the discs.
3. **Spiral Inward Path**: As the fluid loses kinetic energy and velocity relative to the rotating discs, it spirals inward toward central exhaust ports located near the center shaft.
4. **Exhaust Discharge**: Spent fluid exits axially through central holes in the discs and housing vents, providing continuous smooth rotation without blade erosion or shock losses.

## How to create it

### 1. Rotor Disc Stack Assembly

1. Cut multiple thin, flat, circular discs of uniform thickness from **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)** sheets.
2. Drill central exhaust ports around the inner hub area of each disc and precision-cut keyways.
3. Mount the discs on a central **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** shaft using thin ring spacers to maintain a narrow, uniform gap between adjacent discs.

### 2. Cylindrical Casing and Tangential Nozzles

1. Machine or cast a cylindrical outer housing from **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or heavy-gauge steel.
2. Bore one or more tangential inlet nozzles into the perimeter of the housing positioned precisely at a tangent to the outer edges of the disc stack.

### 3. Shaft Bearings and End Plates

1. Secure end plates on both sides of the housing with low-friction high-speed bearings supporting the shaft.
2. Seal the housing perimeter to prevent pressurized fluid leakage while allowing central axial exhaust.

## Materials needed

- **Rotor Discs & Shaft**: Smooth **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** sheet or **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)** plate and ground steel shafting.
- **Housing & End Plates**: Heavy **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or structural steel casing.
- **Working Fluid**: Pressurized **[Water](/en/materials/water)**, steam, or compressed air.
- **Hardware**: Precision spacers, high-speed bearings, and heat-resistant seals.

## Variants and improvements

- **Reversible Tesla Turbine**: Features dual symmetrical nozzles angled in opposite directions, allowing instantaneous reversal of shaft rotation by switching the active nozzle valve.
- **Tesla Pump**: Reverses operation by driving the central shaft with an external engine to pump fluids radially outward with minimal fluid shearing.
- **Composite/Plastic Discs**: Modern lightweight disc materials for reduced rotational inertia and rapid acceleration in low-temperature compressed air applications.

## Limits and risks

- **Lower Thermal Efficiency**: At conventional operating speeds, thermal efficiency is lower than multi-stage bladed turbines due to fluid shear losses.
- **High Rotational Speed Requirements**: Optimal efficiency requires extremely high RPMs, placing high centrifugal stress on disc materials and bearings.
- **Precision Gap Criticality**: Disc spacing must be precisely matched to the viscosity and density of the working fluid; incorrect spacing severely degrades performance.
