# Brief description

A planimeter is a mechanical analog integrating instrument used to measure the surface area of arbitrary, irregular two-dimensional planar figures by tracing their perimeter. Invented by Swiss mathematician Jakob Amsler-Laffon in 1854, the polar planimeter replaced tedious manual geometric approximations and numerical approximations with a single smooth manual trace, becoming an indispensable tool for civil surveyors, cartographers, naval architects, and mechanical engineers calculating engine indicated power.

## Use / Function

- **Engine Indicator Diagram Analysis:** Integrating the pressure-volume $(P\text{-}V)$ indicator cards of steam and internal combustion engines to determine indicated horsepower.
- **Topographical Map Surveying:** Measuring watershed areas, agricultural plots, forest reserves, and land parcels on cartographic projections.
- **Naval Architecture:** Calculating cross-sectional waterplane areas, displacement hulls, and centers of buoyancy for vessel design.
- **Structural Engineering:** Finding the moment of inertia, centroid, and cross-sectional area of irregular architectural arches and structural beams.

## Operating principle

1. **Mechanical Linkage:** The instrument consists of two hinged arms: a pole arm anchored at a stationary pivot point, and a tracer arm carrying a fine tracing stylus and a precision measuring wheel.
2. **Boundary Tracing:** The operator uses the tracer stylus to guide the arm clockwise around the entire closed boundary perimeter of the irregular shape.
3. **Kinematic Wheel Motion:** The measuring wheel rests directly on the drawing paper; as the linkage articulates, the wheel simultaneously rolls along its perpendicular axis and slips parallel to its axis.
4. **Green's Theorem Integration:** Pure rolling displacement registers only movement perpendicular to the tracer arm, mechanically performing line integration $\oint (x\,dy - y\,dx)$ around the boundary contour in accordance with Green's theorem.
5. **Direct Area Readout:** A revolution counter drum with a vernier scale attached to the measuring wheel indicates the net integrated area directly in square centimeters, inches, or scaled square meters.

## How to create it

1. **Machine the Linkage Arms:** Mill two rigid, non-flexing arms from seasoned brass bars: the pole arm (with a heavy anchored pivot needle) and the tracer arm.
2. **Fabricate the Elbow Joint:** Join the two arms with a hardened steel conical pivot bearing that allows free coplanar articulation without lateral backlash or play.
3. **Turn the Integrating Wheel:** Turn a precision measuring wheel from hardened steel or brass with a knife-edged rim ground to an exact known rolling diameter.
4. **Mount Precision Bearings:** Mount the measuring wheel axle on low-friction adjustable steel screw centers positioned parallel to the tracer arm centerline.
5. **Engrave the Vernier Drum:** Divide the wheel perimeter into 100 equal graduations, and attach a geared revolution disc and stationary vernier plate reading to fractions of a division.
6. **Balance and Calibrate:** Fit a tracing point or magnifying reticle at the outer end of the tracer arm, and calibrate the instrument against an accurately scribed square or circular test template of known area.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** High-grade **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** for structural arms, weight anchors, and vernier frames; hardened **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** for pivot pivots, needle anchors, tracing pointers, and the knife-edged integrating wheel.
- **Tools:** Precision dividing engine for rotary scale graduation, metal lathe, jewelers' screwdrivers, abrasive polishing stones.
- **Substitutes:** A linear (rolling) planimeter uses wheels mounted on a straight track rather than a fixed polar pivot for very long drawing sheets.

## Variants and improvements

- **Amsler Polar Planimeter:** The classic dual-arm rotating linkage anchored to a single fixed point on the drawing board.
- **Linear / Rolling Planimeter:** Moves along an external cylindrical roller or guide rail, permitting measurement of long geographic strips without polar limit restrictions.
- **Compensating Planimeter:** Allows reversal of tracing arms to mathematically cancel slight mechanical eccentricities or wheel alignment errors.
- **Radial Planimeter:** Specialized for reading circular recording charts from barographs, pressure gauges, and gas meters.

## Limits and risks

- **Paper Surface Friction:** Uneven paper texture, wrinkles, or greasy smudges cause the measuring wheel to slip instead of roll, producing significant integration errors.
- **Backlash and Pivot Wear:** Any mechanical play or looseness in the hinged elbow bearing alters the effective arm geometry and skews calculations.
- **Tracing Drift:** Deviations from the drawn boundary line by the human operator directly distort the perimeter integral; skilled, steady tracing is essential.
