# Brief description

A De Laval nozzle (or convergent-divergent nozzle) is a tube pinched in the middle, resembling an hourglass. It accelerates a hot, pressurized gas passing through it to supersonic speeds, converting thermal and pressure energy into directional kinetic energy.

## Use / Function

- **Steam Turbines**: Directing high-velocity steam jets onto turbine blades to maximize mechanical energy output.
- **Rocket Propulsion**: Accelerating exhaust gases to high supersonic velocities to generate forward thrust.
- **Supersonic Ejectors and Jet Pumps**: Creating strong suction or high vacuum levels in chemical and industrial processes.
- **Scale**: Small laboratory ejectors to massive industrial rocket nozzles.

## Operating principle

The nozzle operates by exploiting the thermodynamic and gas dynamic properties of compressible fluids transitioning from subsonic to supersonic flow.

1. **Convergent Section**: Subsonic gas enters the wide inlet. As the cross-sectional area decreases toward the throat, the gas speeds up while its pressure and temperature decrease.
2. **Throat**: At the narrowest point (the throat), if the pressure ratio is sufficient, the gas reaches Sonic Velocity (Mach 1).
3. **Divergent Section**: Past the throat, as the cross-sectional area expands, compressible supersonic gas continues to accelerate rather than slow down, reaching supersonic speeds (Mach > 1).
4. **Energy Conversion**: Thermal and pressure energy from the high-pressure reservoir are converted efficiently into kinetic exhaust energy.

## How to create it

1. **Calculate Dimensions**: Determine the throat area and exit area ratio based on the available inlet pressure and target pressure ratio.
2. **Machining the Internal Profile**: Turn a cylinder of brass, bronze, or heat-resistant steel on a lathe, boring a smooth converging cone (around 20° total angle) down to the exact calculated throat diameter.
3. **Boring the Divergent Cone**: Machine a gradually expanding exit cone (typically 10° to 12° total angle) from the throat to the exit bell to avoid flow separation and shock losses.
4. **Polishing**: Smooth and mirror-polish the inner walls using fine abrasives to eliminate surface roughness that causes turbulence or internal shockwaves.
5. **Mounting**: Thread or flange the wide inlet onto a high-pressure gas chamber or steam boiler.

## Materials needed

- **Essential materials**:
  - **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or **[Stainless Steel](/en/materials/stainless-steel)**: High strength and thermal tolerance for steam or combustion gas nozzles.
  - **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** or **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)**: High thermal conductivity and easy machinability for low-temperature or compressed air testing nozzles.
- **Tools**:
  - **[Lathe](/en/inventions/lathe)** or **[Screw-Cutting Lathe](/en/inventions/screw-cutting-lathe)**: For precise internal conical boring and turning.
  - **[Calipers](/en/inventions/calipers)** or **[Vernier Caliper](/en/inventions/vernier-caliper)**: For verifying throat and exit diameters.
- **Possible substitutes**:
  - Dense hardwood or epoxy resin: Acceptable for cold-gas or low-pressure demonstrations.

## Variants and improvements

- **Conical Nozzle**: Simple straight-walled cone design; easy to manufacture but slightly less efficient due to radial gas divergence at the exit.
- **Bell Nozzle (Parabolic)**: Curved contour that redirects gas parallel to the centerline, shortening length and maximizing axial thrust.
- **Aerospike Nozzle**: Altitude-compensating nozzle that maintains high efficiency across varying atmospheric pressures.

## Limits and risks

- **Over-expansion and Under-expansion**: Operating at ambient pressures off design specifications causes efficiency losses and internal shockwaves.
- **Thermal Stress and Erosion**: High-temperature gas flow can melt or erode the narrow throat area, destroying nozzle geometry.
- **Pressure Requirement**: Supersonic flow requires a critical pressure ratio (inlet pressure roughly double ambient pressure); otherwise, the gas remains subsonic throughout.
