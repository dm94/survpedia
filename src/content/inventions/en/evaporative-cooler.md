# Brief description

An evaporative cooler is a device or structure that cools air through the evaporation of water. Utilizing the principle of sensible heat reduction through the latent heat of vaporization, it provides energy-efficient thermal cooling and food preservation in dry, hot climates without electrical refrigeration.

## Use / Function

- **Food Preservation:** Keeping produce, dairy, and perishable goods cold in off-grid environments.
- **Space Cooling:** Reducing ambient temperatures inside living quarters, workshops, or greenhouses.
- **Water Cooling:** Chilling drinking water stored in porous unglazed clay vessels.
- **Scale:** Household (pot-in-pot / Zeer pot), building ventilation (windcatchers with wet pads), and industrial evaporative pads.

## Operating principle

The evaporative cooler works on **Thermodynamic Phase Change**:

1. **Evaporation:** Un-saturated dry air moves across a damp, porous media soaked in **[Water](/en/materials/water)**.
2. **Heat Absorption:** Liquid water absorbs sensible thermal energy from the passing air and evaporates into water vapor.
3. **Temperature Drop:** The sensible heat used to evaporate water transitions into latent heat of vaporization, lowering the dry-bulb temperature of the air stream.
4. **Humidity Increase:** The exiting air stream is cooler but has higher relative humidity.
5. **Efficiency Limit:** Cooling effectiveness depends on dry-bulb and wet-bulb temperature difference; performance is highest in hot, low-humidity environments.

## How to create it

1. **Constructing the Vessel or Enclosure:** Form an outer housing or pot using porous **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)** or a **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** frame with mesh sides.
2. **Preparing the Inner Container:** For food preservation, insert an inner watertight or glazed clay container inside the outer housing, leaving a 2–5 cm gap between them.
3. **Adding Absorbent Medium:** Fill the gap between containers with wet sand, coarse soil, or line the frame with porous **[Fabric](/en/materials/fabric)**, charcoal, or cellulose pads.
4. **Moistening the System:** Saturate the sand or fabric pads thoroughly with **[Water](/en/materials/water)**.
5. **Ensuring Airflow:** Place the assembly in a shaded, well-ventilated location where ambient breeze or solar chimney air currents can continually pass over the exterior surface.
6. **Maintenance:** Regularly top up water to keep the absorbent medium constantly moist.

## Materials needed

- **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)**: For porous earthen pots or bricks.
- **[Water](/en/materials/water)**: The primary working fluid for evaporative phase change.
- **[Fabric](/en/materials/fabric)** or Sand: Porous wicking medium to hold water and expand surface area.
- **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)**: For structural framing or ventilation louvers.

## Variants and improvements

- **Zeer Pot (Pot-in-Pot):** Two nested clay pots separated by damp sand, capable of maintaining food down to 15°C (59°F) in desert conditions.
- **Swamp Cooler:** Mechanical fan pushing air through water-saturated pads, providing large-scale room cooling.
- **Windcatcher Integration:** Traditional architectural tower guiding wind over wet underground channels or porous jars.

## Limits and risks

- **Humidity Dependent:** Ineffective in humid or tropical climates where air is already near moisture saturation ($100\%$ relative humidity).
- **Water Consumption:** Requires a continuous supply of non-potable or brackish water.
- **Mold Growth:** Stagnant, wet media without sufficient ventilation can foster mold or bacteria.
