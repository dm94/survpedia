# Brief description

A dynamometer is an instrument designed to measure mechanical force, torque, or shaft power delivered by a prime mover such as a steam engine, water wheel, electric motor, or internal combustion engine. Pioneered by engineers like Gaspard de Prony in 1821 with his eponymous friction brake, dynamometers allowed early industrial societies to quantitatively evaluate mechanical efficiency, determine fuel economy, and establish standard units of mechanical power (brake horsepower).

## Use / Function

- **Engine Power Verification:** Measuring the brake horsepower and torque output of steam engines, turbines, and draft animals under variable mechanical loads.
- **Frictional Brake Testing:** Dissipating rotational energy via friction blocks to assess shaft load limits and thermal tolerance.
- **Tension & Drawbar Pull Measurement:** Quantifying the pulling force exerted by locomotives, tractors, towing cables, or industrial winches.
- **Machine Efficiency Audits:** Comparing raw chemical or thermal energy input against net usable shaft work to diagnose losses.

## Operating principle

1. **Torque Absorption:** An adjustable braking mechanism (such as wooden friction blocks clamped around a revolving flywheel) absorbs the engine's mechanical output, converting kinetic energy into friction heat.
2. **Moment Arm Counteraction:** As the shaft rotates against the friction surfaces, torque tends to drag the entire clamping assembly in the direction of rotation.
3. **Restoring Moment:** A rigid lever arm of precisely known radial length $L$ extends horizontally from the brake collar to resist rotation.
4. **Force Balancing:** The free end of the lever arm bears down upon a platform scale, counterweights, or a calibrated spring scale to measure the tangent reaction force $F$.
5. **Power Calculation:** The total shaft power is computed using the torque formula $P = 2 \pi N L F$, where $N$ is rotational speed in revolutions per second, yielding instantaneous mechanical work rate without altering internal engine kinematics.

## How to create it

1. **Fabricate the Friction Collar:** Shape semi-circular hardwood blocks (such as beech, oak, or lignum vitae) grooved to encircle a smooth cast iron pulley or engine flywheel.
2. **Assemble Tension Linkages:** Connect the two halves of the wooden brake band using threaded steel tie rods equipped with adjustment handwheels to vary clamping pressure.
3. **Attach the Moment Lever:** Bolt a rigid, balanced steel or seasoned timber lever beam extending radially outward at a known distance from the shaft center.
4. **Fit Cooling Quench Channels:** Drill water-cooling channels or weld a flanged internal rim onto the flywheel so water can be continuously circulated to carry away intense friction heat.
5. **Install Counterbalance & Scales:** Mount a knife-edge contact at the tip of the lever arm bearing directly on a calibrated weighing scale or tension spring dynamometer.
6. **Integrate Tachometer:** Position a mechanical revolution counter or centrifugal tachometer on the driven shaft to simultaneously log rotational velocity alongside force.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Hardened **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** for threaded clamp rods, lever arms, and pivot knife-edges; cast **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** for the brake pulley; dense **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** for high-friction brake blocks; **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** for bushing guides and scale fittings.
- **Tools:** Woodworking chisels and saws, metal lathe for turning the brake drum, thread-cutting dies, precision calibration weights.
- **Substitutes:** Leather or woven hemp belt linings can replace wooden blocks for lower-power absorption dynamometers.

## Variants and improvements

- **Prony Brake:** The classic absorption dynamometer using friction blocks, lever arm, and weights to determine brake horsepower.
- **Rope Brake Dynamometer:** Employs a flexible rope wrapped around a water-cooled flywheel, providing smooth friction without severe block chattering.
- **Hydraulic (Water Brake) Dynamometer:** Uses an impeller churning inside a water-filled stator casing to absorb huge shaft outputs through fluid turbulence.
- **Transmission Dynamometer:** Measures power transmitted through an elastic shaft or spring coupling without absorbing or wasting the mechanical output.

## Limits and risks

- **Intense Thermal Dissipation:** Frictional braking generates extreme heat; lack of continuous water cooling will scorch wooden blocks, boil lubricants, or seize the shaft.
- **Torque Chattering & Vibration:** Uneven friction or dry sticking can induce violent harmonic vibrations that damage engine bearings and destabilize scale readings.
- **Overspeed Risk:** If the brake slips or is suddenly released while the engine is at wide-open throttle, catastrophic flywheel burst or runaway may occur.
