# Brief description

An interferometer is a high-precision optical instrument that splits a beam of light into two or more separate paths and recombines them, creating an interference pattern of bright and dark fringes. It allows sub-micrometer measurement of distances, light wavelengths, and surface flatnesses.

## Use / Function

- **Precision Metrology:** Measuring distances and thermal expansion with accuracy on the order of light wavelengths (nanometers).
- **Surface Inspection:** Testing optical elements, mirrors, and lenses for microscopic surface defects or flatness.
- **Refractive Index Measurement:** Analyzing gases and liquids by observing phase shifts when light passes through a sample cell.
- **Fundamental Physics:** Measuring the speed of light, testing wave theories, and detecting gravitational waves (e.g., LIGO).

## Operating principle

1. **Beam Splitting:** A coherent or monochromatic light source strikes a semi-transparent mirror (beam splitter), dividing the beam into two paths at 90 degrees.
2. **Reflection:** The split beams travel along reference and test arms of equal or adjustable length, reflecting off flat mirrors back toward the beam splitter.
3. **Recombination & Interference:** When the reflected beams recombine at the beam splitter, their light waves overlap:
   - **Constructive Interference:** Wave crests align, creating bright fringes.
   - **Destructive Interference:** Crests meet troughs, creating dark fringes.
4. **Fringe Shift:** Any minute movement of one mirror by half a wavelength ($\lambda / 2$) shifts the fringe pattern by one full line.

## How to create it

1. **Light Source:** Use a monochromatic light source (such as a sodium lamp or laser diode) positioned behind a pinhole aperture.
2. **Beam Splitter:** Mount a partially silvered glass plate (50% reflective, 50% transmissive) at a 45-degree angle on a rigid, vibration-isolated base.
3. **Reference & Object Mirrors:** Place two optical flat mirrors on fine thread screws perpendicular to each other relative to the beam splitter.
4. **Compensator Plate:** Position an un-silvered glass plate of equal thickness in the reference arm to balance glass path lengths.
5. **Viewing Screen:** Position a frosted glass screen or eyepiece to observe the interference fringe rings or lines.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** High-flatness **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** optical plates (beam splitter and mirrors), rigid **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** or heavy iron mounting frame, **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or dampening base.
- **Tools:** Precision screws (micrometer threads), glass polishing abrasive powders.
- **Substitutes:** Silver-coated window glass for mirrors; helium-neon laser or monochromatic LED light.

## Variants and improvements

- **Michelson Interferometer:** The classic two-arm configuration used in precision metrology and the famous ether-drift experiment.
- **Mach-Zehnder Interferometer:** Uses two beam splitters and two mirrors; ideal for testing transparent gas flows or plasma dynamics.
- **Fabry-Pérot Interferometer:** Uses two highly reflective parallel glass plates to create sharp multipath interference fringes for high-resolution spectroscopy.

## Limits and risks

- **Vibration Sensitivity:** Environmental vibrations (footsteps, acoustic noise) ruin fringe stability unless mounted on heavy isolated tables.
- **Thermal Drift:** Temperature fluctuations expand mechanical arms, causing false fringe shifts.
- **Coherence Length:** Requires a light source with sufficient coherence length; broadband white light only forms fringes near zero path difference.
