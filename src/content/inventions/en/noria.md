# Brief description

A hydropowered water-raising machine consisting of a large vertical wooden wheel fitted with paddles around its rim and a series of buckets or earthenware pots attached along its perimeter. Driven solely by the kinetic energy of a flowing river current, it automatically scoops water from the river, lifts it as the wheel rotates, and empties it into an elevated aqueduct or trough.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Continuous agricultural irrigation of elevated crop fields and terraces without requiring human or animal labor or fossil fuel.
- **Secondary Uses:** Supplying clean river water to village aqueducts, public fountains, and storage cisterns.
- **Scale:** Community irrigation networks, riverbank agricultural estates, and municipal waterworks.

## Operating principle

1. **Hydraulic Thrust:** The river current strikes flat wooden paddles attached around the wheel's outer rim, providing continuous torque.
2. **Water Scooping:** Submerged buckets or clay pots mounted at an angle along the rim submerge completely at the bottom of the rotation, filling with river water.
3. **Elevation:** As the wheel turns, the filled pots are carried upward along the ascending arc of the wheel.
4. **Gravity Spill:** Near the top apex of the rotation, the tilt of the pots causes water to pour out into a stationary wooden trough or head aqueduct positioned alongside the wheel hub.

## How to create it

1. **Construct the Axle & Support Piers:** Build two sturdy stone or heavy timber piers in the riverbed and mount a thick oak or iron axle horizontally across them on bearings.
2. **Assemble Spoke Wheel Framework:** Build a large rimmed wheel (typically 5 to 20 meters in diameter) using triangular wood trussing radiating from central axle hubs.
3. **Install Drive Paddles:** Attach broad, flat wooden boards radially around the outer rim at uniform intervals to catch the stream current.
4. **Attach Water Containers:** Secure open-mouthed clay pots, hollow bamboo tubes, or wooden buckets along the rim, tilting them slightly so they fill quickly when submerged.
5. **Erect Aqueduct Receiving Trough:** Position a elevated wooden flume or stone channel right beside the top of the wheel arc to catch the spilling water stream.

## Materials needed

- **Wheel Framework & Paddles:** Rot-resistant timber **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** (oak, cedar, or cypress).
- **Water Containers:** Fired **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)** pots, wooden buckets, or hollow bamboo.
- **Axle & Reinforcements:** Forged **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** axle pins, bands, and bolts.
- **Bindings & Fasteners:** Tarred **[Rope](/en/materials/rope)** or leather lashings to bind pots securely to the wooden rim.

## Variants and improvements

- **Pot-Rim Noria:** Classic design where earthenware jars are lashed directly around the outer wheel rim.
- **Box-Rim Noria:** Wooden compartments are integrated into hollow rim boxes, reducing weight and eliminating fragile ceramic pots.
- **Geared Animal Noria (Saqiya):** Uses a horizontal animal-gear drive (ox or donkey) to turn a vertical bucket chain when flowing river current is unavailable.

## Limits and risks

- **Seasonal River Flow Dependency:** Requires a steady river flow velocity; flooding can tear away paddles, while dry seasons stop operation.
- **Structural Weight & Bearing Wear:** Large wooden norias are extremely heavy and require continuous axle lubrication to prevent grinding wear.
- **Fragility:** Ceramic pots can crack from debris floating in the river current; timber framework requires periodic rot maintenance and re-tying.
