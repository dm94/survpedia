# Brief description

A cable-tool mechanical drilling rig designed to penetrate hard rock strata and reach deep subterranean aquifers or mineral deposits. By repeatedly raising and dropping a heavy, chisel-shaped iron or steel bit suspended from a cable, the rig pulverizes rock at the bottom of the borehole. A flap-valve bailer tube is periodically lowered to remove crushed rock slurry.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Drilling deep water wells to secure safe potable water and irrigation without digging hazardous open shafts.
- **Secondary Uses:** Boring for brine, geothermal heat, natural gas, and crude oil deposits.
- **Scale:** Small tripod-mounted rigs for rural homesteads to heavy wooden derrick towers for industrial well drilling.

## Operating principle

1.  **Chisel Impact:** A heavy drill bit attached to a hemp rope or steel cable is raised several feet using a walking beam or pulley system and allowed to fall under gravity, shattering rock by direct kinetic impact.
2.  **Slurry Formation:** Water poured into the hole mixes with pulverized rock dust to form a thick muddy slurry.
3.  **Bailing:** The drill bit is hoisted out, and a hollow iron cylinder with a bottom check valve (bailer) is lowered to scoop up and clear the slurry.
4.  **Casing Drive:** Iron or steel casing pipes are driven down as drilling progresses to prevent borehole collapse and seal off contaminated surface water.

## How to create it

1.  **Derrick Frame:** Construct a tripod or four-legged wooden A-frame derrick over the target drilling site, fitted with a top pulley axle.
2.  **Walking Beam & Power:** Erect a balanced wooden walking beam driven by a hand crank, horse sweep, or engine pitman arm to alternate raising and dropping the cable.
3.  **Percussion Bit:** Forge a heavy, wedge-shaped chisel bit from wrought iron with a hardened tool steel cutting edge, attached securely to the cable.
4.  **Bailer Bucket:** Fabricate a long iron tube featuring a bottom leather or metal flap valve that opens when dropped into slurry and seals shut when hoisted.

## Materials needed

- **Derrick & Beam:** Strong **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** timbers and structural braces.
- **Cable:** Heavy **[Rope](/en/materials/rope)** (hemp/manila) or flexible **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** rope.
- **Drill Bit & Bailer:** Wrought **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** body with a **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** cutting tip.
- **Tools:** **[Forge](/en/inventions/forge)**, **[Anvil](/en/inventions/anvil)**, and **[Wrench](/en/inventions/wrench)** sets.

## Variants and improvements

- **Manual Spring-Pole Rig:** Simple primitive version using a bent wooden pole to spring the bit back up after manual foot-treadle drops.
- **Walking-Beam Cable Tool Rig:** Powered by steam or internal combustion engines for continuous 24-hour deep well drilling.
- **Rotary Drilling Rig:** Modern successor using rotating tri-cone bits and continuous fluid circulation for much faster penetration.

## Limits and risks

- **Borehole Collapse:** Unconsolidated sand or gravel layers can cave in without immediate casing installation.
- **Stuck Bit:** The bit can become jammed in rock fissures or under falling rubble, requiring specialized recovery spears.
- **Gas Blowouts:** Penetrating high-pressure gas or oil pockets can trigger violent blowouts or fires.
