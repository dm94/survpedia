# Brief description

Gauge blocks (also known as Jo-blocks or slip gauges) are precision-ground metal or ceramic blocks produced in precise standard lengths. Invented by Carl Edvard Johansson in 1896, they serve as the fundamental reference standard for dimensional measurement in mechanical engineering and precision manufacturing.

## Use / Function

- **Tool & Instrument Calibration:** Calibrates micrometers, calipers, dial indicators, and coordinate measuring machines against standardized physical lengths.
- **Interchangeable Manufacturing Setup:** Sets up precision fixtures, jigs, and machine tools to achieve exact tolerances required for mass-producing interchangeable mechanical parts.
- **Direct Precision Measurement:** Combines multiple blocks to measure narrow gaps, slots, and workpiece dimensions directly with sub-micrometer accuracy.
- **Scale:** Workshop toolmaking and industrial manufacturing.

## Operating principle

1. **Precision Surface Lapping:** Opposite measuring faces are lapped so flat and parallel (within fractions of a micrometer) that surface roughness is nearly zero.
2. **Molecular Wringing Effect:** When two clean gauge blocks are slid together with light pressure, atmospheric pressure and intermolecular attraction (van der Waals forces) bind them firmly together without adhesive.
3. **Additive Length Combination:** By wringing multiple blocks of varying standard thicknesses together, any precise length value can be assembled with minimal cumulative error.
4. **Thermal Stability Reference:** Calibrated at a standard reference temperature of 20°C (68°F) to account for thermal expansion.

## How to create it

1. **Select High-Stability Alloy:** Choose high-carbon chromium tool steel, hardened stainless steel, or tungsten carbide to withstand wear and dimensional drift.
2. **Rough Machining and Heat Treatment:** Cut steel blanks to oversize dimensions, harden through heat treatment, and stress-relieve via deep cryogenic cooling to prevent microstructural transformation over time.
3. **Rough Surface Grinding:** Precision-grind opposite parallel faces flat to within a few micrometers using a flat surface grinding machine.
4. **Precision Fine Lapping:** Lap parallel faces against a flat lap plate using ultra-fine abrasive slurries (aluminum oxide or diamond dust) until surface flatments reach optical interference level.
5. **Dimensional Calibration:** Compare finished blocks against primary laser interferometer standards or master gauge blocks on a surface plate, then mark standard length values.

## Materials needed

- **Block Substrate:** High-carbon chromium steel, tool steel, or ceramic blanks. Sourced from **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**.
- **Abrasives:** Fine polishing powder and diamond slurry for lapping. Sourced from **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** or silica abrasives.
- **Cleaning Agents & Oil:** Anhydrous alcohol for cleaning and light mineral oil for corrosion protection.

## Variants and improvements

- **Steel Gauge Blocks:** Traditional high-carbon tool steel blocks; durable and economic, requiring oil protection against rust.
- **Carbide Gauge Blocks:** Tungsten or titanium carbide blocks; offering extreme wear resistance and longevity in production environments.
- **Ceramic Gauge Blocks:** Zirconia ceramic blocks; non-magnetic, corrosion-proof, and highly resistant to scratching or burring.

## Limits and risks

- **Corrosion Susceptibility:** Steel blocks rust rapidly from skin oils or humidity, permanently degrading measurement accuracy.
- **Wringing Wear & Scratches:** Dust particles or burrs on faces can scratch precision surfaces during wringing, creating measurement errors.
- **Thermal Expansion Drift:** Temperature variations outside 20°C alter physical lengths significantly if thermal expansion coefficients are not factored in.
