# Brief description

The Turgo turbine is an impulse water turbine designed for medium hydraulic heads (typically 15 to 300 meters). Modified from the Pelton wheel, the Turgo turbine directs a high-velocity jet of water at an angle (usually 20 degrees) into one side of the runner buckets, allowing the water to exit from the opposite side. This side-entry design enables a higher volume of water flow for a runner of the same diameter compared to a Pelton wheel, achieving higher rotational speeds for direct coupling to generators.

## Use / Function

- **Primary use**: Generating medium-to-large-scale hydroelectric power in mountain streams and rivers with medium head and fluctuating water flow.
- **Secondary uses**: Direct mechanical power production for remote mechanical mills or water pumping stations.
- **Scale**: Small-to-medium installations (from low-kilowatt micro-hydro systems up to multi-megawatt regional power stations).

## Operating principle

**Angled Impulse Jet and Continuous Flow-Through**:

1. **High-Velocity Nozzle**: Water under high pressure from a penstock passes through one or more adjustable spear nozzles, converting hydraulic pressure into a high-speed free jet.
2. **Angled Side Impact**: The jet strikes the curved runner blades at an oblique angle (typically 20°), transferring impulse energy to the runner shaft.
3. **Unobstructed Exit**: Water flows across the single-curved bucket profile and exits on the opposite side without interfering with adjacent incoming water jets.
4. **Direct High-Speed Rotation**: Because the runner can handle larger water flow volumes relative to its size, it rotates faster than a comparable Pelton wheel, often eliminating the need for step-up speed gearboxes.

## How to create it

### 1. Runner and Bucket Fabrication

1. Cast or machine individual runner buckets from corrosion-resistant **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** using a asymmetrical curved profile with a single entry edge and an angled exit rim.
2. Fabricate a central runner hub out of forged carbon **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**.
3. Weld or bolt the buckets securely around the perimeter of the hub at the design angle.

### 2. Nozzle and Needle Mechanism

1. Machine a tapered nozzle housing out of **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or steel.
2. Craft an internal needle valve (spear) mounted on a threaded control rod to regulate nozzle aperture and water velocity.

### 3. Casing and Shaft Assembly

1. Mount the runner on a heavy-duty **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** shaft supported by sealed bearings.
2. Enclose the runner in a steel or cast-iron splash housing with a bottom discharge chute to guide spent water back into the tailrace.

## Materials needed

- **Runner Buckets & Hub**: **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)**, stainless steel, or high-strength **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**.
- **Housing & Nozzles**: **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or structural steel plate.
- **Shaft & Bearings**: Forged steel shafting and heavy-duty roller bearings.
- **Working Fluid**: High-velocity **[Water](/en/materials/water)** delivered under medium head pressure.

## Variants and improvements

- **Multi-Nozzle Turgo**: Utilizes two to four nozzles positioned around the runner perimeter to increase power output and flow capacity.
- **Horizontal vs. Vertical Axis**: Horizontal layout for simpler installation and maintenance; vertical shaft layout for multi-nozzle high-capacity plants.
- **Micro-Hydro Turgo**: Scaled-down simplified units with cast polyurethane or aluminum buckets for off-grid rural electrification.

## Limits and risks

- **Abrasion from Silt**: High-velocity water jets carrying sand or silt cause accelerated erosion on the nozzle tip and bucket surfaces.
- **Jet Alignment Criticality**: Minor misalignments in the jet entrance angle significantly drop mechanical efficiency.
- **Impulse Free-Discharge**: Requires a atmospheric air cavity in the housing; cannot operate fully submerged under tailwater.
