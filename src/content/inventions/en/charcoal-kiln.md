# Brief description

A charcoal kiln is a specialized enclosure designed for pyrolyzing wood under controlled, low-oxygen conditions. It transforms raw wood into carbonized charcoal, an essential high-density fuel required for high-temperature metallurgy and iron smelting.

## Use / Function

- **High-Temperature Fuel Production**: Converts **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** into **[Charcoal](/en/materials/charcoal)**, which burns hotter (exceeding 1100 °C) and cleaner than raw timber.
- **Metallurgical Reducing Agent**: Produces elemental carbon required for reducing iron ore in blast furnaces and bloomeries.
- **Smokeless Fuel**: Creates fuel that generates minimal smoke, ideal for blacksmith forging and indoor cooking.

## Operating principle

The charcoal kiln relies on **pyrolysis** (destructive distillation):
1. **Dehydration**: Heat from initial combustion evaporates remaining moisture from stacked wood (up to 200 °C).
2. **Thermal Breakdown**: At temperatures between 280 °C and 500 °C, complex wood molecules (cellulose, hemicellulose, lignin) decompose in the absence of sufficient oxygen.
3. **Volatilization & Carbonization**: Volatile organic compounds (tars, methanol, water vapor, wood gas) are driven off through vent holes, leaving behind a carbon-dense matrix (charcoal).

## How to create it

### Minimum functional version

1. **Prepare Ground Base**: Clear a flat, circular bed on dry ground, sloping slightly toward the center for drainage.
2. **Erect Central Flue**: Set up three or four wooden poles tied together vertically at the center to serve as a central chimney shaft.
3. **Stack Wood Tight**: Pack dense billets of dry **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** tightly around the central flue in concentric vertical layers, tapering toward the top.
4. **Cover Shell**: Seal the stack with a layer of turf, straw, and thick damp **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)** or earth (10–15 cm), leaving small draft holes around the bottom rim and top flue.
5. **Ignite and Control Draft**: Drop burning embers down the central flue. Once lit, plug the top chimney and regulate air entry using bottom vent holes to maintain slow smoldering without flame.
6. **Cool and Harvest**: When white smoke turns thin and transparent blue, seal all vents completely with mud and allow to cool sealed for 24–48 hours before opening.

### Required technological level

Basic to Intermediate. Requires careful monitoring of air vents to prevent total combustion into ash.

## Materials needed

- **Wood Supply**: Dense hardwoods or softwoods cut into uniform logs (**[Wood](/en/materials/wood)**).
- **Sealing Layer**: Damp **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)**, mud, turf, or sod.
- **Kiln Masonry (Optional for permanent versions)**: Heat-resistant **[Stone](/en/materials/stone)** or **[Brick](/en/materials/brick)**.

## Variants and improvements

- **Earth Mound / Earth Pit (Meiler)**: Primitive temporary mound covered in earth and turf; zero masonry required.
- **Brick Hive Kiln**: Permanent beehive-shaped brick masonry kiln providing higher carbon yield and weather resistance.
- **Retort Kiln**: Metal vessel with external heating; captures byproduct gases (wood tar, pyroligneous acid) for chemical processing.

## Limits and risks

- **Asphyxiation & Carbon Monoxide**: Pyrolysis releases lethal concentrations of carbon monoxide ($\text{CO}$) and toxic volatile tars. Never operate in enclosed spaces.
- **Accidental Ash Combustion**: Excess air leakage will turn the entire charge into worthless ash instead of charcoal.
- **Spontaneous Ignition**: Hot uncooled charcoal absorbs oxygen rapidly upon exposure to air and can spontaneously catch fire.
