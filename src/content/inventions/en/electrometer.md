# Brief description

A quadrant electrometer is a high-precision electrostatic measuring instrument that determines electric potential differences and minute static charges through electrostatic deflection without drawing continuous electrical current. Designed and perfected by William Thomson (Lord Kelvin), it superseded crude gold-leaf electroscopes by providing quantified, calibrated potential measurements vital for submarine telegraphy, atmospheric electricity studies, and early radioactivity research.

## Use / Function

- **Voltage Measurement without Load:** Measuring high or small potential differences in circuits where any current draw would collapse the voltage source (infinite input impedance).
- **Submarine Telegraphy:** Serving as a frictionless, high-sensitivity receiving detector for weak electrical pulses traveling through transatlantic telegraph cables.
- **Atmospheric Electricity & Radioactivity:** Quantifying atmospheric charge gradients and measuring the ionization rate of gases exposed to radioactive minerals (as used by Marie Curie).

## Operating principle

1. **Quadrant Geometry:** Four hollow brass cylindrical quadrants are arranged in a horizontal plane to form a shallow closed cylindrical box with a cross-shaped internal gap, insulated from each other.
2. **Opposite Cross-Connection:** Diagonally opposite quadrants are connected together in pairs, with the unknown voltage source applied across the two pairs.
3. **Suspended Needle:** An ultra-light horizontal aluminum or gilded mica needle shaped like a figure-eight or paddle is suspended horizontally inside the quadrants by a fine conducting wire or quartz fiber.
4. **Electrostatic Deflection:** The needle is held at a high constant potential. When a voltage difference exists between the quadrant pairs, electrostatic attraction to one pair and repulsion from the other exerts a mechanical torque proportional to the potential difference ($F \propto (V_1 - V_2)$), deflecting the needle.
5. **Optical Mirror Readout:** A small silvered mirror mounted on the suspension stem reflects a light spot onto a distant curved scale, magnifying minute deflections with zero friction.

## How to create it

1. **Form Quadrants:** Cast or cut four quadrants from a flat brass plate, hollowing them out to create a shallow box with upper and lower plates separated by a 5 mm internal gap.
2. **Mount on Insulating Pillars:** Secure the four quadrants on polished amber, ebonite, or paraffin-coated glass insulating pillars within a protective grounded metal chassis.
3. **Cross-Wire Quadrants:** Connect diagonally opposite quadrants using insulated copper wire, bringing their terminals out through insulated binding posts.
4. **Fabricate Light Needle:** Cut a symmetrical butterfly-wing needle from lightweight aluminum sheet, gilded mica, or beaten silver foil, and mount it to a rigid vertical stem.
5. **Install Suspension and Mirror:** Hang the stem from a delicate silvered quartz thread or fine phosphor bronze wire. Affix a concave lightweight mirror to the stem just below the top suspension head.
6. **Incorporate Leyden Jar / Acid Bath:** At the base, attach a platinum contact dipping into a small lead-glass cup of concentrated sulfuric acid to maintain the needle charge and act as both an internal desiccator and fluid oscillation damper.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** plate and hardware, high-insulation **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** pillars and jar, fine **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)**, **[Silver](/en/materials/silver)** mirror fragment, concentrated **[Sulfuric Acid](/en/materials/sulfuric-acid)** for desiccation and damping.
- **Tools:** Metal shears, jeweler's screwdrivers, soldering iron, scale lamp and graduated viewing scale.
- **Substitutes:** Aluminum sheet or gilded paper can substitute for the needle; clean mineral oil can serve as mechanical damping fluid if sulfuric acid is unavailable.

## Variants and improvements

- **Kelvin Quadrant Electrometer:** The canonical high-precision instrument fitted with a replenisher electrostatic generator and internal Leyden jar to keep the needle at high constant charge.
- **Bohnenberger Electrometer:** An early precursor using a single gold leaf suspended between the poles of a dry pile.
- **String Electrometer (Lutz-Edelmann):** Replaces quadrants and needle with a single conductive platinum-coated quartz fiber stretched between two knife-edge electrodes, yielding high frequency response.

## Limits and risks

- **Humidity Sensitivity:** Atmospheric moisture forms conductive film across insulating pillars; internal desiccant (sulfuric acid) must remain active or leakage will destroy readings.
- **Vibration Sensitivity:** High torsional compliance makes the suspended needle prone to room vibrations and tremors, requiring solid stone pedestal mounting.
- **Hazard of Corrosive Acid:** The internal reservoir of concentrated sulfuric acid creates severe chemical burn and splash hazards if the apparatus is tilted or jarred violently.
