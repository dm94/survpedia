# Brief description

A Crookes radiometer (also known as a light mill) is a glass bulb containing a partial vacuum and a rotor with lightweight metallic vanes that rotate when exposed to radiant light energy.

## Use / Function

- **Measuring Radiation:** Demonstrates and measures light intensity and electromagnetic radiation intensity.
- **Physics Demonstration:** Serves as an educational tool to illustrate thermal radiation, gas kinetics, and radiation pressure.
- **Sensors:** Inspires early designs for light-intensity measuring equipment and solar radiation detection.
- **Scale:** Small tabletop device.

## Operating principle

The radiometer operates due to differential heating of the vane surfaces in a low-pressure gas environment.

- **Vane Design:** Four vanes mounted on a central spindle have one side painted black (absorbing light) and one side polished or painted white (reflecting light).
- **Thermal Difference:** When exposed to light, the dark sides absorb radiant heat and become warmer than the shiny sides.
- **Gas Molecule Kinetics:** Gas molecules inside the partial vacuum strike the warmer dark sides and bounce off with higher kinetic energy, producing a net thermal transpiration/rebound force that pushes the dark sides away and spins the rotor.

## How to create it

1. **Vanes:** Cut four small, lightweight square leaves of thin **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)** (such as aluminum or mica with metal coating).
2. **Coating:** Coat one surface of each vane with dark **[Paint](/en/materials/paint)** or soot, leaving the opposite side shiny and reflective.
3. **Spindle Assembly:** Mount the four vanes symmetrically around a central needle pivot made of **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)** to minimize rotational friction.
4. **Enclosure:** Enclose the rotor assembly inside a airtight bulb made of **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)**.
5. **Evacuation:** Evacuate air from the glass bulb using a vacuum pump until reaching a low partial pressure (roughly $10^{-2}$ torr).
6. **Sealing:** Flame-seal the glass vacuum tube at the base to permanently maintain the partial vacuum.

## Materials needed

- **Enclosure:** High-clarity **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** bulb.
- **Rotor Vanes:** Thin sheet **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)** or lightweight metallic foil.
- **Absorptive Coating:** Matte black **[Paint](/en/materials/paint)** or soot.
- **Pivot:** Hardened metal needle bearing.

## Variants and improvements

- **Nichols Radiometer:** Measures true radiation pressure in a high vacuum using torsion balances rather than gas kinetic effects.
- **Solar Insolation Sensors:** Modern pyranometers and radiometers built for weather and solar energy stations.

## Limits and risks

- **Vacuum Sensitivity:** If the vacuum is too high or too low, the rotation slows down or stops completely.
- **Fragility:** Thin glass enclosure can shatter if knocked or subjected to thermal shock.
