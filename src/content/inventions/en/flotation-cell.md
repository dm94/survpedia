# Brief description

An advanced mineral processing vessel that separates target metallic ore minerals from worthless crushed gangue rock. By bubbling compressed air through a stirred chemical pulp slurry, hydrophobic target mineral particles cling to rising air bubbles, forming a concentrated froth overflow while waste material sinks.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Concentration of low-grade sulfide ores (copper, lead, zinc, nickel, molybdenum) and iron ore.
- **Secondary Uses:** Recycling paper fibers, separating microplastics, and purifying fine industrial coal dust.
- **Scale:** Industrial mineral processing plants and metallurgical refining facilities.

## Operating principle

1. **Slurry Preparation:** Crushed, finely ground mineral ore is mixed with water to form a fluid pulp mixture inside an open tank.
2. **Collector Chemical Addition:** Surfactant collector chemicals (such as xanthates or pine/vegetable oil derivatives) are added, coating target mineral grains to render their surface hydrophobic (water-repellent).
3. **Air Dispersion and Agitation:** A high-speed central impeller thoroughly agitates the pulp while injecting fine micro-bubbles of compressed air throughout the tank.
4. **Bubble Attachment:** Hydrophobic target mineral particles attach selectively to rising air micro-bubbles, while hydrophilic gangue rock remains suspended or sinks to the bottom.
5. **Froth Skimming:** A stable mineralized foam accumulates on the fluid surface and is continuously scraped off by rotating paddles into discharge launders for smelting.

## How to create it

1. **Build Tank Shell:** Fabricate a deep rectangular or cylindrical vessel from welded steel plate or wood lined with waterproof sealant.
2. **Mount Central Shaft and Rotor:** Install a vertical drive shaft fitted with a bladed impeller at the bottom center of the tank.
3. **Install Air Induction Tube:** Run a hollow central shroud or pressurized air line surrounding the impeller shaft to supply steady air flow to the agitation zone.
4. **Fabricate Overflow Launder and Skimmers:** Attach side collection troughs and motor-driven rotating rubber paddle wheels along the top edges to scrape off accumulated foam.
5. **Connect Slurry Feed and Underflow Discharge:** Position an inlet feed box on one side and an adjustable height tailing discharge weir at the opposite bottom corner.

## Materials needed

- **Cell Vessel:** Welded **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** sheet or treated **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** tank.
- **Impeller Drive:** **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** shaft and abrasion-resistant cast rotor.
- **Process Fluids:** **[Water](/en/materials/water)** for slurry suspension and **[Vegetable Oil](/en/materials/vegetable-oil)** or organic collectors/frothers.

## Variants and improvements

- **Sub-Aeration Flotation Cell:** Mechanically driven submerged rotor pulls atmospheric air down a hollow standpipe without needing external air compressors.
- **Column Flotation Cell:** Tall vertical pipe column using spargers at the bottom to produce micro-bubbles with counter-current wash water for high purity concentrates.
- **Pneumatic Flotation Cell:** Aeration occurs outside the main tank in high-shear mixers prior to rapid froth discharge.

## Limits and risks

- **Chemical Toxicity:** Collector and frother chemicals can pose environmental contamination risks if wastewater tailings are not contained.
- **Fine Particle Sensitivity:** Requires very fine ore grinding (typically under 100 microns); overly coarse grains fail to float.
- **Mechanical Abrasion:** Slurry rapidly wears down impellers, stator vanes, and pump liners, requiring frequent maintenance.
