# Brief description

A Van de Graaff generator is an electrostatic machine that uses a moving flexible belt to continuously transport electrical charges to a hollow insulated metal sphere, accumulating exceptionally high electrical potentials (up to millions of volts) at low continuous currents.

## Use / Function

- **High-Voltage Physics:** Accelerating charged particles in early nuclear physics experiments.
- **X-Ray & Sterilization:** Powering high-voltage X-ray tubes and industrial radiation beams.
- **Insulation Testing:** Testing dielectrics and high-voltage electrical breakdown limits.
- **Electrostatic Demonstrations:** Illustrating charge accumulation, electric fields, and corona discharge.

## Operating principle

1. **Triboelectric Charging / Induction:** Friction or an external DC bias between a roller (e.g., nylon or Teflon) and a continuous moving belt (rubber or silk) transfers negative or positive charges onto the belt.
2. **Mechanical Transport:** The insulated moving belt mechanically carries the charge upward against the repelling electric field of the high-voltage sphere.
3. **Charge Collection:** Inside the hollow metal sphere, a sharp comb of metal points positioned near the belt collects the charges via corona discharge.
4. **Faraday Cage Distribution:** Because charges repel each other, all collected charge immediately migrates to the outer surface of the hollow conductor, leaving the interior charge-free and ready to receive more incoming charge.

## How to create it

1. **Terminal Sphere:** Construct a hollow, smooth metal sphere or hemisphere (such as two joined stainless steel or aluminum bowls) with no sharp edges to prevent prematurely leaking charge into the air.
2. **Pulleys & Belt:** Mount a top pulley made of Teflon or nylon inside the sphere on an insulated plastic or glass column, and a bottom pulley made of aluminum or metal driven by a motor or hand crank. Loop a seamless rubber belt between them.
3. **Combs:** Position fine metallic combs (wire mesh or pointed copper pins) near the belt at both the bottom and top pulleys. Connect the bottom comb to ground and the top comb to the inside of the metal sphere.
4. **Insulating Column:** Use a dry, clean glass, acrylic, or plastic pipe to isolate the top sphere from the grounded base.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Smooth hollow metal sphere (**[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** or aluminum), **[Natural Rubber](/en/materials/natural-rubber)** belt, plastic/acrylic insulating tube.
- **Combs & Conductors:** Fine **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** or **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** wire mesh and **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** connections.
- **Base & Drive:** **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or metal base with a manual crank or small motor.
- **Substitutes:** Silk or synthetic plastic ribbon instead of rubber; hollow brass or tin cans for the top terminal.

## Variants and improvements

- **Tandem Generators:** Using two stages to double the acceleration energy of charged ions.
- **Pressurized Gas Tanks:** Enclosing the generator in sulfur hexafluoride (SF6) or pressurized nitrogen gas to reduce spark leakage and achieve much higher voltages in compact volumes.

## Limits and risks

- **Low Current:** Produces high voltage but minimal current (microamperes), so continuous power delivery is low.
- **Humidity Sensitivity:** Moisture on the insulating tube causes charge leakage and prevents high voltage buildup.
- **High-Voltage Discharge:** Large units store enough energy in the sphere's capacitance to cause painful shocks or damage sensitive electrical components.
