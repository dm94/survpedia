# Brief description

A retort is a classic airtight vessel with a long, downward-pointing neck used for the dry distillation (pyrolysis) and thermal decomposition of solid materials, as well as the distillation of high-boiling chemical substances. It is one of the foundational tools of alchemy and chemical technology.

## Use / Function

- **Dry Distillation & Pyrolysis:** Heats organic materials (such as wood or coal) in the absence of oxygen to produce charcoal, wood tar, pyroligneous acid, and coal gas.
- **Acid Synthesis:** Enables the distillation and purification of strong mineral acids (nitric acid, sulfuric acid, and hydrochloric acid) from salt and vitriol mixtures.
- **Metal Recovery:** Used in metallurgy to distil volatile metals like mercury or zinc from crude ores.
- **Scale:** Small glass laboratory retorts to large iron or earthenware industrial retorts.

## Operating principle

1. **Thermal Charging:** The solid or liquid reactant mixture is loaded into the main bulb of the retort.
2. **Airtight Heating:** The vessel is sealed and intense heat is applied directly to the bulb base.
3. **Vapor Generation:** Thermal decomposition or vaporization releases volatile gaseous components inside the sealed chamber.
4. **Condensation along Neck:** Vapors travel upward into the extended, angled neck, where air cooling causes them to condense into liquid along the interior walls.
5. **Collection:** Liquid condensate flows down the angled neck under gravity into a receiving flask attached at the end.

## How to create it

1. **Shape Refractory Vessel:** Mold a spherical bulb vessel with a long, smoothly curving downward stem using heat-resistant borosilicate glass, glazed earthenware, or hammered copper.
2. **Form Charging Neck:** Ensure the top opening of the bulb can be tightly fitted with a ground glass stopper or clay plug.
3. **Assemble Heat Shielding:** Place the retort bulb over an adjustable hearth, brazier, or **[Bunsen Burner](/en/inventions/bunsen-burner)** with an iron tripod support.
4. **Attach Condenser Extension:** Connect an extended cooling tube or wet cloth jacket over the long neck to enhance vapor condensation.
5. **Connect Receiver Vessel:** Secure a collection receiving flask to the end of the retort neck with a sealed joint or vent.
6. **Charge and Heat:** Place wood chips or chemical reactants into the bulb, seal the stopper, apply heat, and collect the distilled liquid distillate.

## Materials needed

- **Vessel Body:** Borosilicate glass, fireclay, or copper sheet metal. Sourced from **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)**, **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)**, and **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)**.
- **Seals & Stopper:** Ground glass stopper, clay lute paste, or heat-resistant sealant.
- **Heat Source:** Furnace, hearth, or lamp burner.

## Variants and improvements

- **Glass Laboratory Retort:** One-piece blown glass vessel used for small-scale chemical reactions and mineral acid distillation.
- **Iron Industrial Retort:** Massive cast iron cylinder or box used in gasworks for coal gasification and wood charcoal manufacture.
- **Retort Stand & Clamp:** Metal support frame allowing precise positioning over heating flames.

## Limits and risks

- **Thermal Stress & Cracking:** Direct flame contact on glass or ceramic retorts can cause sudden thermal cracking and spill dangerous boiling contents.
- **Pressure Accumulation:** Blocking or clogging of the long neck during pyrolysis can cause pressure build-up and catastrophic vessel rupture.
- **Toxic Vapors:** Thermal decomposition produces toxic gases (carbon monoxide, sulfur dioxide, nitrous fumes) requiring fume extraction.
