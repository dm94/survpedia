# Brief description

A dynamo is an electrical generator that produces direct current (DC) using electromagnetism. Featuring a rotating armature of conductive coils spinning within a magnetic field and a mechanical commutator, the dynamo was the first electrical generator capable of delivering continuous industrial power to early electrical grids.

## Use / Function

- **Direct Current Supply:** Providing steady DC electricity for industrial machinery, arc lighting, electroplating, and early urban electrical power networks.
- **Self-Excited Generation:** Generating its own magnetic field current without needing external batteries.
- **Scale:** Small hand-cranked generators to massive power plant dynamos driven by steam engines or hydro turbines.

## Operating principle

1. **Rotational Induction:** Mechanical rotation spins an armature containing multiple coils of copper wire within a magnetic field provided by field electromagnets.
2. **Electromotive Force:** As the coils rotate through the magnetic lines of force, an alternating electromotive force (AC) is induced inside each coil segment according to Faraday's Law.
3. **Mechanical Rectification:** The rotating commutator (a split ring of conductive segments insulated with mica) mechanically reverses the electrical connections to the stationary carbon brushes twice per revolution.
4. **Unidirectional Current:** By synchronizing the current reversal with the coil rotation, the alternating current generated internally is converted into a continuous, unidirectional direct current (DC) output.

## How to create it

1. **Construct Field Stator:** Mount two curved soft iron pole shoes opposite each other on a heavy **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** frame. Wind field coils of insulated **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** around the iron poles.
2. **Build Armature Core:** Stack laminated soft iron discs onto a central steel shaft to form a cylindrical armature core while suppressing eddy current loss.
3. **Wind Armature Coils:** Wrap multiple insulated copper wire coils evenly around slots along the circumference of the armature core.
4. **Assemble Commutator:** Fix a segmented copper ring insulated with mica onto the rotating shaft, connecting each segment to specific armature coil ends.
5. **Install Brushes & Bearings:** Mount carbon or copper leaf brushes on spring-loaded holders so they press firmly against opposing sides of the commutator ring. Mount the shaft on lubricated bearings within a rigid **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or iron support structure.
6. **Self-Excitation Wiring:** Connect the field coils in series or shunt (parallel) configuration with the output brushes so residual magnetism in the stator starts current buildup upon rotation.

## Materials needed

- **Stator & Stator Coils:** Iron poles wrapped with copper magnet wire. Sourced from **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)**, **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)**, and **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)**.
- **Armature & Commutator:** Laminated iron discs, copper wire, copper commutator bars, and carbon brushes. Sourced from **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** and **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)**.
- **Frame & Mounting:** Rigid structural iron or wooden base. Sourced from **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** and **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)**.

## Variants and improvements

- **Permanent Magnet Dynamo (Magneto):** Uses permanent magnets for field excitation; simple and reliable, but output voltage is limited.
- **Shunt Dynamo:** Field windings connected in parallel with the load; maintains stable output voltage under varying electrical loads.
- **Series Dynamo:** Field windings connected in series with the load; voltage increases with output current, suitable for constant-current arc lighting circuits.
- **Compound Dynamo:** Combines both shunt and series field windings to automatically compensate for voltage drops under heavy load conditions.

## Limits and risks

- **Commutator Sparking:** Friction and inductance between brushes and commutator segments cause electrical sparking, brush wear, and radio frequency noise.
- **Speed Dependency:** Output voltage is directly proportional to rotational speed; requires a constant mechanical governor on the driving engine.
- **Thermal Breakdown:** Overloading or short circuits generate extreme resistive heat that can melt insulation and burn out armature windings.
