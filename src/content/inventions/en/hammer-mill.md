# Brief description

A hammer mill is a high-speed mechanical mill that uses revolving pivoted or fixed steel hammers attached to a central rotor to pulverize, shred, or crush materials by repeated impact against internal breaker plates and sizing screens. Essential for agricultural processing, mineral beneficiation, and waste recycling, it efficiently grinds grain, limestone, biomass, and ores into uniform particle sizes.

## Use / Function

- **Agricultural Milling:** Grinding cereal grains (corn, wheat, barley) into flour or livestock feed.
- **Biomass & Biofuel Processing:** Shredding wood chips, agricultural residue, and plant waste for combustion, composting, or gasification.
- **Mineral & Material Reduction:** Crushing limestone, gypsum, clay, and soft ores for mortar, cement, and chemical synthesis.
- **Waste Recycling:** Shredding paper, glass cullet, scrap metal, and organic waste.

## Operating principle

1. **High-Speed Rotor Rotation:** A central horizontal shaft fitted with rotor disks and free-swinging steel hammers rotates at high velocity driven by a motor, engine, or water turbine.
2. **Impact Shattering:** Raw material fed into the top feed hopper enters the grinding chamber and is violently struck in mid-air by the fast-moving hammers.
3. **Breaker Plate Impact:** Shattered fragments are driven against hardened steel liner plates surrounding the chamber, causing further fragmentation.
4. **Perforated Screen Sizing:** Materials are repeatedly swept over a curved perforated screen at the bottom; particles reduced below the screen hole size discharge freely while larger pieces remain in the grinding zone until fully pulverized.

## How to create it

1. **Construct Rotor Assembly:** Mount a series of heavy circular steel disks onto a central steel shaft, installing hardened steel pins across the outer perimeter of the disks.
2. **Mount Swinging Hammers:** Pivot rectangular hardened steel hammer bars onto the perimeter pins, allowing them to swing freely under centrifugal force.
3. **Build Chamber and Breaker Plates:** Fabricate a heavy steel or cast iron cylindrical housing with internal serrated breaker plates mounted opposite the direction of rotation.
4. **Install Curved Screen Bar:** Mount a curved perforated steel plate or mesh screen under the lower portion of the rotor chamber with interchangeable hole sizes.
5. **Mount Shaft Bearings & Drive:** Support the rotor shaft on heavy-duty pillow block bearings, attaching a pulley or coupling connected to an engine, electric motor, or power take-off.

## Materials needed

- **Rotor & Hammers:** Forged or heat-treated **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** for high impact resistance.
- **Chamber Housing:** Heavy **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or welded structural **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** plate.
- **Frame & Support:** Heavy structural **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** or **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** timber frame.
- **Screen:** Perforated **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** sheet or heavy wire mesh.

## Variants and improvements

- **Reversible Hammer Mill:** Features a reversible motor/drive allowing the rotor to spin in either direction to double hammer edge life before replacement.
- **Pneumatic / Air-Swept Hammer Mill:** Uses an integrated suction fan to draw fine particles rapidly through the screen, preventing clogging and thermal buildup.
- **Fixed-Hammer Mill:** Uses rigid, non-swinging hammers or cutter blades tailored for fibrous organic materials and wood waste.

## Limits and risks

- **High Power Requirement:** Rapid impact crushing requires substantial continuous rotational energy and torque.
- **High Wear Rate:** Hammers and screens experience abrasive wear when crushing hard materials and require frequent replacement or hardfacing.
- **Dust & Explosion Hazard:** Fine airborne organic or coal dust created during dry milling can cause dangerous dust explosions if ignited by sparks or friction.
