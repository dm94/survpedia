# Brief description

A chemical processing vessel in which fine solid particles (such as sand, catalyst beads, or biomass) are suspended in an upward-flowing stream of gas or liquid. When fluid velocity reaches the fluidization threshold, the solid bed behaves like a boiling liquid, enabling rapid heat transfer, uniform temperature distribution, and intimate contact between gases and solids.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** High-efficiency biomass gasification, catalytic chemical synthesis, ore roasting, and industrial drying.
- **Secondary Uses:** Low-emission combustion of low-grade fuels (such as waste or coal) and calcination of limestone.
- **Scale:** Pilot experimental gasifiers to massive power plant boilers and petroleum cracking units.

## Operating principle

1.  **Gas Distribution:** Compressed pressurized gas (air, steam, or oxygen) enters through a perforated distributor plate at the bottom of the reactor column.
2.  **Fluidization:** As gas velocity increases through the bed of solid particles (like silica sand), drag forces balance gravity, expanding the bed and causing particles to swirl like a boiling fluid.
3.  **Mixing & Reaction:** Fuel or chemical reactants injected into the bed mix rapidly with hot circulating particles, achieving near-instantaneous thermal equilibrium and fast chemical reaction rates.
4.  **Particle Separation:** Exhaust gas exiting the top carries fine dust into a cyclone separator, which returns trapped solids back to the bed.

## How to create it

1.  **Reactor Vessel:** Fabricate a cylindrical vertical steel column insulated internally with refractory firebricks to withstand high temperatures.
2.  **Distributor Plate:** Install a heat-resistant perforated steel plate or nozzle grid at the base to evenly distribute inlet gas across the column cross-section.
3.  **Bed Material:** Fill the lower vessel section with uniform silica sand or alumina particles of optimal grain size (typically 0.1 to 1 mm).
4.  **Plenum & Cyclone:** Attach a bottom plenum chamber for gas intake and a top cyclone separator to recover elutriated bed material from exit gases.

## Materials needed

- **Vessel & Piping:** Structural **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or stainless steel shell.
- **Thermal Lining:** **[Refractory Brick](/en/materials/refractory-brick)** or castable refractory mortar.
- **Bed Media:** Granular **[Sand](/en/materials/sand)** (silica), crushed limestone, or catalyst beads.
- **Gas Source:** Blower or **[Air Compressor](/en/inventions/air-compressor)** to generate fluidizing velocity.

## Variants and improvements

- **Bubbling Fluidized Bed (BFB):** Operating at lower gas velocities where distinct gas bubbles rise through a defined bed surface; ideal for gasification and drying.
- **Circulating Fluidized Bed (CFB):** High gas velocity sweeps solids continuously out the top; a high-efficiency cyclone separates solids and recycles them back to the bottom.
- **Pressurized Fluidized Bed (PFBC):** Operates under high pressure to drive gas turbines directly for combined-cycle power generation.

## Limits and risks

- **Erosion:** High-velocity circulating abrasive particles cause severe wear on internal walls, boiler tubes, and cyclone separators.
- **Agglomeration & Defluidization:** High bed temperatures can melt ash or chemicals, causing particles to stick together into large clinkers that collapse fluidization.
- **Elutriation Loss:** Fine particles get entrained in exit gas and require robust dust filtration (cyclones or baghouses).
