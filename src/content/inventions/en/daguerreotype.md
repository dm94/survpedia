# Brief description

The daguerreotype was the first publicly announced photographic process and the first to achieve widespread commercial use. It captures a direct-positive image on a silver-plated copper sheet without using a negative, producing a sharp, highly detailed, and unique mirror-like photograph.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Permanent image recording and visual portraiture.
- **Secondary Uses:** Scientific documentation, astronomical photography, topographical surveying, and historical archiving.
- **Scale:** Individual studio portraits and small portable field photographic plates.

## Operating principle

1. **Photosensitization:** A highly polished silver-plated copper plate is exposed to halogen vapors (such as iodine or bromine in an airtight box), creating a thin light-sensitive surface layer of silver halide (silver iodide/bromide).
2. **Exposure:** The sensitized plate is placed inside a **[Camera Obscura](/en/inventions/camera-obscura)** equipped with a **[Lens](/en/inventions/lens)** and exposed to light. Photons hit the silver halide, reducing minute amounts to elemental silver clusters and forming a latent (invisible) image.
3. **Development:** The exposed plate is placed over heated mercury (or exposed to red/yellow light in modified processes). Mercury vapor selectively amalgamates with the light-exposed silver grains, rendering the image visible as a whitish amalgam on the dark polished silver background.
4. **Fixing:** The plate is rinsed in a solution of sodium thiosulfate (hyposulfite of soda) or strong salt water to dissolve remaining unexposed silver halides, rendering the image permanent and light-insensitive.
5. **Protection:** The delicate surface is rinsed with distilled water, dried, and sealed behind **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** in a protective frame or case to prevent oxidation and physical abrasion.

## How to create it

1. **Plate Preparation:** Clad a thin layer of pure **[Silver](/en/materials/silver)** onto a sheet of **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)**. Polish the silver side with fine abrasives (pumice, rouge) until it achieves a flawless mirror finish.
2. **Sensitizing Box:** Construct an airtight wooden box containing iodine crystals at the bottom. Suspend the plate face-down over the crystals in darkness until the silver turns a golden-yellow hue.
3. **Camera Exposure:** Insert the plate into a light-tight holder, load it into a camera obscura, and expose the scene through a glass lens for several seconds to minutes (depending on available sunlight).
4. **Mercury Fuming Chamber:** Place the exposed plate inside an inclined wooden chamber over a dish of mercury heated by an **[Oil Lamp](/en/inventions/oil-lamp)** (~60°C / 140°F). Observe the latent image develop as mercury vapors interact with the plate.
5. **Fixing and Casing:** Immerse the developed plate in a sodium thiosulfate bath to fix the image, wash carefully with distilled water, dry over a flame, and seal under glass with a protective wooden or metal mat.

## Materials needed

- **Plate Materials:** **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** sheet plated with pure **[Silver](/en/materials/silver)**.
- **Sensitizing Chemicals:** Elemental iodine (or bromine/chlorine accelerators).
- **Developing Agent:** Metallic mercury (for vapor development) or hypo fixing salt (sodium thiosulfate).
- **Optical Tools:** **[Camera Obscura](/en/inventions/camera-obscura)** and high-quality optical **[Lens](/en/inventions/lens)**.
- **Enclosure:** **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** for sensitizing and developing boxes, **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** cover plate for protection.

## Variants and improvements

- **Quickstuff Accelerators:** Adding bromine or chlorine fumes reduced exposure times from 15 minutes down to several seconds, making studio portraiture practical.
- **Gold Toning (Gilding):** Treating the fixed plate with gold chloride and heat improved image contrast and durability against physical damage.
- **Calotype & Collodion Processes:** Later photographic methods replaced daguerreotypes by introducing reusable negatives, allowing unlimited copies from a single exposure.

## Limits and risks

- **Toxicity:** Mercury vapors are extremely toxic to the nervous system and organs; work must be conducted with strict ventilation or remote observation.
- **Single Unique Image:** Daguerreotypes produce direct positive images on metal plates that cannot be duplicated or printed as negatives.
- **Surface Fragility:** The image consists of microscopic particles resting on a mirror finish; touching the plate destroys the image instantly.
- **Mirror Reflection:** Viewing the image requires holding the plate at a specific angle relative to light sources.
