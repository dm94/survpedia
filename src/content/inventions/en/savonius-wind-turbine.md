# Brief description

A Savonius Wind Turbine is a drag-type vertical-axis wind turbine (VAWT) invented by Finnish engineer Sigurd Johannes Savonius in 1922. Featuring two or three scoop-shaped semi-cylindrical blades mounted on a vertical shaft, it rotates under aerodynamic drag regardless of wind direction. Because of its extreme mechanical simplicity, self-starting capability under low wind speeds, and high torque at low rotational speeds, the Savonius turbine is an essential low-tech device for mechanical pumping, ventilation, and decentralized off-grid power generation.

## Use / Function

- **Mechanical Water Pumping:** Drives positive displacement pumps or deep-well piston pumps directly without complex speed-increasing gearboxes.
- **Low-Wind Power Generation:** Rotates low-speed permanent magnet alternators or dynamos in environments with gentle or turbulent wind currents.
- **Aeration and Agitation:** Powers mechanical agitators for composting, wastewater aeration, or agricultural grain mixing.
- **Ventilation:** Rotates rooftop exhaust extractors to draw hot air and fumes out of agricultural structures and workshops.
- **Scale:** Small domestic or farm-scale installations (50W–1000W mechanical or electrical equivalent).

## Operating principle

The Savonius rotor operates primarily on differential aerodynamic drag combined with cross-flow impulse:

1. **Differential Drag Force:** When wind blows across the vertical axis, it hits both the concave inner side and convex outer side of the scoop blades. The concave scoop experiences high aerodynamic drag ($C_d \approx 1.2$), while the convex scoop presents a smooth profile with significantly lower drag ($C_d \approx 0.4$).
2. **Torque Generation:** The net difference in drag forces creates a strong rotational torque around the central vertical axis, causing the turbine to self-start even in light breezes.
3. **Cross-Flow Recirculation:** Air striking the concave scoop flows around its inner curve and is redirected across the central gap into the opposing scoop, providing an auxiliary impulse boost that enhances efficiency.
4. **Omnidirectional Operation:** Because the scoops are arranged symmetrically around a vertical axis, the turbine operates efficiently regardless of wind direction without needing a tail-vane or yaw mechanism.

## How to create it

### Vertical-Axis Savonius Rotor Construction

1. **Fabricate the Scoop Blades:** Cut a cylindrical metal drum or heavy PVC pipe longitudinally into two halves, or curve sheet metal into two semi-cylindrical scoops.
2. **Prepare the Shaft and Hub Plates:** Cut two circular end plates from thick **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** plate. Drill a central hole for the vertical shaft.
3. **Mount the Scoops with Overlap:** Secure the scoops between the top and bottom end plates in an offset "S" shape. Position them so that the inner edges overlap by approximately 15% to 20% of the scoop diameter, leaving a central air gap for cross-flow recirculation.
4. **Construct Support Frame and Bearings:** Mount the vertical shaft on top and bottom low-friction bearings anchored to a sturdy **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** frame. Use a thrust bearing at the base to support the total weight of the rotor.
5. **Connect Power Take-Off:** Attach a pulley, sprocket, or direct coupling to the bottom shaft to drive a mechanical pump or low-speed **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** permanent-magnet generator.

## Materials needed

- **Scoops & End Plates:** Sheet metal, halved metal drums, or thick outdoor **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** plywood.
- **Vertical Shaft:** Solid **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** rod or structural pipe.
- **Frame & Bearings:** Structural **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** or wooden support frame, ball bearings, or brass sleeve bearings.
- **Hardware:** Bolts, lock nuts, bracket clamps, and drive belts/pulleys.

## Variants and improvements

- **Helical Savonius Turbine:** Twisted scoop blades (typically 90° twist) that eliminate torque fluctuation during rotation, providing smooth continuous torque and reduced vibration.
- **Multi-Stage Savonius:** Two or more Savonius rotors stacked vertically on the same shaft, offset by 90 degrees to eliminate dead spots in rotation.
- **Deflector Shield Augmentation:** Wind deflectors positioned upstream to shield the returning convex blade from counter-drag, significantly boosting power coefficient ($C_p$).

## Limits and risks

- **Lower Aerodynamic Efficiency:** Maximum power coefficient ($C_p \approx 0.15 - 0.20$) is significantly lower than lift-based horizontal axis wind turbines ($C_p \approx 0.45$).
- **High Material Mass per Output Unit:** Requires more physical material per watt generated compared to slender high-speed blades.
- **Dynamic Structural Stress in High Winds:** Large surface area experiences significant lateral aerodynamic forces during severe storms, requiring robust guy wires and frame anchoring.
