# Brief description

A quadrant is an astronomical and navigational measuring instrument shaped as a quarter-circle arc (90 degrees). It is used to measure the altitude angle of celestial bodies or terrestrial objects above the horizon.

## Use / Function

- **Celestial Navigation**: Measuring the altitude of the Sun or Polaris to calculate geographical latitude.
- **Timekeeping**: Measuring solar altitude to determine local solar time (in horary quadrants).
- **Surveying & Heights**: Calculating heights of buildings, trees, or mountains using elevation angles and basic trigonometry.
- **Scale**: Portable navigational hand-held tool or large fixed observatory equipment.

## Operating principle

The quadrant exploits gravity and circle geometry. A quarter-circle frame is calibrated with angular degree markings from 0° to 90° along its outer curved rim. Sighting vanes or pinholes along one straight edge align the line of sight with a target star or landmark. A weighted plumb line hangs freely from the center pivot point (apex). Gravity pulls the plumb line straight down, and where the string intersects the graduated arc indicates the altitude angle ($\theta$) relative to horizontal:

$$\text{Altitude Angle} = 90^\circ - \text{Plumb Line Angle}$$

## How to create it

1. **Craft Arc Frame**: Cut or cast a flat quarter-circle plate (radius approx. 20–40 cm) out of dry hardwood, brass, or bronze plate.
2. **Mount Sights**: Attach two raised sighting vanes with tiny alignment holes (pínulas) along the top straight edge.
3. **Graduate Arc**: Carefully engrave 90 equal degree divisions along the curved edge using a dividing compass and protractor geometry.
4. **Attach Plumb Line**: Bore a small hole at the exact apex (center of curvature) and thread a fine silk or linen cord through it.
5. **Attach Lead Weight**: Tie a small lead bob to the loose end of the cord to ensure steady vertical gravitational pull.

## Materials needed

- **Essential materials**:
  - **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)**: Rigidity for the quarter-circle body.
  - **[Lead](/en/materials/lead)**: High-density metal for the hanging plumb weight.
  - **[String](/en/materials/string)** or **[Silk](/en/materials/silk)**: Fine thread for the plumb line.
- **Tools**:
  - **[Compass](/en/inventions/drawing-compass)**: For inscribing the precise circular arc.
  - Scribing tool or chisel: For engraving degree graduations.
- **Possible substitutes**:
  - Dense stone or heavy metal nut: In place of cast lead bob.

## Variants and improvements

- **Geometric Quadrant**: Designed specifically for land surveying, featuring a shadow square for direct height/distance calculations without trigonometric tables.
- **Horary Quadrant**: Marked with seasonal curves to tell local time directly from solar height.
- **Gunter's Quadrant**: Includes logarithmic scales and stereographic projections of stars to simplify celestial navigation math.
- **Mural Quadrant**: Large, permanently mounted observatory quadrant (several meters in radius) for high-precision astronomical observation.

## Limits and risks

- **Wind Interference**: Gusts of wind sway the plumb line, making accurate readings difficult on sea vessels or open terrain.
- **Ship Acceleration**: Vessel pitching and rolling introduces centrifugal forces that displace the plumb line from true vertical gravity.
- **Sun Sight Strain**: Sighting the Sun through pinholes requires casting a shadow onto the rear vane to avoid eye damage.
