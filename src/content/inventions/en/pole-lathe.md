# Brief description

A primitive, human-powered reciprocating lathe used for turning wooden objects such as bowls, spindles, tool handles, and machine components. Operating entirely without electricity or complex metal gears, it uses a flexible wooden spring pole suspended overhead and a foot treadle below to rotate the workpiece back and forth.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Shaping cylindrical wooden stock into tool handles, furniture legs, spindles, bowls, and wooden pulleys.
- **Secondary Uses:** Manufacturing wooden machine parts (spindles, rollers, gear blanks) for early mechanical equipment.
- **Scale:** Workshop or field-portable timber frame.

## Operating principle

1.  **Reciprocating Motion:** A drive cord is wrapped around the wooden workpiece, with its upper end tied to a flexible sapling (spring pole) and its lower end tied to a foot treadle.
2.  **Cutting Stroke:** Pressing down on the foot treadle pulls the cord downward, rotating the wood workpiece forward towards the woodturner's chisel.
3.  **Return Stroke:** Releasing foot pressure allows the spring pole to flex upward, pulling the cord back up and rotating the wood in reverse.
4.  **Intermittent Cutting:** Cutting occurs strictly during the forward stroke when the treadle is depressed.

## How to create it

1.  **Bed & Poppets:** Construct a heavy timber bench bed with two parallel beams and two moveable wooden uprights (poppets/tailstocks) held in place with wedges.
2.  **Centers:** Install iron or hard wood pointed center pins into the poppets to hold the wooden workpiece securely between them.
3.  **Spring Pole:** Mount a 10–12 foot flexible green sapling (such as ash, hazel, or larch) overhead or anchored securely behind the lathe bed.
4.  **Treadle & Cord:** Fabricate a hinged wooden foot treadle on the floor, and tie a strong hemp cord from the treadle, wrapped once around the workpiece, up to the end of the spring pole.

## Materials needed

- **Lathe Frame & Pole:** Durable **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** timber (oak/pine for frame; ash/hazel for spring pole).
- **Drive Line:** High-tensile **[Rope](/en/materials/rope)** or leather cord.
- **Cutting Tools:** Handheld **[Chisel](/en/inventions/chisel)** or gouge forged from carbon steel.

## Variants and improvements

- **Bow Lathe:** Hand-operated predecessor using a violin-like bow wrapped around the stock.
- **Treadle Lathe with Flywheel:** Continuous rotation lathe utilizing a crank drive and heavy wooden flywheel to maintain forward momentum.
- **Power Lathe:** Industrial motor-driven metal or woodworking lathe (**[Lathe](/en/inventions/lathe)**).

## Limits and risks

- **Intermittent Cutting:** Cutting only occurs on half the cycle (downstroke), making turning slower than continuous-rotary lathes.
- **Sharp Tool Hazard:** Chisels can catch on uneven wood grain or knots (a kickback event), throwing the tool or workpiece.
- **Pole Fatigue:** Green wood spring poles lose elasticity over time and require eventual replacement.
