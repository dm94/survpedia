# Brief description

A microtome is a precision mechanical instrument used to slice biological tissue, botanical specimens, or synthetic materials into extremely thin sections (typically 1 to 100 micrometers thick). These transparent slices are mounted on glass slides for detailed examination under an optical microscope.

## Use / Function

- **Histology & Pathology:** Preparing tissue cross-sections from biopsy samples to diagnose disease, tumors, and cellular anomalies.
- **Botanical Research:** Cutting delicate plant stem, root, and leaf structures to examine vascular bundles and cell walls.
- **Materials Science:** Sectioning polymers, fibers, and composite materials to analyze grain boundary structures under magnification.

## Operating principle

1. **Specimen Fixation & Embedding:** Samples are chemically fixed and embedded in paraffin wax, resin, or frozen ice matrix to provide mechanical rigidity.
2. **Precision Advance:** A fine leadscrew or micrometer mechanism advances the embedded sample block toward a fixed, razor-sharp blade in precise sub-millimeter or micron increments with each stroke.
3. **Shear Cutting:** As the specimen block passes across the razor edge, a continuous ribbon of uniform, paper-thin sections is shaved off without crushing or tearing cellular structures.

## How to create it

1. **Construct Heavy Base:** Cast or machine a heavy wooden or cast-iron base plate to minimize vibration during sectioning.
2. **Fabricate Feed Mechanism:** Assemble a fine threaded steel leadscrew (e.g., 0.5 mm pitch) connected to a calibrated ratchet wheel or micrometer dial for specimen advance.
3. **Mount Specimen Stage:** Attach a locking specimen clamp to the leadscrew assembly to hold paraffin-embedded tissue blocks securely.
4. **Mount Cutting Blade:** Install a rigid blade holder designed to securely clamp razor-sharp hardened high-carbon steel or glass blades at an adjustable angle (typically 5°–10° clearance angle).
5. **Calibrate Stroke:** Test slide motion ensuring rigid vertical stroke guidance so that each pass produces uniform, unwrinkled ribbons floating onto a warm water bath.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Hardened tool **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** blade, **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** leadscrew and ratchet gears, **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or metal base plate, **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** slides and coverslips.
- **Tools:** Lathe, thread cutting tap/die, fine sharpening hones (leather and Belgian yellow stone).
- **Substitutes:** Heavy-duty razor blades or broken glass knife edges (Latta-Hartmann knife) can substitute for specialty microtome steel blades.

## Variants and improvements

- **Hand / Table Microtome:** Simple screw-feed cylinder held in hand or clamped to a bench, suitable for coarse botanical sections (20–50 microns).
- **Rotary Microtome:** Features a hand crank operating a vertical slide fly-wheel mechanism, producing rapid, highly uniform ribbons of 1–10 micron sections.
- **Cryostat / Freezing Microtome:** Encloses the microtome in a refrigerated chamber to rapidly freeze unfixed tissue samples for immediate intraoperative surgical diagnosis.

## Limits and risks

- **Blade Dullness:** Even microscopic nicks in the blade cause scoring, tears, and compression artifacts in thin tissue sections.
- **Laceration Hazard:** The ultra-sharp, unshielded blade presents a severe cut and laceration risk during specimen setup and cleaning.
- **Paraffin Deformation:** Incorrect paraffin wax melting temperature or knife angle can cause specimen chatter, folding, or section compression.
