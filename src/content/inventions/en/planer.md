# Brief description

A metal planer (or planing machine) is a heavy industrial machine tool designed to cut long, true flat surfaces, keyways, and dovetail guide ways on large metal workpieces. Unlike a metal shaper where the cutting tool moves back and forth over a stationary piece, a planer reciprocates a heavy worktable bearing the workpiece beneath a stationary overhead tool bridge, enabling precision machining of massive engine beds, lathe beds, and machine frames.

## Use / Function

- **Primary Use:** Machining long, perfectly flat surfaces and linear precision ways on heavy castings and forged steel plates.
- **Secondary Uses:** Cutting multiple parallel slots, dovetail slides, and machine tool guide rails simultaneously using multiple tool heads.
- **Scale:** Heavy workshop and industrial factory scale.

## Operating principle

1. **Reciprocating Table Drive:** A long reciprocating worktable (platen) carrying the workpiece moves linearly back and forth under an elevated cross-rail gantry.
2. **Stationary Cutting Stroke:** Heavy tool posts mounted on the rigid bridge cross-rail remain stationary during the forward cutting stroke, removing metal along the entire length of the workpiece.
3. **Quick-Return Table Motion:** Bull-gear and rack-and-pinion or hydraulic drive mechanisms return the heavy table rapidly after each cutting stroke.
4. **Automatic Tool Feed & Relief:** At each end stroke reversal, a mechanical ratchet advances the tool head laterally across the cross-rail, while clapper boxes swing open on the return stroke to prevent tool dragging.

## How to create it

1. **Cast Bed & Precision Ways:** Pour heavy **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** bed channels fitted with V-shaped or flat parallel guide ways. Scrape and lap the ways flat using a surface plate.
2. **Fabricate the Platen (Table):** Cast a long heavy table with T-slots for clamping workpieces, machining matching V-ways on its underside to ride smoothly on the bed ways.
3. **Build the Upright Columns & Cross-Rail:** Erect two massive vertical cast-iron housings flanking the bed and bridge them with a heavy horizontal cross-rail that adjusts vertically.
4. **Install Drive Mechanism:** Fit a rack along the bottom of the platen driven by a large bull gear powered by a reversing belt shifter or electric motor drive.
5. **Mount Tool Heads & Clapper Assemblies:** Mount adjustable tool heads on the cross-rail featuring clapper boxes with hardened **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** cutting tools and mechanical feed screws.

## Materials needed

- **Bed, Platen & Columns:** Heavy **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** for vibration damping and structural rigidity.
- **Drive Gears, Rack & Lead Screws:** Tough alloy or carbon **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**.
- **Bearings & Shaft Bushings:** **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)** or **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)**.
- **Cutting Tools:** High-carbon tool steel or high-speed tool bits.

## Variants and improvements

- **Open-Side Planer:** Features a single column support instead of two uprights, accommodating oversized workpieces that overhang one side of the table.
- **Edge Planer (Plate Planer):** Designed specifically to trim and bevel the edges of long armor plates and boiler shells, where the plate remains fixed while the carriage moves.
- **Double-Housing Planer:** The standard twin-column bridge design offering maximum rigidity for heavy cuts.

## Limits and risks

- **Large Floor Space Requirements:** Requires a floor footprint more than twice the maximum stroke length to accommodate table travel.
- **High Inertia and Power:** Reversing heavy worktables places severe mechanical stress on gears and belts.
- **Safety Hazards:** Long reciprocating table movements create severe pinch hazards; flying chips require eye protection and safety barriers.
