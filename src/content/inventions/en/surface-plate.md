# Brief description

A surface plate is a solid, flat plate used as the main horizontal reference plane for precision inspection, layout, and tooling setup in machine shops and engineering manufacturing.

## Use / Function

- **Datum Plane:** Serves as an unyielding, ultra-flat baseline for measuring height, parallelism, and flatness of mechanical components.
- **Scraping Calibration:** Acts as the master reference when hand-scraping machine tool ways and bearing slides.
- **Scale:** Toolroom and workshop scale, ranging from small benchtop plates to large floor inspection tables.

## Operating principle

1. **Flat Reference Standard:** Provides a rigid surface with microscopic flatness (typically accurate to within a few micrometers across the entire span).
2. **Transfer Measurement:** A height gauge, dial indicator, or scribing block rests on the surface plate alongside the workpiece to take relative measurements.
3. **Whitworth Three-Plate Method:** High precision flat surfaces are created without needing an existing flat reference by taking three raw plates (A, B, C) and systematically rubbing and hand-scraping them against each other in rotation (A-B, B-C, C-A) until all three fit together seamlessly without high spots or curvature.

## How to create it

1. **Cast or Quarry the Base:** Cast a ribbed **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** plate with a thick top surface or quarry a stress-free slab of dense granite **[Stone](/en/materials/stone)**.
2. **Stress Relief:** Allow the metal casting to age or heat-treat it to relieve internal thermal stresses that could cause warping over time.
3. **Rough Machining:** Planer-machine or grind the top surface to a rough level plane.
4. **Three-Plate Scraping Method:** Prepare three identical plates. Coat one plate with pigment (Prussian blue), rub it against a second plate, and use a hand scraper to pare down the highlighted contact points. Repeat this rotational procedure across plates A, B, and C until all high spots disappear.
5. **Final Lapping:** Lap the surface using fine abrasive paste and oil to achieve a mirror-like finish and sub-micrometer flatness across the plate.

## Materials needed

- **Plate Body:** Deep-ribbed **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or black granite **[Stone](/en/materials/stone)**.
- **Scraping Tools:** Hardened carbon steel or carbide hand scrapers.
- **Abrasives & Marking Fluid:** Prussian blue oil color, fine emery or silicon carbide lapping compounds.

## Variants and improvements

- **Cast Iron Surface Plates:** Traditional design with heavy underside ribbing; highly durable but requires periodic scraping maintenance.
- **Granite Surface Plates:** Non-magnetic, corrosion-proof, temperature-stable, and does not form burrs if scratched.
- **Optical & Laser Alignment:** Modern calibration using autocollimators or laser interferometers to verify micro-flatness.

## Limits and risks

- **Thermal Expansion:** Temperature fluctuations cause uneven expansion and bowing across the plate.
- **Wear and Dropping Hazards:** Dropping heavy tools damages the plate calibration; localized wear from frequent work creates micro-hollows.
- **Corrosion:** Cast iron plates rust easily if exposed to moisture or fingerprints, requiring constant oiled maintenance.
