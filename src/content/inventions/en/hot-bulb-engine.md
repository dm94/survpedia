# Brief description

A hot bulb engine is a low-compression internal combustion engine that ignites fuel in a separate uncooled chamber (the "hot bulb") preheated by an external blowtorch. Once running, the heat of combustion keeps the bulb hot enough to ignite subsequent charges of heavy, unrefined fuels.

## Use / Function

- **Agricultural Machinery**: Powering heavy tractors and stationary farm engines.
- **Marine Propulsion**: Driving fishing vessels and small coastal cargo boats due to high reliability in harsh environments.
- **Pumping and Milling**: Operating water pumps, sawmills, and grain grinders in remote areas.
- **Scale**: Heavy stationary engines to medium marine and tractor engines.

## Operating principle

1. **Preheating**: The hot bulb (vaporizer) at the top of the cylinder head is heated with an external torch for 5–10 minutes until red-hot.
2. **Air Compression**: The piston compresses fresh air into the hot bulb chamber.
3. **Fuel Injection**: Low-pressure fuel is injected into the hot bulb just before top dead center.
4. **Ignition & Power**: The fuel vaporizes on contact with the hot metal walls and ignites spontaneously, forcing the **[Piston](/en/inventions/piston)** down.
5. **Self-Sustaining Heat**: Subsequent combustion cycles maintain the red-hot temperature of the bulb without requiring external heating or electrical spark plugs.

## How to create it

1. **Cast the Cylinder & Bulb**: Use heavy **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** to withstand thermal shock and repeated explosion cycles.
2. **Build the Vaporizer Chamber**: Mount an uncooled hollow bulb or bulb insert at the cylinder head.
3. **Piston & Rod Assembly**: Fit a heavy-duty piston with iron sealing rings connected to a heavy **[Crankshaft](/en/inventions/crankshaft)**.
4. **Fuel Pump**: Construct a simple mechanical plunger pump to squirt fuel into the bulb at low pressure (much lower pressure than modern diesel injectors).
5. **Flywheel**: Attach a large **[Flywheel](/en/inventions/flywheel)** to carry momentum through two-stroke or four-stroke cycles.

## Materials needed

- **Structural Components**: **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** and **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** for engine block, piston, crankshaft, and bulb.
- **Bearings**: **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** or bronze bushings for crankshaft and connecting rod.
- **Fuels**: **[Crude Oil](/en/materials/crude-oil)**, **[Kerosene](/en/materials/kerosene)**, **[Vegetable Oil](/en/materials/vegetable-oil)**, or heavy residual fuel oils.

## Variants and improvements

- **Two-Stroke Hot Bulb Engine**: Extremely simple design with crankcase scavenging, eliminating valves entirely.
- **Semi-Diesel Engine**: Improved compression ratio with water injection into the bulb to control pre-ignition under heavy loads.
- **Modern Evolution**: Evolved into direct-injection diesel engines as high-precision fuel injection technology matured.

## Limits and risks

- **Start-up Time**: Requires several minutes of preheating with a blowtorch before starting.
- **Thermal Stress**: The hot bulb experiences severe temperature cycles and can crack over time.
- **Runaway Risk**: If fuel accumulates in the crankcase, the engine can experience thermal runaway and overspeed uncontrollably.
