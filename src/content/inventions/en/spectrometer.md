# Brief description

A spectrometer is an optical instrument designed to disperse incoming light into its component wavelengths and quantify their deflection angles or spectral intensity. Evolving from the simple spectroscope of Gustav Kirchhoff and Robert Bunsen, the spectrometer incorporated precision graduated circles and vernier calipers to measure indices of refraction, identify chemical elements via emission and absorption spectra, and pioneer quantitative astrophysics.

## Use / Function

- **Chemical Identification:** Detecting unknown chemical elements and compounds by their distinctive spectral emission and Fraunhofer absorption lines.
- **Refractive Index Measurement:** Measuring the dispersion and refractive index of optical glass, crystals, and transparent liquids.
- **Wavelength Determination:** Calculating the exact wavelengths of light using calibrated optical diffraction gratings or prisms ($n\lambda = d \sin\theta$).
- **Astronomical Analysis:** Determining the elemental composition, radial velocity (Doppler shift), and temperature of celestial bodies.

## Operating principle

1. **Light Collimation:** Light from a source enters through an adjustable narrow slit located at the focal point of an achromatic collimator lens, producing a beam of parallel rays.
2. **Angular Dispersion:** The parallel light beam strikes a dispersing element—either a triangular optical glass prism or a ruled diffraction grating mounted on a leveled central turntable.
3. **Wavelength Separation:** Different wavelengths bend through different angles according to Snell's law of refraction (prisms) or constructive wave interference (diffraction gratings).
4. **Telescopic Focusing:** An objective lens inside an observing telescope gathers the dispersed rays, focusing sharp images of the entry slit (spectral lines) into an eyepiece fitted with crosshairs.
5. **Precision Angle Measurement:** The telescope rotates smoothly around the prism table along a finely graduated circular protractor equipped with vernier scales, allowing angular measurements down to seconds of arc.

## How to create it

1. **Fabricate Base and Turntable:** Cast and machine a heavy circular brass or iron base with three adjustable leveling screws, topped by an independently rotatable central prism table.
2. **Construct Collimator Tube:** Fit a brass tube with an adjustable knife-edge slit at one end and an achromatic convex glass lens at the other, set exactly one focal length apart.
3. **Mount Observing Telescope:** Construct a brass telescope containing an achromatic objective lens, crosshair reticle, and focusing eyepiece, mounted on a counterweighted rotating arm.
4. **Engrave Graduated Circle:** Etch a precise 360-degree scale onto the circular perimeter of the base and mount fine vernier scales on the rotating telescope arm.
5. **Align Optical Prism:** Mount an equilateral flint glass prism on the leveled central table so that its refracting edge aligns parallel with the slit and instrument axis.
6. **Calibrate Optical Train:** Focus the telescope at infinity on a distant object, align the collimator slit to sharp focus, and zero the scale using direct illumination before placing the prism.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Heavy **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** or **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** castings for the stable base and arms, optical **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** for achromatic lenses and the dispersing prism, seasoned **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** for mounting cases and baffles.
- **Tools:** Lathe, graduated dividing engine or circular protractor, fine files, jewelers' screwdrivers, optical polishing paste.
- **Substitutes:** A reflection or transmission diffraction grating ruled onto aluminized glass can replace the glass prism; a hollow glass prism filled with carbon disulfide or water can serve for lower-cost dispersion.

## Variants and improvements

- **Prism Spectrometer:** Employs one or more dense flint glass prisms to achieve high dispersion across the visible spectrum.
- **Grating Spectrometer:** Uses a reflection or transmission grating to produce a linear dispersion directly proportional to wavelength.
- **Constant Deviation Spectrometer:** Uses a Pellin-Broca prism where the selected wavelength always emerges at an exact 90-degree angle from the incident beam.
- **Spectrophotometer:** Integrates a calibrated photometer to measure the quantitative intensity of specific spectral bands for quantitative analytical chemistry.

## Limits and risks

- **Optical Distortion & Aberration:** Uncorrected spherical or chromatic aberration in the collimator or telescope lenses can blur adjacent spectral lines.
- **Thermal Drift:** Temperature fluctuations change the refractive index of glass prisms and alter the physical groove spacing of diffraction gratings.
- **Mechanical Backlash:** Any play or misalignment in the rotating vernier mechanism leads to systematic angular readout errors.
