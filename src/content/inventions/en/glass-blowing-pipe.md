# Brief description

A glass blowing pipe (or blowpipe) is a long, hollow metallic tube used to gather molten glass from a furnace and inflate it into hollow glass vessels like bottles, flasks, and laboratory glassware. Its invention transformed glassmaking from a slow, painstaking molding process into a rapid, versatile craft essential for civilization, science, and optics.

## Use / Function

- **Container Production:** Mass manufacturing of lightweight, airtight hollow glass containers, bottles, and storage jars.
- **Scientific & Chemical Apparatus:** Crafting custom chemical flasks, retorts, test tubes, and distillation receivers.
- **Optical Glass Preforms:** Forming thick glass bulbs and cylinders that can be sliced, flattened, or ground into optical lenses and window panes.

## Operating principle

The blowpipe relies on thermal management and pneumatic expansion:
- **Gathering:** The cool tip of the iron tube is dipped into molten glass (~1,000°C–1,200°C); the viscosity of the glass causes it to adhere to the metal tip as a thick gather.
- **Pneumatic Expansion:** Blowing compressed air down the tube forces air into the center of the soft glass, expanding it uniformly into a hollow sphere (parison).
- **Thermal Isolation:** The long metal tube dissipates heat along its length, allowing the operator to hold and turn the pipe safely without burning their hands, often augmented by a wooden handle or heat shield near the mouthpiece.

## How to create it

1. **Select Metal Tubing:** Obtain a seamless **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** pipe approximately 1.2 to 1.5 meters long, with an outer diameter of 15–20 mm and a wall thickness of 2–3 mm.
2. **Form the Mouthpiece:** Taper one end slightly using a hammer or lathe to create a smooth, comfortable rim that seals comfortably against human lips.
3. **Shape the Tip:** Flare or thicken the opposite tip slightly to create a swollen collar (knob), which helps retain heavy gathers of viscous molten glass.
4. **Add Heat Shield or Handle:** Wrap or fit a wooden sleeve or heat-resistant grip around the upper middle section of the pipe for comfortable manual rotation.
5. **Ream Internal Channel:** Ensure the central bore remains completely clean and free of rust or internal obstructions that could block airflow.

## Materials needed

- **Pipe Body:** Seamless **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or wrought **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** tubing.
- **Grip/Handle:** **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or leather wrap for insulation.
- **Tools:** Hammer, anvil, file, reamer, and lathe or pipe bender (optional).

## Variants and improvements

- **Puntel / Pontil Rod:** A solid metal iron rod used to hold the bottom of a glass vessel while working on its neck or rim after detachment from the blowpipe.
- **Valved Blowpipes:** Modern industrial versions equipped with compressed air lines and pedal-controlled air valves for uniform inflation.
- **Mold-Blowing Pipes:** Used in conjunction with wooden or iron two-part molds to produce identical standardized glass bottles.

## Limits and risks

- **Extreme Heat:** The working end operates near 1,000°C; direct contact causes severe burns.
- **Inhalation Hazards:** Blowing back down the pipe while glass is soft can suck toxic hot gases into the mouth or lungs; breath control is critical.
- **Thermal Stress:** Rapid cooling can crack glass on the pipe tip, causing hot glass shards to fly off unpredictably.
