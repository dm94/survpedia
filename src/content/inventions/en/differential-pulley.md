# Brief description

A differential pulley (also called a Weston differential hoist) is a mechanical lifting device consisting of two fixed pulleys of slightly different diameters joined on the same axle, connected to a continuous loop of chain or rope. It provides massive mechanical advantage, allowing one person to lift loads weighing thousands of kilograms.

## Use / Function

- **Heavy Lifting**: Raising engines, heavy machinery, architectural stone, and timber without powered machinery.
- **Maintenance and Repair**: Holding heavy structural elements safely in place during repairs.
- **Scale**: Workshops, foundries, construction sites, and agricultural applications.

## Operating principle

The mechanism relies on the difference in diameter between two coaxially fixed pulley sheaves (radii $R$ and $r$).

1. **Dual Sheaves**: Two sheaves of slightly different radii are rigidly attached to each other on a single top axle.
2. **Looping Chain/Rope**: An endless chain or rope loops around the larger sheave, drops down around a movable bottom pulley holding the load, loops up around the smaller top sheave, and hangs down.
3. **Mechanical Advantage**: Pulling the chain turns both upper sheaves simultaneously. The larger sheave winds up rope while the smaller sheave lets down rope.
4. **Differential Displacement**: The net upward movement per revolution is equal to half the difference in circumference $\pi(R - r)$.
5. **Self-Locking Feature**: Because the difference between $R$ and $r$ is small, internal friction overcomes the tending torque of the suspended weight, keeping the load automatically suspended without slipping when released.

## How to create it

1. **Casting or Machining Top Sheaves**: Machine or cast two circular sheaves with pocketed teeth (for chain) or deep grooves (for rope) on a single axle. Ensure one sheave is slightly larger than the other (e.g., 20 cm vs 18 cm diameter).
2. **Fabricating Bottom Pulley**: Machine a single smooth bottom pulley with a central hook or eyelet capable of supporting heavy loads.
3. **Assembling Axle and Frame**: Mount the double top sheave in a heavy steel or cast-iron frame equipped with a top hook for hanging from an overhead beam.
4. **Threading the Loop**: Thread an endless welded steel pocket chain or high-tensile rope around the larger top sheave, down under the load pulley, up over the smaller top sheave, and join the ends to form a closed loop.
5. **Testing**: Test under incremental load to confirm smooth chain engagement and automatic self-locking behavior.

## Materials needed

- **Essential materials**:
  - **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**: For the heavy top and bottom sheaves and supporting hooks.
  - **[Rope](/en/materials/rope)** or **[Steel Cable](/en/materials/steel-cable)** (or welded link pocket chain): Continuous loop to transfer pulling force.
- **Tools**:
  - **[Lathe](/en/inventions/lathe)** or Milling Machine: For turning cylindrical sheaves and cutting matching chain pockets or rope grooves.
  - **[Forge](/en/inventions/forge)** or **[Anvil](/en/inventions/anvil)**: For forging hooks, frames, and chain links.
- **Possible substitutes**:
  - Dense hardwood sheaves with grooved rims: Usable for lighter loads up to a few hundred kilograms.

## Variants and improvements

- **Weston Chain Hoist**: Uses pocketed iron wheels and a calibrated welded steel chain to prevent slippage under heavy industrial loads.
- **Worm Gear Hoist**: Uses a worm gear mechanism instead of differential radii; offers even higher mechanical advantage and smoother motion.
- **Spur Gear Block**: Modern evolution offering higher mechanical efficiency using planetary gear sets.

## Limits and risks

- **Frictional Losses**: Mechanical efficiency is under 50% due to high friction (which enables self-locking), requiring more manual pulling effort than theoretical calculations suggest.
- **Rope or Chain Jamming**: Disaligned chains can jump off pockets or jam between sheaves, risking sudden load drops.
- **Structural Beam Failure**: The overhead support beam or tripod must be strong enough to hold both the hoist and the heavy lifted load.
