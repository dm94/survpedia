# Brief description

A psychrometer is a specialized hygrometer designed to measure relative humidity in the atmosphere. By comparing the temperatures of two liquid-in-glass thermometers—one dry and one wrapped in a wet fabric wick—it measures the cooling effect of water evaporation, which is directly linked to atmospheric moisture content.

## Use / Function

- **Meteorological Forecasting:** Assessing atmospheric moisture levels to predict humidity trends, fog formation, and rainfall.
- **Grain & Food Storage:** Monitoring ambient moisture in granaries, cold storage, and silos to prevent mold growth and crop decay.
- **Industrial Drying Operations:** Regulating humidity in textile mills, timber drying kilns, and paper manufacturing plants.

## Operating principle

1. **Evaporative Cooling:** Water evaporating from a surface absorbs latent heat from its surroundings, lowering the local temperature.
2. **Humidity Differential:** Dry air causes rapid evaporation and a significant temperature drop on the wet bulb, whereas saturated humid air slows evaporation, keeping the wet bulb temperature close to the dry bulb temperature.
3. **Psychrometric Calculation:** The difference between dry-bulb temperature ($T_d$) and wet-bulb temperature ($T_w$), known as the wet-bulb depression, allows direct lookup or calculation of relative humidity and dew point.

## How to create it

1. **Mount Thermometers:** Mount two identical liquid-in-glass thermometers side by side on a sturdy wooden or brass frame.
2. **Attach Wick:** Cover the bulb of one thermometer with a clean, un-sized cotton muslin fabric sleeve.
3. **Moisten Fabric:** Saturate the muslin fabric wick with pure distilled water, extending the wick into a small water reservoir if building a stationary unit.
4. **Ventilate:** Provide airflow over the bulbs (at least 3 m/s) either by whirling the frame on a swivel handle (sling psychrometer) or using a mechanical fan.
5. **Read Temperatures:** Allow airflow for 1–2 minutes until the wet-bulb reading stabilizes at its minimum temperature, then record both dry and wet bulb values.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Two liquid-in-glass thermometers, clean cotton **[Fabric](/en/materials/fabric)** wick, distilled **[Water](/en/materials/water)**, **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or metal frame, **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)**.
- **Tools:** Scissors, mounting hardware, psychrometric lookup chart or slide rule.
- **Substitutes:** Porous paper or linen can substitute for cotton muslin.

## Variants and improvements

- **Sling Psychrometer:** Mounted on a chain or hinged handle that is whirled by hand to ensure consistent air velocity over the wet bulb.
- **Aspirated Psychrometer (Assmann):** Uses a spring-wound or electric fan inside polished shielding tubes to shield against radiant heat while providing constant airflow.

## Limits and risks

- **Freezing Point:** Below 0°C, water on the wick freezes; latent heat of sublimation must be accounted for in calculations.
- **Impure Water:** Using tap water causes mineral deposits on the fabric wick, impeding capillary action and skewing readings.
- **Radiant Heat:** Direct sunlight warming the thermometers will cause false high readings unless proper radiation shields are used.
