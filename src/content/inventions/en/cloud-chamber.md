# Brief description

A cloud chamber (or Wilson cloud chamber) is a sealed particle detector that renders the paths of ionizing radiation visible. It relies on supersaturated vapor that condenses around ions created along the trajectory of charged subatomic particles.

## Use / Function

- **Radiation Detection:** Visualizes cosmic rays, alpha particles, beta particles, and other forms of ionizing radiation without electronic sensors.
- **Particle Identification:** Distinct track lengths and thicknesses allow identification of particle types (e.g., short, thick tracks for alpha particles; thin, wispy tracks for beta particles).
- **Fundamental Physics Research:** Served as a foundational instrument in early particle physics and nuclear discovery.
- **Scale:** Small tabletop laboratory equipment.

## Operating principle

The chamber operates on the principle of supersaturation created by a temperature gradient.

- **Supersaturated Vapor:** Isopropyl or ethyl alcohol evaporates near the warm top of the container and sinks toward the cold bottom, creating a region of supersaturated alcohol vapor.
- **Ionization:** As energetic charged particles pass through the chamber, they strip electrons from air and alcohol molecules along their paths, creating lines of ions.
- **Condensation:** The ions act as nucleation points. Vapor instantly condenses onto these ions, forming visible droplets that trace the trajectory of the particle.

## How to create it

1. **Chamber Base:** Prepare a flat, dark metal plate at the bottom of a transparent container made of **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** to provide contrast for the white mist tracks.
2. **Absorbent Top:** Attach felt or sponges soaked in high-purity **[Alcohol](/en/materials/alcohol)** (isopropyl or ethanol) near the inside top of the chamber.
3. **Sealing:** Seal the transparent chamber to the base to prevent air currents and maintain a closed system.
4. **Cooling:** Place the base plate on top of dry ice or an active cooling plate to create a steep vertical temperature gradient.
5. **Illumination:** Shine a concentrated, horizontal light beam through the glass side of the chamber across the bottom cold zone.
6. **Observation:** Once supersaturated vapor forms, observe thin white condensation trails appearing illuminated against the dark base.

## Materials needed

- **Chamber Body:** High-transparency **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** or clear container.
- **Base Plate:** Dark, heat-conductive **[Metal](/en/materials/metal)** plate.
- **Vapor Source:** High-purity isopropyl or ethyl **[Alcohol](/en/materials/alcohol)**.
- **Absorbent Pad:** Felt or sponge to hold the alcohol.

## Variants and improvements

- **Diffusion Cloud Chamber:** Continuously cooled bottom creates a steady supersaturated layer without needing expansion valves.
- **Expansion Cloud Chamber:** Uses sudden volume expansion to adiabatically cool the vapor.
- **Bubble Chamber:** Uses superheated liquid instead of vapor, offering higher density for detecting high-energy particles.

## Limits and risks

- **Flammability:** Concentration of alcohol vapor presents a fire risk if exposed to open sparks or flames.
- **Cold Hazards:** Dry ice causes severe cold burns upon direct skin contact.
- **Short Lifetime:** Requires continuous replenishment of cold source or alcohol vapor.
