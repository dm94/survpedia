# Brief description

A Kaplan turbine is a propeller-type reaction water turbine with adjustable blades, specifically engineered for generating power under low hydraulic heads (low water drop height, typically 2 to 70 meters) and high volumetric flow rates. Unlike fixed-blade propeller turbines, the Kaplan turbine allows both its runner blades and guide vanes to adjust automatically according to changing water flow, maintaining high efficiency across a wide range of operating conditions.

## Use / Function

- **Primary use**: Generating continuous, large-scale hydroelectricity from low-head river systems, tidal barrages, and run-of-the-river dams.
- **Secondary uses**: Regulating water release rates in irrigation canals while harvesting mechanical energy, and powering large-scale water transfer pumps in reverse configuration.
- **Scale**: Medium-to-large industrial installations (from hundreds of kilowatts in small canal installations to hundreds of megawatts in major river dams).

## Operating principle

**Axial Flow and Adjustable Hydrodynamics**:

1. **Water Entry**: Pressurized water enters radially through a spiral scroll case and passes through adjustable guide vanes (wicket gates) arranged in a circle.
2. **Radial-to-Axial Flow Transition**: The guide vanes swirl the water and direct it axially along the turbine shaft toward the propeller runner.
3. **Lift Generation**: As water flows axially past the propeller blades, the hydrofoil shape of the blades produces aerodynamic-like lift, causing the runner to spin rapidly.
4. **Variable Pitch Adjustment**: Internal pitch mechanisms dynamically rotate the individual runner blades to match the angle of incoming water, maintaining peak aerodynamic efficiency even when water flow rates fluctuate.
5. **Draft Tube Recovery**: Water exits axially through a widening draft tube below the tailrace, recovering remaining kinetic energy as static pressure to maximize total hydraulic head.

## How to create it

### 1. Runner Hub and Adjustable Blade Assembly

1. Machine a hollow central runner hub out of cast **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)** to house the blade pitch control linkages.
2. Cast hydrofoil propeller blades (typically 3 to 6 blades) from corrosion-resistant steel or bronze.
3. Mount the blades onto the hub using pivot trunnions and internal lever linkages connected to a central control rod extending through the hollow shaft.

### 2. Guide Vanes and Housing Assembly

1. Construct a cylindrical outer housing with an expanding lower conical section (**[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)**).
2. Install a circular ring of pivoting guide vanes (wicket gates) above the runner, linked to an external control ring.

### 3. Shaft and Governor System

1. Connect the runner hub to a vertical **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** shaft supported by thrust and guide bearings.
2. Link the blade control rod and wicket gate ring to a hydraulic governor mechanism or mechanical linkages for synchronized pitch control.

## Materials needed

- **Runner Blades & Hub**: High-strength **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or corrosion-resistant **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)**.
- **Outer Casing & Draft Tube**: **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)**, structural steel plate, or reinforced concrete.
- **Shaft & Linkages**: Heavy-duty forged carbon steel.
- **Working Fluid**: Low-head **[Water](/en/materials/water)** supplied from a river or low-dam reservoir.

## Variants and improvements

- **Bulb Turbine**: A horizontal inline variant where the generator and turbine are sealed inside a streamlined "bulb" submerged directly in the water flow, reducing civil engineering costs for low-head sites.
- **Tube Turbine**: An inclined or horizontal inline Kaplan turbine where the generator is mounted externally outside the water duct, simplified for smaller river projects.
- **VLH (Very Low Head) Turbine**: A slow-rotating, large-diameter Kaplan variant operating efficiently on water drops under 3 meters with minimal impact on aquatic life.

## Limits and risks

- **Cavitation Risk**: Low pressure on the suction side of the propeller blades can cause destructive cavitation bubbles, requiring precise depth submergence below the tailrace.
- **Mechanical Complexity**: The internal hub mechanism required to adjust moving blades under immense water pressure increases maintenance demands and risk of oil leaks.
- **Fish Mortality**: High-speed blade rotation can harm migratory fish unless fish screens or eco-friendly low-blade-count designs are utilized.
