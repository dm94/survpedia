# Brief description

A hydrocyclone is a static device that utilizes centrifugal force within a conical chamber to separate solids from liquids or separate liquids of different densities. Featuring zero moving parts, it is a highly reliable tool for water desanding, slurry clarification, and mineral processing.

## Use / Function

- **Water Desanding:** Removing sand, silt, and grit from raw river or well water before filtration.
- **Mineral Processing:** Classifying particle sizes in mining slurries or separating dense ores from waste.
- **Wastewater Treatment:** Primary separation of heavy solid suspended particles.
- **Scale:** Agricultural irrigation pre-filtration, municipal water pre-treatment, and heavy industrial plant operations.

## Operating principle

The hydrocyclone operates on **Vortex Centrifugal Acceleration**:

1. **Tangential Feed:** Fluid mixture is pumped under pressure through a tangential inlet at the top of a cylindrical section.
2. **Outer Vortex:** The tangential entry forces the liquid into a high-speed downward spiral along the outer wall.
3. **Centrifugal Separation:** High centrifugal forces push dense particles outward against the wall. Dense solids slide down the conical section toward the bottom discharge apex (underflow).
4. **Inner Vortex:** Near the narrow apex, fluid pressure forces the lighter, clarified liquid to reverse direction and form an inner upward vortex.
5. **Overflow Discharge:** The clean liquid inner vortex exits through a central top tube called a vortex finder (overflow).

## How to create it

1. **Fabricating the Outer Body:** Roll **[Sheet Metal](/en/materials/sheet-metal)** into a cylindrical upper section attached to an elongated lower conical section tapering downward at a 10°–20° angle.
2. **Fitting the Tangential Inlet:** Weld a rectangular or pipe inlet tangentially onto the upper cylindrical chamber so incoming fluid enters smoothly along the curved wall.
3. **Installing the Vortex Finder:** Position a smaller vertical pipe centrally through the top plate, extending slightly below the tangential inlet level to prevent raw fluid from bypassing directly into the overflow.
4. **Machining the Apex Nozzle:** Fit an adjustable or replaceable abrasion-resistant **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** nozzle at the bottom cone tip for solid underflow discharge.
5. **Sealing Assembly:** Weld all seams pressure-tight to withstand operating pressures of 1 to 3 bar.
6. **Connecting Supply:** Connect a pump or elevated header tank supplying **[Water](/en/materials/water)** slurry into the tangential inlet.

## Materials needed

- **[Sheet Metal](/en/materials/sheet-metal)** or **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**: For the cylindrical body, conical cone, and vortex finder pipe.
- **[Water](/en/materials/water)**: Primary liquid carrier for slurry separation.
- **Flanges or Threaded Couplings**: For piping connections to pump feed, overflow, and underflow.

## Variants and improvements

- **Multi-cyclone Manifold:** Linking multiple small hydrocyclones in parallel for high flow rates and finer particle separation.
- **Polymer / Ceramic Lined Hydrocyclone:** Lining steel interiors with wear-resistant polyurethane or ceramic tiles to resist severe abrasive slurries.
- **Dense Medium Cyclone:** Utilizing a dense magnetic slurry medium to float light coal or minerals while sinking dense ores.

## Limits and risks

- **Abrasion:** High-velocity grit causes severe erosion on the conical lower walls and apex nozzle over time.
- **Clogging:** Excessive large debris or fibers can block the narrow apex orifice, interrupting separation.
- **Pressure Sensitivity:** Requires consistent feed pressure; sudden pressure drops collapse the internal vortex and degrade separation efficiency.
