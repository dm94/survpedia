# Brief description

A torsion balance is an exquisitely sensitive physical apparatus designed to measure extremely weak forces—such as electrostatic attraction/repulsion or gravitational pull—by observing the angular twisting of a thin wire or fiber. Invented independently by Charles-Augustin de Coulomb and John Michell (and refined by Henry Cavendish), it transformed empirical physics by allowing the direct quantification of fundamental physical constants.

## Use / Function

- **Force Quantification:** Measuring minuscule electrostatic, magnetic, or gravitational forces acting between small masses or charges.
- **Fundamental Constants:** Determining Newton's universal gravitational constant ($G$) and testing inverse-square laws of physics.
- **Geophysical Surveying:** Detecting subtle regional variations in gravitational gradient to locate underground mineral, salt, or petroleum formations.

## Operating principle

1. **Equilibrium Suspension:** A light horizontal beam with two small spherical masses at its ends is suspended at its center by a fine, elastic vertical wire or quartz fiber inside a draft-free housing.
2. **Torque Deflection:** When known test charges or external large masses are positioned near the beam's end spheres, their attraction or repulsion exerts a torque ($\tau = F \cdot r$) on the beam.
3. **Restoring Torque Balance:** The beam rotates horizontally until the twisting force of the suspension wire equals the applied external torque ($\tau = -\kappa \theta$).
4. **Optical Amplification:** A tiny lightweight mirror attached to the vertical suspension wire reflects a beam of light onto a graduated scale, amplifying minute angular rotations into easily readable deflections without introducing mechanical friction.

## How to create it

1. **Construct Isolation Housing:** Build a sturdy wooden enclosure fitted with glass windows to shield the suspension assembly completely from air currents, thermal drafts, and static electricity from observers.
2. **Prepare Suspension Wire:** Mount a very fine, uniform metallic wire (such as silver, phosphor bronze, or drawn fused quartz fiber) from an adjustable top torsion head featuring a circular graduated dial.
3. **Fabricate Light Beam:** Assemble a rigid, ultra-light wooden dowel or glass rod balance beam with two identical small lead or gilded pith balls affixed to each end.
4. **Attach Optical Mirror:** Mount a tiny fragment of silvered glass mirror at the junction where the wire meets the horizontal balance beam.
5. **Install External Masses or Electrodes:** Provide movable external lead spheres or charged metal probes positioned on an outer rotating frame to interact with the suspended masses without disturbing the inner atmosphere.
6. **Calibrate Oscillation Period:** Measure the natural period of torsional oscillation of the free beam to precisely determine the wire's torsional spring constant ($\kappa$).

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Fine metallic **[Wire](/en/materials/wire)** or quartz filament, **[Silver](/en/materials/silver)** mirror fragment, **[Lead](/en/materials/lead)** spheres or test masses, clear **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)** panels, seasoned **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** for framing.
- **Tools:** Jewelers' pliers, collimated light source or candle, graduated scale, stopwatch for oscillation timing.
- **Substitutes:** Drawn tungsten filament or silk fiber can serve as the suspension line; copper or brass spheres can replace lead.

## Variants and improvements

- **Coulomb's Electrostatic Balance:** Features insulating glass rods and gilded elder-pith spheres to verify Coulomb's law of electrostatic force.
- **Cavendish Gravitational Balance:** Utilizes large, heavy lead counterweights outside the wooden isolation chamber to measure gravitational force directly between laboratory masses.
- **Eötvös Torsion Balance:** Arranges weights at two different heights to measure gravitational gradients and tidal curvatures with exceptional precision for geophysical exploration.

## Limits and risks

- **Draft and Thermal Convection:** Even microscopic convective air currents caused by nearby human body heat can overpower the tiny forces being measured, requiring long settling times and thick insulation.
- **Fiber Drift and Creep:** Metallic suspension wires suffer from elastic fatigue, hysteresis, and thermal expansion, altering calibration over prolonged periods.
- **Electrostatic Interference:** Stray static charges on the housing walls or observer can produce electrostatic attraction far greater than the gravitational forces under observation.
