# Brief description

The spinning mule is an industrial spinning machine invented by Samuel Crompton in 1779. It combines the drafting rollers of Richard Arkwright's water frame with the movable carriage and spindle twisting mechanism of James Hargreaves' spinning jenny, allowing for the mass production of exceptionally strong, fine, and uniform yarn.

## Use / Function

- **High-Quality Yarn Spinning:** Produces both soft weft yarns and high-tensile warp yarns suitable for fine fabrics like muslin.
- **Large-Scale Textile Manufacturing:** Enabled factory-level yarn production operating hundreds of spindles simultaneously on a single frame.
- **Automation of Textile Production:** Drastically reduced human labor requirements per unit of yarn spun while dramatically expanding manufacturing output.
- **Scale:** Industrial factory scale.

## Operating principle

1. **Fiber Feeding & Drafting:** Roving (loose bundles of fibers) passes through multiple pairs of differential rollers that rotate at increasing speeds to draw out and attenuate the fiber bundle.
2. **Carriage Extension & Twisting:** A movable carriage holding vertical spinning spindles rolls away from the drafting rollers. As it recedes, the spindles spin rapidly to twist the stretched fibers into thread.
3. **Drafting and Twisting Balance:** By combining simultaneous drawing out (drafting) and twisting on the moving carriage, stress is distributed evenly along the yarn, preventing weak spots and producing thin, uniform thread.
4. **Carriage Return & Winding:** Once twisted, the carriage returns toward the rollers, and a guiding faller wire layers the newly spun yarn neatly onto bobbins mounted on the spindles.

## How to create it

1. **Build the Main Frame:** Construct a sturdy long wooden and iron frame capable of remaining rigid under mechanical strain and vibration.
2. **Mount the Drafting Rollers:** Install series of paired metal rollers at the back of the frame, geared to rotate at increasing differential speeds.
3. **Assemble the Movable Carriage:** Build a wheeled carriage on track rails in front of the rollers, carrying a bank of vertical iron spindles.
4. **Install Drive Mechanisms:** Connect a central power shaft (powered by water wheel or steam engine) via belts, pulleys, and ropes to turn the drafting rollers, drive the carriage in and out, and rotate the spindles.
5. **Attach Faller Wires:** Position spring-loaded faller wires above the spindles to control yarn tension and guide the thread onto bobbins during the carriage return stroke.

## Materials needed

- **Wood:** Hardwood for structural beams, carriage frames, and tracks.
- **Iron:** Cast iron and wrought iron for gearing, rollers, spindles, rails, and shafts.
- **Cotton Yarn / Fiber:** Raw cotton or wool rovings for spinning input, along with cordage for drive bands.

## Variants and improvements

- **Hand Mule:** Early version where the carriage was pushed back manually by the spinner.
- **Self-Acting Mule (Roberts Mule):** Developed by Richard Roberts in 1825, fully automating the carriage return, twisting, and winding cycle.
- **Ring Spinning Frame:** Continuous spinning system that eventually superseded the mule for many standard cotton yarns due to higher continuous output speed.

## Limits and risks

- **Intermittent Cycle:** Unlike ring spinning, the mule operates in discrete steps (draw/twist, then wind/return), limiting continuous throughput speed.
- **Mechanical Complexity:** Requires precise timing and synchronization of rollers, carriage motion, spindle speeds, and faller wire movement.
- **Safety Hazards:** Rapidly moving carriages and exposed belts present severe pinch and crushing risks for operators in historical mill settings.
