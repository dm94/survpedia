# Brief description

A Geneva drive (or Maltese cross mechanism) is a gear mechanism that translates a continuous rotation movement into an intermittent rotary motion. The rotating drive wheel has a pin that reaches into a slot of the driven wheel, advancing it by one step at a time.

## Use / Function

- **Intermittent Indexing**: Advancing film frame-by-frame in motion picture projectors, index tables, and automated assembly machinery.
- **Mechanical Clocks and Watches**: Preventing over-winding of mainsprings (Geneva stopwork mechanism).
- **Automated Tooling**: Rotating tool carousels or turret indexing on industrial lathes and milling machines.
- **Scale**: Small precision instruments to industrial automation machinery.

## Operating principle

The mechanism consists of a driving wheel equipped with a raised pin and a concentric locking disc, and a driven wheel (the cross) with radial slots and matching arc cutouts.

1. As the drive wheel rotates continuously, its locking disc slides along the matching arc cutout of the driven wheel, locking the driven wheel in place.
2. At a specific angle in the rotation, the pin on the drive wheel enters one of the radial slots on the driven wheel.
3. As the drive wheel continues to turn, the pin pushes the slot, rotating the driven wheel by a precise fraction of a full circle (e.g., 90 degrees for a 4-slot cross).
4. Concurrently, a cutout in the drive wheel's locking disc allows the driven wheel to turn.
5. Once the pin exits the slot, the solid circular locking disc re-engages the arc cutout of the driven wheel, locking it firmly until the next complete rotation of the drive wheel.

## How to create it

1. **Machining the Driven Cross**: Cut a flat metal disc into a wheel with 4 or more symmetrical radial slots. Machine circular concave arcs between the slots along the perimeter to match the curvature of the drive wheel's locking disc.
2. **Fabricating the Drive Wheel**: Create a circular disc with a raised cylindrical pin offset near its outer radius. Machine a notch in the concentric locking rim adjacent to the pin to provide clearance for the driven wheel's points while advancing.
3. **Mounting Axles**: Mount both wheels on parallel rigid shafts set at an exact center distance so the drive pin enters the radial slots smoothly without jamming or excessive play.
4. **Assembly and Lubrication**: Align the locking rim and pin with the driven cross slots, secure the shafts on bearings or bushings, and apply machine oil or grease for smooth operation.

## Materials needed

- **Essential materials**:
  - **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**: High durability steel for the pin, slots, and locking ring to withstand repeated contact impacts.
  - **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)**: Low-friction alloy suitable for bushings or low-load driven wheels.
- **Tools**:
  - **[Lathe](/en/inventions/lathe)** or Milling Machine: For turning cylindrical discs and cutting precise radial slots and locking arcs.
  - **[Calipers](/en/inventions/calipers)** or **[Vernier Caliper](/en/inventions/vernier-caliper)**: For measuring center distance, slot width, and pin diameter.
- **Possible substitutes**:
  - Hardwood or dense plastic (e.g., Bakelite): Usable for low-speed, light-duty demonstration mechanisms.

## Variants and improvements

- **External Geneva Drive**: The standard form where the driven cross is outside the drive wheel diameter (driven wheel rotates in the opposite direction of the drive wheel).
- **Internal Geneva Drive**: The driven cross surrounds the drive wheel, resulting in a smaller overall profile and longer duration of engagement per revolution (driven wheel rotates in the same direction).
- **Spherical Geneva Drive**: Operates on perpendicular axes (3D layout) rather than parallel shafts.

## Limits and risks

- **Impact Shock and Wear**: At high rotational speeds, the entry of the pin into the slot creates mechanical impact shock, leading to wear and noise.
- **Fixed Kinematics**: The ratio of motion time to dwell time is fixed by the geometry and number of slots (e.g., 4 slots mean 1/4 rotation time and 3/4 dwell time).
- **Precision Sensitivity**: Misalignment of shaft center distances or improperly sized pins will cause binding, severe backlash, or mechanical jamming.
