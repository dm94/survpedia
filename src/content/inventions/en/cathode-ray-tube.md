# Brief description

A Cathode Ray Tube (CRT) is a specialized vacuum tube that directs a focused beam of electrons onto a phosphorescent screen to create visible images or signal waveforms. It served as the foundation for early television screens, oscilloscopes, and radar displays.

## Use / Function

- **Visual Display:** Converts electronic signals into immediate visual wave displays or image patterns.
- **Signal Diagnosis:** Displays voltage variations over time when paired with sweep circuits.
- **Radar & Navigation:** Plots echo reflections and targets on polar coordinates.
- **Early Television:** Scans raster lines to display video images.

## Operating principle

1. **Electron Gun:** A **[Tungsten](/en/materials/tungsten)** filament is heated, releasing electrons via thermionic emission. An accelerating anode pulls them into a narrow beam.
2. **Deflection System:** Electrostatic deflection plates (or electromagnetic coils) steer the electron beam horizontally and vertically based on input signals.
3. **Fluorescent Screen:** The focused beam hits a screen coated with phosphors (such as zinc sulfide), producing a glow at the point of impact.
4. **Vacuum Environment:** Inside a glass envelope evacuated by a **[Vacuum Pump](/en/inventions/vacuum-pump)**, electrons travel freely without colliding with air molecules.

## How to create it

1. **Form the Envelope:** Blow a glass bulb with a funnel body and flat faceplate, capable of withstanding atmospheric pressure.
2. **Construct the Gun Assembly:** Align the heater filament, control grid, and focusing anodes on a central stem.
3. **Mount Deflection Plates:** Position perpendicular metal plates along the neck for horizontal and vertical beam steering.
4. **Apply Phosphor Coating:** Coat the inner faceplate with phosphor powder suspended in a volatile binder and dry thoroughly.
5. **Evacuate and Seal:** Connect to a high-capacity **[Vacuum Pump](/en/inventions/vacuum-pump)**, bake to degas internal parts, seal the neck, and flash a getter metal to absorb residual gases.

## Materials needed

- **Envelope:** Heavy **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)**.
- **Emitter Filament:** **[Tungsten](/en/materials/tungsten)**.
- **Deflection & Grid:** **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)**, **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)**, or nickel sheets.
- **Internal Coating:** Phosphor powder (such as zinc sulfide) and graphite (Aquadag coating for charge return).

## Variants and improvements

- **Electrostatic CRT:** Uses deflection plates inside the tube; ideal for high-frequency oscilloscopes.
- **Electromagnetic CRT:** Uses external deflection coils; ideal for wider screen televisions and radar displays.
- **Color CRT:** Employs three electron guns (red, green, blue) and a shadow mask.
- **Storage CRT:** Retains screen traces for prolonged analysis without constant refreshing.

## Limits and risks

- **Implosion Risk:** High interior vacuum creates significant mechanical force on the glass bulb; shattered glass can implode violently.
- **High Voltage Hazard:** Requires kilovolts (kV) to accelerate the electron beam, posing severe electrical shock hazards.
- **X-Ray Emission:** High-voltage acceleration can emit soft X-rays, requiring thick leaded glass shielding.
- **Size and Mass:** Bulkier and heavier than modern solid-state displays.
