# Brief description

A tachometer is a mechanical or electromechanical instrument that measures the rotational speed of a shaft, spindle, or machine rotor, typically expressed in revolutions per minute (RPM). Developed during the Industrial Revolution by engineers such as Bryan Donkin to regulate steam engines and milling equipment, the centrifugal mechanical tachometer provided operators with real-time feedback on prime-mover velocity, preventing catastrophic overspeed fly-apart accidents and ensuring consistent power distribution.

## Use / Function

- **Engine RPM Monitoring:** Measuring crankshaft and output shaft speeds in steam engines, internal combustion engines, and water turbines.
- **Machinery Calibration:** Setting optimal spindle speeds on lathes, milling machines, and boring engines to preserve cutting tools and achieve precise tolerances.
- **Governor Feedback:** Providing direct kinematic feedback to throttle linkages or steam valves to maintain steady operational pace under changing mechanical loads.
- **Conveyor and Pulley Speed Tuning:** Verifying that belt drives, grain elevators, and industrial pumps run within safe structural design limits.

## Operating principle

1. **Rotational Coupling:** The tachometer spindle connects directly (via a rubber friction tip or geared drive shaft) to the revolving component under test.
2. **Centrifugal Flyweight Action:** As the internal spindle spins, a pair of diametrically opposed flyweights hinged on link arms swings outward against the restraint of an opposing calibrated steel spring.
3. **Axial Collar Displacement:** The outward swing of the weights translates into axial sliding movement along the central shaft via a grooved collar.
4. **Mechanical Amplification:** A rack-and-pinion sector or bell-crank lever engages the sliding collar, converting linear axial displacement into angular rotation.
5. **Dial Indication:** A balanced needle sweeping across a calibrated circular dial face indicates instantaneous revolutions per minute with steady damping to prevent flutter.

## How to create it

1. **Turn the Main Spindle:** Turn a precision steel spindle on a lathe, mounting two radial pivot brackets for the centrifugal weight arms.
2. **Fabricate Centrifugal Flyweights:** Cast and balance two identical brass or lead-loaded flyweights and connect them to the spindle brackets with articulated linkage arms.
3. **Mount Counterbalance Spring:** Install a calibrated helical compression or tension spring of spring steel around the spindle to resist the centrifugal throw of the flyweights.
4. **Assemble Sliding Sleeve:** Fit a grooved bronze or brass sleeve that slides freely along the spindle, linked to the flyweight arms.
5. **Build Gear Sector and Pinion:** Machine a fine-toothed brass quadrant rack engaging a hardened steel center pinion that carries the dial needle.
6. **Enclose and Calibrate:** House the mechanism in a cylindrical cast iron or brass casing with a protective glass lens; calibrate the scale against a known stroboscopic pulse or a mechanical rev counter timed with a seconds stopwatch.

## Materials needed

- **Essential:** Hardened **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** for shafts, springs, and drive pins; **[Brass](/en/materials/brass)** for centrifugal linkages, rack sectors, and bushings; cast **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)** for the outer housing.
- **Tools:** Metal lathe, gear-cutting hob or files, drill press, spring winding jig, precision stopwatch for calibration.
- **Substitutes:** A drag-cup magnetic tachometer uses a spinning permanent magnet inducing eddy currents in a copper or aluminum cup against a hairspring.

## Variants and improvements

- **Centrifugal Mechanical Tachometer:** The classic flyweight design, self-contained and completely independent of electrical power.
- **Chronometric Tachometer:** Combines a revolution-counting wheel and a clockwork escapement that samples rotations every second, eliminating centrifugal spring calibration drift.
- **Eddy-Current (Magnetic) Tachometer:** Uses rotating magnets to induce eddy currents in a lightweight metal cup, producing a deflection proportional to speed.
- **Electrical Generator Tachometer:** Coupes a miniature DC generator or dynamo where output voltage directly indicates rotational speed on a voltmeter.

## Limits and risks

- **Frictional Wear:** High continuous RPM causes friction on pivot pins and sliding sleeves, requiring regular lubrication to avoid sluggish response.
- **Spring Fatigue:** Extended mechanical cycling or heat exposure can soften the steel restraining spring, leading to over-reading of actual RPM.
- **Slippage on Drive Contact:** Handheld models relying on friction cones or rubber tips can slip against greasy shafts, yielding falsely low readings.
