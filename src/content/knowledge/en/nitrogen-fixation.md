# Brief description

Nitrogen fixation is the chemical or biological process by which inert atmospheric nitrogen gas ($N_2$) is converted into bioavailable nitrogenous compounds such as ammonia ($NH_3$), nitrates ($NO_3^-$), or nitrites ($NO_2^-$) necessary for soil fertility and life.

## Use / Function

- **Agricultural Soil Restoration**: Replenishes bioavailable nitrogen in nitrogen-depleted agricultural soils.
- **Crop Rotation and Yield Expansion**: Leguminous cover crops convert atmospheric nitrogen to dramatically boost cereal yields.
- **Industrial Fertilizer Raw Material**: Produces precursors for synthetic nitrogen fertilizers like ammonium nitrate and urea.
- **Scale**: From natural legume root nodules to multi-ton industrial plants.

## Operating principle

Atmospheric nitrogen ($N_2$) contains an extremely strong triple covalent bond ($N \equiv N$) that renders it chemically unreactive to most organisms. Nitrogen fixation breaks this bond through specialized enzymes or extreme energy input:

1. **Biological Fixation**: Symbiotic _Rhizobium_ bacteria inside legume root nodules use the nitrogenase enzyme to reduce atmospheric $N_2$ to ammonia ($NH_3$) at ambient pressure and temperature.
2. **Thermal/Electric Arc Fixation**: High-voltage electric arcs force atmospheric $N_2$ and $O_2$ to combine into nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$), which dissolve in water to form nitric acid.
3. **Chemical Catalysis**: Industrial catalytic reaction of nitrogen gas with hydrogen under extreme pressure and heat (Haber-Bosch process).

## How to implement

1. **Incorporate Leguminous Crops**: Plant clover, alfalfa, peas, or soybeans as cover crops or intercrops to naturally introduce _Rhizobium_ nodules into depleted fields.
2. **Apply Organic Composting**: Combine nitrogen-rich organic matter like **[Manure](/en/materials/manure)** with carbon materials to encourage aerobic nitrogen-fixing soil microbe proliferation.
3. **Electric Arc Nitrogen Fixation**: Construct a simple high-voltage **[Electric Arc Furnace](/en/inventions/electric-arc-furnace)** through which air is blown to produce nitric acid solutions for soil fertilization.
4. **Bio-Inoculation**: Inoculate legume seeds with specific _Rhizobium_ bacterial strains prior to sowing to maximize root nodule formation.

## Materials needed

- **Biological Hosts**: Legume seeds (clover, beans, peas, vetch) and bacterial inoculants.
- **Organic Waste**: Livestock **[Manure](/en/materials/manure)** and plant residues for green manuring.
- **Arc Nitrogen Fixer**: High-voltage spark generator, reaction chamber, and **[Water](/en/materials/water)** absorber.

## Variants and improvements

- **Green Manuring**: Tilling nitrogen-rich leguminous biomass directly into topsoil prior to planting primary staple crops.
- **Birkeland-Eyde Process**: Historical electric arc technology that utilizes renewable hydroelectric power to synthesize nitrates from air and water.
- **Free-Living Bacteria Inoculation**: Utilizing free-living soil microbes (_Azotobacter_, _Clostridium_) for non-legume crops.

## Limits and risks

- **High Energy Requirement**: Abiotic nitrogen fixation requires immense thermal or electrical energy to break the $N \equiv N$ bond.
- **Soil Acidification**: Excess ammonium fertilizer application without buffering lime leads to progressive soil acidification.
- **Eutrophication**: Runoff of excess nitrates into water bodies causes oxygen-depleting algal blooms that collapse aquatic ecosystems.
