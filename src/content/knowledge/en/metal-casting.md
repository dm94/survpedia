# Brief description

Metal casting is a manufacturing process in which liquid molten metal is poured into a mold cavity containing the negative shape of the desired component, where it cools and solidifies into a precise metallic object.

## Use / Function

- **Engine and Machine Production**: Creates complex engine blocks, cylinders, gears, and structural housings.
- **Tooling and Hardware**: Produces anvil blocks, valves, pump bodies, and heavy industrial machinery frames.
- **Artistic and Domestic Items**: Casts bells, cookware, statues, and decorative metalwork.
- **Scale**: Small artisan foundries to massive industrial steel mills.

## Operating principle

Metal casting utilizes the fluidity of liquid metals above their melting points to replicate intricate shapes:

1. **Pattern Creation**: A replica (pattern) of the object is made from wood, wax, or metal.
2. **Mold Preparation**: The pattern is packed in a refractory molding material (such as green sand or ceramic slurry) to form a cavity with gating systems and risers.
3. **Melting**: Metals or alloys are heated in a **[Furnace](/en/inventions/furnace)** using a **[Crucible](/en/inventions/crucible)** until completely molten.
4. **Pouring**: Molten metal is poured smoothly into the mold cavity.
5. **Solidification and Shakeout**: The metal cools, shrinks, and hardens. The mold is broken or separated to retrieve the finished casting.

## How to implement

1. **Prepare Green Sand Molding**: Mix silica **[Sand](/en/materials/sand)** with **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)** (bentonite) and a small amount of water to create reusable molding sand.
2. **Form Flask Cavity**: Place the wooden pattern in a two-part wooden or metal flask (cope and drag), pack sand firmly around it, and cut channels for metal flow and air venting.
3. **Melt the Charge**: Heat scrap metal (e.g., bronze, cast iron, or aluminum) in a refractory-lined furnace until it achieves uniform fluidity.
4. **Pour and Vent**: Pour liquid metal continuously through the sprue while air and gases escape through riser channels.
5. **Finish the Cast Component**: Once cooled, shake out the sand mold, cut off sprues and risers, and grind surface imperfections.

## Materials needed

- **Molding Medium**: Silica sand, refractory clay, and water (green sand) or gypsum plaster.
- **Furnace Fuel**: **[Charcoal](/en/materials/charcoal)**, coke, or propane.
- **Crucible**: Graphite or ceramic container capable of withstanding temperatures exceeding 1,100°C–1,500°C.
- **Metals**: Copper, bronze, brass, **[Iron](/en/materials/iron)**, or aluminum.

## Variants and improvements

- **Sand Casting**: The most versatile method using packed sand molds suitable for high temperatures and large components.
- **Lost-Wax (Investment) Casting**: Uses wax patterns dipped in ceramic slurry to produce highly detailed components with fine surface finishes.
- **Die Casting**: Forces molten metal under high pressure into reusable steel molds for rapid mass production of non-ferrous parts.

## Limits and risks

- **Shrinkage and Porosity**: Metal contracts upon cooling, which can cause internal voids or dimensional distortions if risers are inadequate.
- **Extreme Heat and Burn Hazards**: Molten metal poses extreme thermal burn risks; moisture inside molds can cause explosive steam expansions.
- **Toxic Fumes**: Melting metals and binders releases heavy metal fumes and harmful carbon monoxide requiring adequate ventilation.
