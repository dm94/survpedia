# Brief description

Scurvy prevention is the set of dietary and preservation techniques used to maintain adequate levels of Vitamin C (ascorbic acid) in the human body. Scurvy was historically the single greatest killer of sailors on long-voyage explorations and a severe threat during winters or famines when fresh produce was scarce. Understanding how to prevent it is a cornerstone of long-term survival medicine.

## Use / Function

- **Preventing Scurvy:** Prevents the breakdown of collagen, which causes lethargy, bleeding gums, tooth loss, old wounds reopening, internal hemorrhaging, and death.
- **Immune System Support:** Bolsters human resistance to infections and disease.
- **Maritime Exploration:** Allows crews to survive voyages lasting several months without access to fresh land-based food.
- **Scale:** Individual, community, and military/maritime.

## Operating principle

Unlike most animals, humans cannot synthesize Vitamin C internally due to a mutation in our evolutionary history. Vitamin C is a water-soluble antioxidant crucial for collagen synthesis:

1. **Collagen Synthesis:** Vitamin C acts as a co-factor for enzymes that cross-link collagen fibers, which form the structural scaffolding of skin, blood vessels, bones, and muscles.
2. **Degradation:** Vitamin C is highly sensitive to heat, light, and oxidation. Boiling or long-term dry storage destroys its nutritional value, meaning preservation methods must be carefully chosen.
3. **Daily intake:** Because it is water-soluble, the body cannot store it for long. Symptoms of scurvy begin to appear after 4 to 12 weeks of complete deprivation.

## How to create it

Several historical and survival methods exist to produce and preserve Vitamin C sources:

### 1. Sauerkraut (Fermented Cabbage)

- Shred fresh cabbage and mix it with about 2% **[Salt](/en/materials/salt)** by weight.
- Pack it tightly into clean wooden barrels or **[clay](/en/materials/clay)** crocks to squeeze out the juice, ensuring the cabbage is fully submerged in its own brine to create an anaerobic environment.
- Let it ferment at room temperature for 2 to 6 weeks. Lactic acid bacteria will produce lactic acid, preserving the cabbage and protecting the Vitamin C from oxidation. It can remain stable and effective for years.

### 2. Sprouting Grains and Seeds

- If fresh fruits and vegetables are completely unavailable, soak dried peas, beans, or barley in **[water](/en/materials/water)** for 12 hours.
- Place them in a damp container out of direct sunlight and rinse them twice daily.
- As the seeds germinate and grow sprouts (2 to 3 days), they synthesize fresh Vitamin C, transforming non-protective dry grains into a powerful anti-scurvy food.

### 3. Conifer Needle Tea

- Gather fresh, young green needles from non-toxic conifers (such as Spruce, Pine, or Douglas Fir). Avoid toxic conifers like Yew.
- Chop the needles finely to break the cell walls.
- Steep them in hot **[water](/en/materials/water)** (not boiling, as excessive heat destroys Vitamin C) for 10-15 minutes to create a medicinal **[tea](/en/materials/tea)**. Drink it regularly in winter or survival conditions.

### 4. Citrus Juice Preservation

- Squeeze fresh lemon, lime, or orange juice.
- To preserve it without heat (boiling degrades the vitamin), mix the juice with about 10% brandy or **[alcohol](/en/materials/alcohol)**, or seal it in clean bottles with a thin layer of olive oil on top to prevent contact with oxygen.

## Materials needed

- **Vitamin C Sources:** Fresh cabbage, citrus fruits (lemons, limes), non-toxic pine/spruce needles, dry peas or beans.
- **Preservatives:** **[Salt](/en/materials/salt)** (for sauerkraut), brandy or **[alcohol](/en/materials/alcohol)** (for juice preservation).
- **Equipment:** Fermentation crocks or wooden barrels, clean glass bottles, knives, clean **[water](/en/materials/water)**.

## Variants and improvements

- **Captain Cook's Strategy:** Used sauerkraut, malt wort, and portable soup (a highly concentrated dried meat broth) alongside regular foraging for wild celery and greens at every landing, successfully keeping his crew scurvy-free.
- **Synthetic Ascorbic Acid:** Modern chemical synthesis (via the Reichstein process) produces pure ascorbic acid powder from glucose, allowing indefinite storage of dry Vitamin C.

## Limits and risks

- **Heat Sensitivity:** Cooking or pasteurizing juices at high temperatures for long durations completely inactivates Vitamin C.
- **Toxic Plants:** Ensure positive identification of pine or spruce species when making needle tea; some conifers, like the Western Yew (_Taxus brevifolia_), contain deadly alkaloids.
- **Decline in Storage:** Even well-preserved juices lose their Vitamin C potency over months of storage.
