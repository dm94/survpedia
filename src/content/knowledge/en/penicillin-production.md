# Post-Collapse Penicillin Production

Penicillin is a beta-lactam antibiotic secreted naturally by *Penicillium chrysogenum* and *Penicillium rubens* molds. Culturing Penicillium mold and extracting pure penicillin without modern pharmaceutical equipment requires strict bio-sanitation, controlled liquid nutrient media, activated charcoal adsorption, and organic solvent extraction.

## Core Process & Biological Principles

1. **Isolation & Selection:** Identifying *Penicillium* molds from wild sources (blue-green molds growing on citrus peels, cantaloupes, or bread).
2. **Nutrient Fermentation:** Growing mold liquid cultures in submerged or surface fermentation on nutrient broths containing sugar, organic nitrogen, and mineral salts (historically corn steep liquor and lactose).
3. **Secretion:** The mold secretes penicillin into the surrounding liquid broth as a secondary metabolite when sugar levels drop.
4. **Filtration & Adsorption:** Filtering out mold mycelium and adsorbing the penicillin dissolved in the broth onto **[Charcoal](/en/materials/charcoal)** particles.
5. **Solvent Extraction:** Eluting penicillin from charcoal into **[Alcohol](/en/materials/alcohol)** or **[Ether](/en/materials/ether)**, shifting pH with mild acids to isolate raw penicillin crystals.

## Step-by-Step Cultivation and Extraction Protocol

### 1. Growth Medium Preparation
- Prepare a liquid broth consisting of boiled water, 2% glucose or lactose (corn sugar or milk whey), 1% corn steep liquor or yeast extract, and small amounts of mineral salts (calcium nitrate, potassium phosphate).
- Sterilize all glass culture jars and broth thoroughly using a pressure cooker or **[Autoclave](/en/inventions/autoclave)** at 121°C (15 psi) for 20 minutes.

### 2. Inoculation and Fermentation
- Transfer spores of verified *Penicillium* mold into cooled sterile jars inside a draft-free, sanitized workspace.
- Incubate jars at 22°C–25°C. A white velvety fungal mat will form on the surface, turning blue-green as spores develop.
- Allow fermentation to proceed for 6 to 7 days until maximum penicillin concentration is secreted into the yellow broth beneath the fungal mat.

### 3. Separation and Extraction
- Siphon off the liquid broth and pass it through a fine cloth **[Water Filter](/en/inventions/water-filter)** to remove all mold solids. Cool the liquid to near 0°C.
- Add 1% finely ground activated **[Charcoal](/en/materials/charcoal)** to the broth and stir for 15 minutes. Charcoal adsorbs the penicillin molecules.
- Filter the charcoal out of the liquid and wash it with cold distilled water.
- Elute (wash) the charcoal with an organic solvent like cold **[Ether](/en/materials/ether)** or **[Alcohol](/en/materials/alcohol)** mixed with water acidified to pH 2.0 using diluted acid. Penicillin dissolves into the solvent phase.
- Neutralize the solvent solution back to pH 6.0 with sodium bicarbonate and evaporate the solvent at low temperature to yield crude **[Penicillin](/en/materials/penicillin)** salt.

## Limits and Safety Warnings

- **Lethal Contamination:** Unsterile culturing can grow lethal pathogens (e.g., *Aspergillus flavus* producing carcinogenic aflatoxins) alongside mold. Never use unpurified moldy bread directly on open wounds.
- **Hypersensitivity:** Penicillin causes severe allergic reactions (anaphylaxis) in sensitive individuals.
- **Potency Variation:** Uncalibrated post-collapse batches vary significantly in concentration; under-dosing promotes bacterial resistance, while over-dosing can cause toxicity.
