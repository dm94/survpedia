# Freeze Drying (Lyophilization)

Freeze drying is a low-temperature dehydration process that involves freezing a product, lowering surrounding pressure, and adding heat to allow the frozen water in the material to sublimate directly from the solid phase to the gas phase.

## Fundamental Principles

1. **Freezing Phase**: The material is frozen completely below its triple point temperature, converting water into ice crystals.
2. **Primary Drying (Sublimation)**: Pressure is lowered using a **[Vacuum Pump](/en/inventions/vacuum-pump)** while small amounts of heat are supplied, causing ice crystals to sublimate directly into water vapor without melting.
3. **Secondary Drying (Desorption)**: The chamber temperature is slightly raised to break ionic bonds between water molecules and the material matrix, removing residual bound moisture down to 1–4%.

## Applications in Civilization Rebuilding

- **Food Preservation**: Preserves biological structure, nutrients, flavor, and color without high heat degradation; stored items remain stable for decades at ambient temperatures.
- **Pharmaceutical Storage**: Crucial for preserving heat-sensitive antibiotics, vaccines, blood plasma, and enzymes without refrigeration.
- **Archival Recovery**: Salvaging water-damaged historical documents and books without paper deformation.

## Primitive and Low-Tech Methods

- **Incan Freeze Drying (Chuño)**: Exposure of potatoes to freezing night temperatures in high Andes altitudes, followed by crushing underfoot to squeeze out moisture and day drying under low atmospheric pressure.
- **Cold Climate Desiccation**: Hanging thin strips of meat or fish in freezing dry winter winds where sublimation occurs naturally over weeks.

## Practical Steps to Build a Freeze Dryer

1. Construct a vacuum-tight drying chamber using heavy **[Steel](/en/materials/steel)** or glass vessels.
2. Connect a two-stage **[Vacuum Pump](/en/inventions/vacuum-pump)** capable of pulling pressures below 0.1 mbar.
3. Position a condenser coil cooled by cold brine or dry ice between the drying chamber and the pump to catch sublimated water vapor before it damages the pump oil.
4. Place frozen samples on internal heating shelves regulated to provide controlled low heat energy.

## Key Considerations and Hazards

- **Triple Point Precision**: If pressure or temperature rises above water's triple point (6.11 mbar and 0.01 °C), ice melts into liquid water, causing structural collapse and shrinkage of the material.
- **Vacuum Pump Maintenance**: Sublimated vapor passing into pump oil causes oil contamination and pump failure if cold traps are inadequate.
