# Brief description

Fractional distillation is a separation process that uses difference in boiling points to segregate a complex liquid mixture into its individual chemical components or fractions. It is a foundational technology for petroleum refining, bio-fuel production, and industrial gas separation.

## Use / Function

- **Petroleum Refining**: Separates crude oil into gasoline, kerosene, diesel, lubricating oils, and heavy residue.
- **Bio-Fuel and Chemical Production**: Purifies bio-ethanol, methanol, and organic solvents from fermentation or wood pyrolysis.
- **Air Separation**: Liquefies and fractionates atmospheric air into pure oxygen, nitrogen, and argon gases.
- **Scale**: From laboratory distillation columns to massive industrial refining towers.

## Operating principle

Fractional distillation relies on repeated evaporation-condensation cycles along a vertical column:

1. **Heating**: The liquid mixture is heated in a reboiler or furnace until it vaporizes.
2. **Vapor Ascent**: The mixed vapor rises through a fractionating column containing packing material or perforated trays.
3. **Temperature Gradient**: The column is hot at the bottom and cooler at the top.
4. **Fractionation**: High-boiling-point components condense early near the bottom, while lower-boiling-point components remain gaseous longer and rise higher before condensing.
5. **Collection**: Liquid fractions with distinct boiling point ranges are drawn off at different heights along the column.

## How to implement

1. **Construct a Reboiler Chamber**: Assemble a sealed boiling vessel with a controlled heat source at the base.
2. **Erect a Fractionating Column**: Mount a tall vertical tube above the vessel packed with high-surface-area materials (glass beads, copper mesh, or structured trays).
3. **Attach Condensers and Tap Points**: Install side outlets and **[Heat Exchangers](/en/inventions/heat-exchanger)** at specific heights to condense and collect separated liquid fractions.
4. **Thermal Monitoring**: Place thermometers or sensors along the column height to maintain a stable vertical temperature profile.

## Materials needed

- **Boiling Chamber and Column**: Corrosion-resistant steel, copper, or **[Glass](/en/materials/glass)**.
- **Column Packing**: Copper mesh, ceramic rings, or glass beads for vapor-liquid contact.
- **Cooling System**: Continuous **[Water](/en/materials/water)** flow jackets for condensers.
- **Heat Source**: Gas burner, electric heating element, or direct furnace.

## Variants and improvements

- **Packed Columns**: Uses random or structured packing to maximize surface area for small-scale or laboratory setups.
- **Tray Towers**: Uses bubble-cap or sieve trays for large-scale industrial petroleum processing.
- **Vacuum Fractional Distillation**: Lowers system pressure to distill heat-sensitive high-boiling compounds without thermal cracking.

## Limits and risks

- **Azeotropes**: Certain mixtures (e.g., ethanol and water at 95.6% ethanol) form constant-boiling mixtures that cannot be separated beyond their azeotropic point by simple fractioning.
- **Fire and Explosion**: Heating volatile, flammable hydrocarbons creates significant fire and explosion hazards requiring flame arrestors and pressure-relief systems.
- **Thermal Energy Demand**: Demands significant thermal energy to maintain continuous vapor generation.
