# Brief description

The puddling process was a fundamental technique developed during the Industrial Revolution to refine high-carbon pig iron into malleable wrought iron using a reverberatory furnace fueled by coal, without letting the metal come into direct contact with the mineral fuel.

## Use / Function

- **Wrought Iron Production:** Produced large structural quantities of tough, malleable, and weldable iron.
- **Coal Utilization:** Allowed the use of coal or coke instead of scarce charcoal, dramatically expanding metal production.
- **Infrastructure Material:** Provided iron for steam engines, rails, bridges, and early machinery before mass steelmaking.

## Operating principle

1.  **Indirect Heating:** Pig iron is melted in the hearth of a reverberatory furnace, heated solely by hot gases reflected down from the arched roof.
2.  **Oxidation:** An iron oxide slag (e.g., hematite or hammer scale) is mixed into the molten pool.
3.  **Stirring ("Puddling"):** A worker (the puddler) manually stirs the liquid bath with long iron rods, exposing carbon in the metal to oxygen in the slag and furnace atmosphere.
4.  **Decarburization & Solidification:** As carbon burns off as carbon monoxide, the melting point of the iron rises. Pure iron begins to solidify into pasty lumps ("puddle balls").
5.  **Shingling:** The glowing puddle balls are gathered, removed, and hammered under a trip hammer to squeeze out liquid slag and weld the iron particles into solid bars.

## How to implement

### 1. The Furnace
- Build a **[Reverberatory Furnace](/en/inventions/reverberatory-furnace)** with a separate firebox and shallow iron hearth lined with iron oxide refractories.
- Construct a low arched roof made of firebrick to reflect heat onto the metal bath.

### 2. The Charge
- Load pig iron from a **[Blast Furnace](/en/inventions/blast-furnace)** together with iron oxide scale into the hearth.
- Heat until the metal fuses into a thick liquid.

### 3. The Puddling Operation
- Stir the molten metal vigorously with iron rabbles (hooks) as carbon burns off in violent boiling.
- When pasty lumps form, roll them into balls weighing roughly 40-50 kg.
- Transfer the hot puddle balls immediately to a **[Trip Hammer](/en/inventions/trip-hammer)** or squeezer to compact into blooms.

## Materials needed

- **Raw Material:** High-carbon **[Pig Iron](/en/materials/pig-iron)**.
- **Oxidizing Agent:** **[Iron Oxide](/en/materials/iron-oxide)** slag / scale.
- **Fuel:** Bituminous **[Coal](/en/materials/coal)** or coke.
- **Refractory Lining:** Heat-resistant **[Clay](/en/materials/clay)** bricks and iron-rich hearth lining.

## Variants and improvements

- **Dry Puddling (Cort process):** Original process using sand hearth lining; slower and wasted significant iron in slag.
- **Wet Puddling (Boiling process / Hall process):** Used iron oxide hearth lining; faster, higher yield, and caused characteristic "boiling" as carbon monoxide escaped.
- **Mechanical Puddling:** Late attempts to rotate the furnace hearth mechanically to reduce extreme manual labor.

## Limits and risks

- **Extreme Labor:** Required grueling manual labor in intense heat to stir tons of molten iron.
- **Energy Intensive:** Consumed large quantities of coal per ton of wrought iron produced.
- **Superseded by Steel:** Replaced in the late 19th century by mass steelmaking processes like the **[Bessemer Process](/en/knowledge/bessemer-process)**.
