# Brief description

Seed saving is the agricultural practice of selecting, harvesting, cleaning, drying, and storing viable seeds from open-pollinated crops to plant in subsequent growing seasons. It ensures food security, crop adaptation, and genetic biodiversity without reliance on commercial seed suppliers.

## Use / Function

- **Agricultural Perpetuation**: Guarantees a continuous supply of crop seeds season after season.
- **Selective Breeding**: Enhances desirable traits like drought tolerance, pest resistance, and yield through selective harvesting.
- **Crop Preservation**: Maintains heritage and heirloom plant varieties adapted to local microclimates.
- **Scale**: Individual household gardens to community seed banks.

## Operating principle

Seed saving relies on preserving seed viability by suspending embryonic metabolic activity through controlled desiccation and cool storage:

1. **Selection**: Prime open-pollinated plants exhibiting vigor, pest resistance, and high yield are tagged for seed harvest.
2. **Harvesting**: Seeds are gathered when fruits/pods reach full physiological maturity.
3. **Cleaning and Separation**: Dry seeds (like **[Wheat](/en/materials/wheat)** or beans) are threshed and winnowed; wet seeds (like tomatoes or squashes) are fermented or washed to remove germination-inhibiting pulp.
4. **Desiccation**: Seeds are thoroughly dried until moisture content drops below 8%–10%.
5. **Dormant Storage**: Dry seeds are stored in airtight **[Containers](/en/inventions/containers)** in cool, dark environments to maintain dormant viable states.

## How to implement

1. **Tag Superior Specimens**: Identify and mark healthiest plants during the peak growing season, avoiding diseased or stunted specimens.
2. **Harvest at Full Maturity**: Allow seed heads, pods, or fruits to ripen completely on the plant before gathering.
3. **Thresh and Winnow Dry Crops**: Crush pods or seed heads to free seeds, then pour them between containers in a light breeze to blow away light chaff.
4. **Ferment Wet Crops**: Ferment tomato seed gel in water for 2–3 days to break down germination inhibitors and kill seed-borne pathogens before rinsing.
5. **Dry and Store**: Spread cleaned seeds in thin layers on mesh screens in a shaded, well-ventilated space until brittle, then seal in moisture-proof containers with desiccant.

## Materials needed

- **Source Crops**: Open-pollinated heirloom crop varieties (**[Wheat](/en/materials/wheat)**, **[Maize](/en/materials/maize)**, legumes, vegetables).
- **Processing Equipment**: Screens, sieves, buckets, and drying trays.
- **Storage Containers**: Hermetic **[Containers](/en/inventions/containers)** (glass jars, clay pots sealed with wax, or metal tins).
- **Desiccant**: Wood ash, dry rice, or silica gel to absorb residual moisture.

## Variants and improvements

- **Community Seed Banks**: Distributed storage networks that safeguard genetic diversity against regional crop failures.
- **Cryogenic and Cold Storage**: Refrigerated seed vaults extending seed viability from years to decades or centuries.
- **Vegetative Propagation**: Preserving clonal crops (such as **[Potatoes](/en/materials/potato)**) through tubers, cuttings, or rootstock division rather than true seeds.

## Limits and risks

- **Cross-Pollination and Hybridization**: Planting related varieties too closely leads to unintended cross-pollination, loss of strain purity, and reduced progeny vigor.
- **Moisture and Mold**: Storing seeds with moisture levels above 12% triggers fungal rot and destroys embryo viability.
- **Pest Infestation**: Weevils, rodents, and insects can consume stored seeds if containers are not pest-proof.
