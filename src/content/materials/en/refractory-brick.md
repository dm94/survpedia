Refractory brick (or firebrick) is a high-performance ceramic block engineered to withstand intense temperatures (typically 1,400°C to over 1,800°C) without softening, melting, or deteriorating under chemical slag attack. It is the core material enabling pyrotechnology and metal extraction.

## Description of what it is like

Refractory bricks are heavy, dense blocks ranging in color from off-white and buff to yellowish or reddish-brown depending on alumina and iron contents. They have a coarse, granular texture and feel notably denser and harder than ordinary red building bricks. They emit a sharp ringing sound when struck together.

## Origin and where to find it

- **Environments**: Industrial brickworks, abandoned smelting sites, kilns, and glass factories.
- **Natural Availability**: Fireclay deposits (kaolinite-rich clay low in iron and alkalis) are found beneath coal seams or in weathered granite deposits.
- **Processed Material**: Requires high-temperature firing in dedicated kilns to achieve vitrification.

### Minimum processing required

1.  **Mining Fireclay**: Excavate refractory kaolin clay that has low contents of iron oxide, lime, and alkalis (which act as fluxes that lower melting points).
2.  **Mixing and Grog Addition**: Blend washed fireclay with "grog" (pre-calcined ground firebrick or crushed fired clay) to reduce shrinkage and cracking during drying.
3.  **Molding**: Compress the moist mixture under high pressure into dense rectangular molds.
4.  **Drying**: Air-dry thoroughly for several days to eliminate free moisture.
5.  **High-Temperature Firing**: Fire in a kiln at 1,300–1,500°C until the clay particles sinter and form mullite crystals.

### Tools needed to work on it

- **Wooden or Steel Molds**: Heavy-duty presses to form dense blocks.
- **High-Temperature Kiln**: Capable of reaching 1,300°C+.
- **Masonry Saw / Chisel**: For cutting hard refractory blocks during lining installation.
- **Fireclay Mortar**: High-alumina mortar used to bond firebricks with ultra-thin joints.

### Common forms of use

- **Standard Straight Bricks**: Rectangular blocks for flat furnace walls and hearths.
- **Wedge and Arch Bricks**: Tapered bricks designed for self-supporting furnace roofs, vaults, and circular kiln walls.
- **Monolithic Refractories**: Castable fireclay mixtures applied like wet concrete.

### Possible substitutes

- **Ordinary Red Brick**: Fails and melts above 1,100°C; acceptable only for low-heat outer chimney walls.
- **Soapstone (Talc Schiff)**: Natural stone with high thermal mass and moderate fire resistance; difficult to carve into complex furnace arches.
- **Rammed Fireclay**: Clay mixed with quartz sand packed directly into forge walls.

### Limitations and common failures

- **Thermal Shock**: Rapid heating or cooling can cause internal stresses, leading to spalling (cracking and flaking).
- **Slag Erosion**: Molten slag containing iron or fluxing agents can chemically dissolve basic or acidic firebricks over time.
- **Thick Mortar Joints**: If installed with thick mortar joints, heat will break down the mortar first; joints must be under 2 mm thick.

### Risks and safety

- **Silica Dust**: Cutting or crushing firebrick releases free crystalline silica dust, causing silicosis if inhaled.
- **Extreme Heat Radiation**: Radiant heat from furnace linings causes severe skin burns and eye cataracts without protective shields.
