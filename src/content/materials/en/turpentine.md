Turpentine (also called spirit of turpentine) is a fluid obtained by the distillation of resin harvested from pine trees. It is a vital organic solvent used extensively in paint formulation, varnish production, and chemical synthesis for industrial and medicinal applications.

## Description of what it is like

A clear, colorless liquid with a strong, characteristic pine odor. It is immiscible with water, highly volatile, and flammable. When exposed to air and light, it gradually oxidizes, thickens, and turns yellow or amber.

## Origin and where to find it

- **Environments:** Coniferous forests rich in pine trees (*Pinus* species such as maritime pine, slash pine, or longleaf pine).
- **Signs:** Resin or pitch bleeding from wounds on pine tree bark; aromatic pine smell near tapped trunks.
- **Natural vs Synthetic:** Natural plant resin distillate; can also be recovered as a byproduct during sulfate kraft pulping (sulfate turpentine).

### Minimum processing required

- **Resin Tapping:** Tapping live pine trees by V-shaped bark incisions to collect oleoresin in cups.
- **Steam Distillation:** Heating the crude resin with steam in an **[Alembic](/en/inventions/alembic)** or **[Distillation](/en/inventions/distillation)** apparatus.
- **Separation:** Condensing the vapor into two layers: volatile liquid turpentine spirits floating on water, leaving solid rosin (colophony) in the boiling kettle.
- **Dehydration:** Separating the top organic layer and drying over anhydrous salt or filtering.

### Tools needed to work on it

- **Tapping Tools:** Hack/chisel and collection pots.
- **Distillation Vessel:** Copper or glass **[Alembic](/en/inventions/alembic)** equipped with a condenser.
- **Heat Source:** Controlled furnace or steam boiler.

### Common forms of use

- **Liquid Solvent:** Thinning oil-based **[Paint](/en/materials/paint)**, lacquers, and varnishes.
- **Chemical Precursor:** Synthetic pinene feedstock for camphor, terpineol, and fragrance compounds.
- **Topical Medicine:** Component of traditional counter-irritant liniments for chest rubs and muscle relief.

### Possible substitutes

- **Mineral Spirits (White Spirit):** Petroleum-derived solvent substitute for paint thinning.
- **[Ether](/en/materials/ether):** Volatile solvent for non-polar extraction, though much more flammable.
- ** Citrus Terpenes (d-Limonene):** Solvent extracted from citrus peels; effective for cleaning and thinning.

### Limitations and common failures

- **Resin Polymerization:** Storing in open or partially filled clear bottles causes oxidation into gummy, sticky residues.
- **Corrosion of Rubber:** Rapidly degrades natural rubber seals, tubing, and containers.

### Risks and safety

- **Flammability:** Highly flammable liquid; vapors can form explosive mixtures with air.
- **Toxicity and Inhalation:** Vapors cause eye irritation, headaches, and dizziness; harmful if swallowed or absorbed through skin.
- **Spontaneous Combustion:** Rags soaked in turpentine and oil paint can self-heat and catch fire if piled up without ventilation.
