Babbitt metal (or bearing metal) is a soft white metal alloy invented by Isaac Babbitt in 1839. Composed predominantly of tin (or lead) alloyed with antimony and copper, it features a unique microstructure of hard intermetallic crystals embedded in a soft matrix, making it the premier anti-friction lining for heavy machinery journal bearings.

## Description of what it is like

A silvery-white, relatively soft metallic alloy with a low melting point (approx. 230–300°C). It can be easily melted in a simple ladle over a wood fire or forge and poured into bearing housings. When machined or polished, it exhibits a smooth, bright finish with minimal surface friction.

## Origin and where to find it

- **Environments:** Metallurgical foundries and machine construction workshops.
- **Signs:** Cast white-metal lining inside bronze or cast-iron pillow blocks, bearing caps, or connecting rods.
- **Natural vs Synthetic:** Synthetic metallurgical alloy; requires smelting and alloying refined metals.

### Minimum processing required

- **Melting:** Heating **[Tin](/en/materials/tin)** (or **[Lead](/en/materials/lead)**) in a crucible or iron ladle.
- **Alloying:** Dissolving precise quantities of antimony and **[Copper](/en/materials/copper)** into the molten base metal under charcoal cover to prevent oxidation.
- **Casting:** Pouring molten Babbitt metal directly into preheated **[Cast Iron](/en/materials/cast-iron)** or **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze)** bearing housings fitted around a mandrel shaft.
- **Scraping & Fitting:** Hand-scraping the cooled lining with bearing scrapers to ensure uniform contact with the rotating shaft journal.

### Tools needed to work on it

- **Ladle & Forge:** Iron ladle and coal/charcoal forge.
- **Mandrel & Clamps:** Cylindrical shaft fixture and clay sealing dams for pouring molten metal into housings.
- **Bearing Scrapers:** Curveted hand scrapers and Prussian blue dye for fitting.

### Common forms of use

- **Poured-in-Place Bearings:** Melted and poured directly into engine pillow blocks and shaft housings.
- **Insert Shells:** Pre-cast thin white-metal sleeve inserts clamped into split bearing blocks.

### Possible substitutes

- **[Bronze](/en/materials/bronze):** Harder copper-tin alloy; provides high strength but requires tighter tolerances and continuous pressure lubrication.
- **Cast Iron:** Cheap bearing material for low-speed machinery; prone to galling without abundant oil.
- **Lignum Vitae (Dense Wood):** Natural self-lubricating hardwood used for underwater stern tube propeller bearings.

### Limitations and common failures

- **Overheating (Wiping):** If lubrication fails or load causes excessive friction, temperatures above 150°C soften the alloy, causing it to "wipe" or melt out.
- **Fatigue Cracking:** Cyclic heavy pounding in diesel or steam engine rods can crack brittle hard-phase crystals over long service lives.

### Risks and safety

- **Lead Toxicity:** Lead-based Babbitt alloys present lead poisoning hazards during smelting and dry sanding/scraping.
- **Molten Metal Splash:** Moisture inside the bearing housing during pouring creates explosive steam pops, spraying molten metal.
