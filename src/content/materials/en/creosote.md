Creosote is a dark, oily, pungent liquid produced by the distillation of wood tar or coal tar. Rich in phenolic compounds, guaiacol, and cresols, it is one of the most powerful and long-lasting wood preservatives known, preventing fungal decay, rot, and insect attack in outdoor timber.

## Description of what it is like

A heavy, viscous, dark yellow to brownish-black oily liquid with a strong, smoky, aromatic odor reminiscent of smoked wood or tar. It is insoluble in water, sticks heavily to organic surfaces, and penetrates deeply into porous timber fibers.

## Origin and where to find it

- **Environments:** Chemical distillation plants, tar refineries, and wood treatment yards.
- **Signs:** Darkly stained, oily wooden railway ties, telegraph poles, and marine pilings smelling strongly of smoke.
- **Natural vs Synthetic:** Synthetic chemical distillate derived from pyrolyzed organic matter.

### Minimum processing required

1. **Tar Pyrolysis:** Subject **[Wood](/en/materials/wood)** or coal to destructive distillation to obtain raw **[Tar](/en/materials/tar)**.
2. **Fractional Distillation:** Heat the raw tar in a steel retort or **[Alembic](/en/inventions/alembic)** vessel.
3. **Fraction Collection:** Collect the heavy oil fraction evaporating between 200°C and 300°C, condensing it into liquid creosote oil.
4. **Timber Pressure Impregnation:** Soak or pressure-inject hot creosote into seasoned wooden beams and posts.

### Tools needed to work on it

- **Distillation Retort:** Steel condensation vessel and condenser coil.
- **Soaking Tank / Pressure Vessel:** Metal vat for immersing timber in heated creosote.
- **Protective Equipment:** Heavy rubber gloves, aprons, and eye protection.

### Common forms of use

- **Wood Preservative Oil:** Brushed or pressure-impregnated onto railroad cross-ties, telephone poles, and fence posts.
- **Marine Pilings:** Coating underwater dock pilings to prevent teredo worm (shipworm) infestation.
- **Medical Antiseptic (Historical):** Diluted beechwood creosote was historically used as an internal and external disinfectant.

### Possible substitutes

- **[Tar](/en/materials/tar):** Crude wood tar provides waterproofing but lacks deep wood penetration.
- **[Copper](/en/materials/copper) Sulfate:** Chemical salt preservative used in waterborne timber treatments.
- **Charring:** Scorching the surface of wooden posts over fire to prevent soil rot.

### Limitations and common failures

- **Leaching & Exudation:** In hot weather, excess creosote can bleed out of treated timber onto the surface.
- **Corrosiveness:** Can soften or degrade certain rubber or plastic seals upon long exposure.

### Risks and safety

- **Toxicity & Carcinogenicity:** Contains polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and phenols; prolonged skin contact or inhalation of vapors poses cancer and chemical burn risks.
- **Flammability:** Highly combustible liquid when exposed to open flame.
- **Skin Sensitization:** Causes severe photosensitivity and burning sensation on exposed skin when combined with sunlight.
